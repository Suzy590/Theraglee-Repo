# Therapist outreach by email — the Hormozi way

This is the outreach email and the plan around it for getting licensed
therapists to list on Theraglee. The rules come from Alex Hormozi's email
marketing video (on YouTube as "Anyone Can Turn $1,000 Into $45,000 Doing
This...", also titled "Learn Email Marketing in 39 Minutes!" and "How I Write
Simple Emails That Make Money"). The title's number is his claim that a dollar
spent on email returns 35 to 45 dollars, because once someone is on your list
every message is nearly free.

One honest caveat before the plan: Hormozi is talking about people who **asked**
to hear from you. Writing to a therapist who has never heard of Theraglee is
colder, so expect smaller numbers at first, and keep the three legal rules at
the bottom of this page.

## The principles, in plain English

| Hormozi says | What it means for Theraglee |
| --- | --- |
| Email is the cheapest lever you have. | Every therapist address we collect is an asset. Write to it on a schedule, not only when we remember. |
| Unsubscribes are normal. Make leaving easy. | A clear unsubscribe link on every message. People who leave were never going to pay, and keeping them hurts delivery to the ones who will. |
| Segment the list. | Keep therapists, members, and Sunday-tool readers in separate lists, and never send a therapist pitch to a member. |
| One email, one idea, about 200 words. | Each message makes one point and asks for one thing. |
| Same shape every time: subject, hook, body, call to action, P.S. | The template below. The P.S. is one of the most-read lines, so it always carries a second small ask. |
| Mostly plain text, one or two links, no promo language. | No image banners, no "LIMITED TIME!!!", no five links. That is what lands in the Promotions tab. |
| Write the preview text. | The gray line after the subject in an inbox. Hormozi saw about 24% more opens when it was written on purpose. |
| Speed to lead: answer within a minute. | When a therapist replies, signs up, or writes to `hello@theraglee.com`, answer the same hour, the same day at worst. |
| A follow-up after sign-up lifts conversion about 40%. | A therapist who starts a listing and stops gets a short, kind nudge, not silence. |
| Ask for a reply. | "Reply yes and I'll send it" tells Gmail the sender is wanted and keeps later emails out of Promotions. |
| Test subject lines on a slice, send the winner to the rest. | Send two subjects to 10% each, wait a few hours, send the better one to the other 80%. |
| Three a week is the goal; start with two a month. | We start at two a month to therapists and grow once replies come in. |
| Business readers open on Wednesdays, 10 to noon or 1 to 3, local time. | Therapists are business readers. Send Wednesday late morning in their time zone. |
| Hooks come from a specific bad moment, not a general complaint. | Open with the moment every therapist recognizes, not "marketing is hard." |
| Watch clicks more than opens. | Apple hides opens. Clicks and replies are the real score. |

## The email

Fill the two placeholders. Keep it plain text. One link in the body, one in
the P.S., nothing else.

```text
Subject:      Theraglee Therapist Note: the client who asked for you
Preview text: Members near you flip a switch that says "please reach out." Here is how to be the one who does.

Hi [First name],

Most therapists know this moment: another month's directory fee goes
out, and the only new message in the inbox is spam.

Theraglee turns the directory around. Members who want a therapist
flip a switch on their dashboard that means one thing: "I'm ready,
please reach out." The ones who match your profile then appear on
your dashboard with an age range, the topics they want help with,
video or in person, their insurance plan, and the first three digits
of their zip code. You send one short message. If they reply, you get
their real name and a first session to book.

There is no fee per contact. Your real phone number and email stay
private; callers and messages come through a Theraglee number and
inbox. Your license is checked before the listing goes live, and you
can cancel any time.

Founding therapists lock in $20 a month for as long as the membership
stays active. The regular rate is $29 a month or $290 a year.

See how it works, in about two minutes:
https://theraglee.com/for-therapists/match-mode

[Your name]
Theraglee

P.S. Reply "yes" and I will send you one worksheet from our clinician
library, free, no listing needed. New ones are added every day.
```

Why it is built this way:

- **Subject** starts with the same brand label every time ("Theraglee
  Therapist Note:") so after two or three emails the name alone earns the open.
- **Hook** is one specific moment, the directory fee with nothing to show for
  it. Hormozi found pain-moment hooks beat "how was life before" every time.
- **Body** is about 200 words on one idea: Match Mode brings them to you.
- **Call to action** is one link, to the explainer page, not the checkout.
  People buy after they understand.
- **P.S.** asks for a reply. The reply is worth more than the worksheet: it
  tells the mail providers we are wanted, and it opens a real conversation.

## Two follow-ups

Hormozi's point is that most people are busy, not uninterested, so asking
again is fine as long as each message gives something.

**One week later, Wednesday.** A value email, no pitch in the body.

```text
Subject:      Theraglee Therapist Note: one tool for the client who says "it will go badly"
Preview text: A behavioral experiment record, ready to print, from the clinician library.

Hi [First name],

Short one today. A client is sure the thing will go badly. "It will go
badly" is not testable, but "I will stammer and at least two people
will look away" is. The Behavioral Experiment Record walks a client
from the vague fear to the specific prediction, runs the test for a
week, and records what actually happened.

Here it is, ready to print:
[link to the worksheet]

That library is part of a Theraglee listing. Five new tools arrive
every day across CBT, ACT, DBT, couples, kids and games.

[Your name]
Theraglee

P.S. Which topic do you see most this month? Reply with one word and I
will send you the newest tool on it.
```

**Two weeks later, Wednesday.** The offer, with the honest deadline.

```text
Subject:      Theraglee Therapist Note: the founding rate closes [date]
Preview text: $20 a month, locked in for as long as you stay. After [date] it is $29.

Hi [First name],

One update and one date.

The update: members in your area have started switching on Match Mode.
When one matches your profile, they appear on your dashboard and you
decide whether to write. Nothing per contact, ever.

The date: the founding therapist rate of $20 a month ends on [date].
Anyone listed before then keeps it for as long as the membership stays
active. After that the rate is $29 a month or $290 a year.

Start your listing here. License check first, then you go live:
https://theraglee.com/therapist-signup.html?plan=founding

[Your name]
Theraglee

P.S. If now is not the time, reply "later" and I will stop writing
until the next time the library has something for your caseload.
```

Only send the third email while the founding offer is on in the admin screen,
and copy the deadline from there, never from memory (`docs/therapist-pages.md`
explains where the price and the deadline live).

## The plan, week by week

**Before the first send.**

1. Build the list in a spreadsheet: first name, email, state, city, specialties,
   and where the address came from. Licensed therapists only.
2. Set up a sending tool that handles unsubscribes for you. Resend already
   sends the site's automatic mail (`docs/email.md`); for a hand-written
   campaign, a small newsletter tool connected to `hello@theraglee.com` is
   simpler, because replies have to land where a person reads them.
3. Warm the address. Send to 20 or 30 people a day for the first two weeks,
   never a thousand on day one. Mail providers judge new senders by how the
   first messages are received.
4. Write the three emails above into the tool as one template, check them on a
   phone, and keep every image out.

**Week 1, Wednesday, 10 to noon local time.** Send email one to the first
segment. Split the subject line on 10% and 10% of the list:

- A: "Theraglee Therapist Note: the client who asked for you"
- B: "Theraglee Therapist Note: no fee per contact"

After three hours, send the subject with more clicks to the other 80%.

**Every day, all weeks.** Answer every reply the same hour. A "yes" gets the
worksheet and one line asking what they see most. A question gets an answer
and the link to the right page, never a copy of the pitch.

**Week 2, Wednesday.** Email two to everyone who did not unsubscribe. Add the
next segment and send them email one.

**Week 3, Wednesday.** Email three to everyone from week 1, email two to week
2's people, email one to the next segment. From here it is a rolling three-week
sequence.

**Week 4 onward.** Keep the Wednesday rhythm at two emails a month to anyone
who finished the sequence: one tool from the library, one plain update about
members in their area. When replies are steady, move to weekly. Hormozi's end
goal is three a week, but only once each email is worth opening.

**Anyone who starts a listing and stops** gets one short note within a day:
what step they were on, what is left, and one link back. That is the 40%
follow-up lift in the video.

## What to write down after each send

Keep a small table. Clicks and replies are the score; opens are a rough guide.

| Send | Sent | Opened | Clicked | Replied | Listed | Unsubscribed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Hormozi's own list runs about 35% opens and 8.5% clicks, with under 1%
unsubscribing per email. A cold list to therapists will be lower; what matters
is that each number climbs from one send to the next. If unsubscribes pass
2% on a send, the hook or the segment is wrong. If clicks are fine and
listings are not, the problem is the page, not the email.

## Three legal rules for cold email

The video assumes an opted-in list. Writing to people who did not ask
for it is allowed in the US, as long as:

1. The subject line is honest and the "from" name is really us.
2. Every message has a working unsubscribe link, and we honor it within
   ten days (a good tool does this the moment they click).
3. Every message shows a real postal address for Theraglee in the footer.

Never buy a list of email addresses, and never write to a therapist's
clients. The members' side of the site has its own rules in
`docs/match-mode-consent.md`, and nothing here changes them.
