# Emails that earn links and listings

Ten short emails, written to be copied and sent as they are. Replace anything
in [brackets]. Each one asks for a single, small thing and tells the reader in
the first two lines why it is worth their time. Nothing here needs editing
for tone; it already sounds like a person, which is the point.

Send from hello@theraglee.com, sign with your name, and keep the subject line
as written unless the person's own page gives you a better one.

## The templates

### (a) University or college counseling center

Who gets it: the counseling center at a university or community college, which
almost always keeps a "resources" or "self-help" page for students and is
glad to add free, no-account, no-ads tools.

>

> self-help resources page at [page address] is one of the better ones I have
> seen. I would like to offer something for it.
>

>

> Every crisis mention points to 988.
>
> If either page fits your list, I would be grateful for a link. Happy to
> answer any questions.
>

## How to find the right address

- **Look for the resources page first, not the contact page.** Search
  `[organization name] mental health resources` or
  `[organization name] self-help`. If there is no such page, the email has
  nowhere to land; move on.
- **The person who maintains it is usually named.** Scroll to the bottom of
  the page for "maintained by," "contact," or "last updated by," or look at
  the department's staff list for the counseling director, the reference
  librarian, the student services coordinator or the wellbeing lead. Write to
  that person by name. A generic info@ address gets a generic no.
- **Read the page before you write.** Mention something on it in your first
  line. It is the difference between a letter and a flyer.
- **Never buy lists** and never paste into a bulk tool. Ten emails a week,
  each written to one person about one page, will outperform a thousand sent
  at once, and the thousand will get the domain marked as spam.
- **Ten a week is the pace.** Two a day, Monday to Friday, in the same half
  hour. Log each one in the table below before you close the tab.

## Follow-up

One follow-up, ten days after the first email, then stop. The follow-up is
two lines: "Hello [Name], I wrote ten days ago about adding Theraglee to
[page]. If it is not a fit, no need to reply; if it is, the links are below."
Then the same two links. Never a third email. A silence is an answer, and the
same person may say yes to a different email a year from now if you leave
them alone today.

The exception is template (i), the founding-therapist invitation: that one
gets no follow-up at all.
