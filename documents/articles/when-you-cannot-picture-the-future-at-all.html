When You Cannot Picture the Future at All

Asked where you will be in a year, you get nothing, and that blankness is frightening. What it usually means, why it is not a prediction, and how to work while it lasts.

DepressionDecision Making Visitor 789 words

Somebody asks what you want to be doing next year and there is nothing there. Not a bad picture. No picture. You can describe tomorrow and possibly next week, and past that the screen is blank.

For a lot of people this is more alarming than feeling low, because it seems like information. It feels as though you have looked ahead and found nothing waiting.

What the blankness usually is

It is worth saying plainly: it is a symptom far more often than it is a forecast.

Imagining the future is a mental task, and it takes energy. Below a certain level people stop being able to do it, the same way they stop being able to follow a film or read a page. The machinery is tired, and the output goes blank.

It also runs on memory. Picturing a plausible next year is mostly assembled from remembered scenes, and a stretch of low mood flattens those, which leaves less to build with.

And it needs some expectation that what you do will matter. When that has gone quiet, the exercise stops making sense rather than returning a bad answer.

None of that is the same as there being nothing ahead. It is the difference between a camera being broken and a room being empty.

Why it feels so convincing

Because blankness does not announce itself as a malfunction. It presents as a result.

There is no visible error. A low mood you can argue with. An absence gives you nothing to push against, so it feels neutral and therefore trustworthy.

It matches the other evidence you are collecting at the time, which is already slanted.

And people around you ask future questions constantly, so you are reminded of it several times a week without ever being able to answer.

Working while it lasts

The useful move is to stop trying to see further and change the distance you are working at.

Shorten the horizon on purpose. What is happening this week. What is happening tomorrow morning. That is not a failure of ambition, it is the right scale for current conditions.

Postpone anything large and reversible-looking that you can postpone. Quitting, ending, moving, selling. Decisions made from a blank screen tend to be regretted, and nothing is lost by waiting.

Keep the appointments and commitments you already have. They are a structure built by a version of you with more information, and letting them lapse removes the scaffolding.

And answer the future question with something honest and small. I am not thinking much past Christmas at the moment. Most people accept that immediately.

What tends to bring it back

Worth knowing, because it does come back, and usually not by being thought about.

It returns gradually and in small things first. Wanting to see somebody next month, having an opinion about a trip, getting mildly interested in a plan. People rarely notice it returning and usually notice afterward that it has.

It tracks the other things. Sleep, eating, movement, the dose being right, whatever was going on at the time. The imagination is downstream of all of that and it recovers with them rather than ahead of them.

And doing a few things that have mattered before helps more than reflecting on it. Borrowed scaffolding works while your own is down.

What not to do with it

Do not treat it as a decision about your life. It is a reading taken with a broken instrument, and acting on it is the main risk here.

Do not argue with it directly. Trying to force a picture of next year tends to produce more blankness and a sense of failure on top.

And do not take it as evidence about who you are. This is one of the most common experiences in low mood and one of the least talked about, which is exactly why it feels so personal.

When to tell somebody

This is worth raising with a doctor or a therapist rather than waiting for it to pass, particularly if it has lasted more than a couple of weeks or arrived with the other things that tend to travel with it.

Say it in plain words, because clinicians recognize it immediately and it is useful information for them. I cannot picture anything ahead is a sentence worth using.

And if the blankness has turned into something more active, a sense that there is no point, or thoughts of not being here, tell your doctor or a therapist today rather than waiting for the next appointment.
