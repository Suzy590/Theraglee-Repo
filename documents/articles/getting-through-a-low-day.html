Getting Through a Low Day

Some days everything is gray and heavy and nothing seems worth the effort. You do not have to fix the day. You just have to get through it, and there are ways to make that easier.

DepressionSelf-Care Visitor 862 words

Some days arrive already heavy. You wake up and the color has drained out of things. Nothing sounds good. The tasks in front of you look enormous and pointless at the same time. Even the things you usually enjoy feel like work you cannot face.

If you are in one of those days right now, this is written for you, and it is deliberately short on grand advice. A low day is not the time to overhaul your life. It is the time to get through, as gently as you can, until the weather changes.

Lower the bar on purpose

The first move is to decide, clearly, that today is not a normal day and you will not hold yourself to normal standards. This is not giving up. It is accuracy. You would not expect yourself to run your usual distance with a fever. A low day is a kind of fever of the mood, and it deserves the same allowance.

So pick the two or three things that truly must happen. Feed yourself and anyone who depends on you. Do the one work task with a real deadline. Take medications if you take them. Everything else can wait, and the world will not end. Say that to yourself out loud if you need to.

Do the smallest possible version

For the things that do need doing, shrink them until they are almost silly. Not, clean the kitchen. Instead, put the dishes in the sink. Not, answer all the emails. Instead, answer the one that matters. Not, exercise. Instead, stand outside for two minutes.

Low mood tells you that nothing will help, so there is no point starting. It is very convincing and it is wrong. Starting, even at the tiniest scale, is what loosens the grip. You do not have to feel like it. You just have to do the small version and notice that you did.

Take care of the body

Mood and body are not separate things. On a low day, the body is usually low too, and small physical care can shift the whole picture more than it seems it should.

Eat something real, even if you are not hungry. Low blood sugar makes everything feel worse. Drink water. Take a shower, or at least wash your face and change your shirt. Open the curtains and let daylight in, or better, step outside into it for a few minutes. Move a little, even if it is just walking to the mailbox. None of these will cure the day. Each one makes it a little lighter.

Be careful with the story

On a low day the mind writes a story: this is how it always is, this is how it will always be, this is who I really am. The story feels like truth because it matches the mood. But it is the mood talking, not the facts.

You do not have to argue with the story. Just label it. There is the low-day story again. Then set it down and go do the next small thing. You can evaluate your whole life some other day, when you are in a better position to be fair to yourself.

Reach for one person

Low days pull you inward. Answering a text feels like too much. Seeing anyone feels impossible. That pull is worth resisting, in a small way. You do not need a deep conversation. Send one message. Sit in the same room as someone. Call a friend and say, I am having a rough day and I do not really want to talk about it, but I wanted to hear your voice. Connection does not fix a low day, but isolation makes it heavier.

Let the day be small

Give yourself permission to do less than usual and to feel worse than usual without treating either as a failure. Watch something easy. Go to bed early. Lie on the floor for a while if that is what you can manage. The day counts even if you did almost nothing in it. Getting through is the whole assignment.

Tomorrow is often different

Low days pass. That is the most important thing to remember while you are in one, because from inside, it does not feel that way. Most people find that a low day is followed by a lighter one, especially if they were gentle with themselves during it. You do not have to believe that right now. You just have to get to tomorrow and see.

When the low days stop being days

A low day now and then is part of being human. A low week, a low month, a stretch where the gray never really lifts, is something else, and it deserves more than an article. If you have felt this way most days for a couple of weeks or longer, if you have stopped caring about things that used to matter, or if you have had thoughts that you would be better off gone, please talk to a therapist or your doctor. This is treatable, and you should not have to carry it alone. In the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
