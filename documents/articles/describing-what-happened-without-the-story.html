Describing What Happened Without the Story

Most of what you replay is interpretation wearing the clothes of fact. How to separate the two, why it lowers the temperature, and what to do when the story turns out to be right.

DBTMindfulness Visitor 753 words

She did not answer my message becomes she is annoyed with me becomes I have done something wrong again. Three separate things, and by the evening they have fused into one, and what you are now upset about is not the thing that happened.

This is one of the few mental habits that is genuinely worth drilling, because almost everything else gets easier once it is in place.

The difference, precisely

A description is what a camera would record. An interpretation is what it means. Both are necessary, and the trouble starts when the second is stored as the first.

He raised his voice is a description. He was furious with me is an interpretation.

Nobody has replied in the group is a description. They are leaving me out is an interpretation.

My chest is tight and my hands are cold is a description. Something is wrong with my heart is an interpretation.

The test is whether another person in the room would have written down the same sentence. Most of what we carry around would not survive that.

Why separating them lowers the temperature

It is not about deciding the interpretation is wrong. Often it is right. The point is that an interpretation can be examined and a fact cannot.

The description is almost always smaller. What actually happened fits in one line, and the thing that has been running for two hours does not.

You stop defending a position you never consciously adopted. Interpretations get held with the certainty of observations, and separating them restores the ordinary ability to be unsure.

And the feeling drops, usually noticeably. Not because you have argued yourself out of it, but because the thing it was attached to turned out to be smaller.

How to actually do it

This works far better written than thought about, at least at first.

Write the event in one sentence, with nothing in it that a camera could not see or a recorder could not catch. No adverbs, no motives, no history.

Then write everything else underneath, in a second list, labeled as what you are making of it. You are not deleting that list. You are putting it where it belongs.

Include your own body in the description. Jaw tight, face hot, hands shaking. Those are facts and they tend to get left out, which makes the feeling seem to come from the situation rather than through you.

And notice the words that smuggle in meaning. Always, never, obviously, deliberately, again. Each of those is an interpretation in the costume of a detail.

When it is hardest

Some situations defeat this, and it helps to know which.

Anything involving somebody you have a long history with, because the history arrives automatically and feels like part of what you saw.

Anything where you are already tired or hungry, because the separation takes effort that is not available.

And anything that has been running for days, because by then the story has been rehearsed enough to feel like a memory of what happened.

In those cases the realistic aim is one line of description, not a full account. One is enough to interrupt it.

What to do if the story turns out to be right

This is the part people expect to be a problem and it is usually the opposite.

A confirmed interpretation gives you something to act on. They were annoyed, now known rather than suspected, is a thing you can address directly.

You will have spent far less on it. The hours lost to this are mostly spent in the uncertain middle, and getting to the answer sooner is a saving regardless of the answer.

And being right about one of them does not retroactively make the habit unnecessary. The point was never that your readings are usually wrong. It was that you cannot tell which are which while they are fused.

When to take it further

A certain amount of this is ordinary and everyone does it.

It is worth bringing to someone if the replaying is costing you sleep, if you cannot get any distance from the interpretations even when you try, if other people's neutral behavior reliably reads as rejection, or if the stories are mostly about what is wrong with you.

That is well-trodden ground for a therapist, and this particular skill is one they teach directly rather than something you have to arrive at alone. Your doctor can point you toward what is available if you are not sure where to start.
