Being the Sober One at the Party

One short sentence, repeated. Why holding a glass matters, how to handle the people who push, and why the evening gets harder after eleven whatever you are drinking.

AssertivenessSocial Anxiety Visitor 845 words

You are not drinking tonight. Maybe you have stopped for good, maybe you are driving, maybe it is medication, maybe you just do not want to. And somehow this ordinary fact has become the most conspicuous thing about you in the room.

Within ten minutes somebody will notice, and you will discover how much explaining other people think a soft drink requires.

Decide your line before you arrive

The scramble happens because you are thinking about it for the first time with a glass being held out at you. So decide in the quiet beforehand.

Pick one short sentence and keep it the same all night. I am not drinking tonight. I am on something that does not mix. I am driving. I am good, thanks.

Short is what makes it work. A reason invites a conversation about the reason. A flat sentence with nothing after it ends it.

You are allowed to say nothing true at all, by the way. Your health is yours and a party is not the place you owe anyone an account of it.

Hold something

This sounds too simple to matter and it is one of the most effective things available.

An empty hand reads as waiting for a drink, and people fill it. A glass with something in it reads as sorted, and people move on.

So get something at the door and keep it topped up. Soda and lime, tonic, a cola, a non-alcoholic beer if that does not make it harder for you, anything in a proper glass rather than a plastic cup.

If you are at a bar, order first and order confidently. Hesitation is what gets questioned.

Handle the pushers without a debate

Most people do not care. A few will push, and it is almost never about you. It is about them wanting company, or being unsettled by someone not drinking, or already being past the point of tact.

Repeat your line word for word rather than finding a new answer. Honestly, I am good. Honestly, I am good. Repetition is boring and boring is what you want.

Change the subject in the same breath. How is the new place. Did you end up going. People follow the new question gratefully.

Do not get drawn into how long it has been, or why, or whether just the one would hurt. Not tonight, not standing up, not with an audience. I would rather not get into it here is a complete sentence.

Have a job

Being the sober one at a loose event is unstructured, and unstructured is when you start counting the minutes.

So give yourself something: take the photos, mind the music, keep the food going, drive people home, talk to the person standing on their own at the edge, who is usually glad of you.

Having a role means you are doing something rather than noticing that you are not drinking, and most of the awkwardness lives in that noticing.

Know that the evening has two halves

The first half is usually fine. Then it tips. Conversations get louder and more repetitive, and the connection you were enjoying at nine o'clock is not available at eleven.

That is not you going flat. It is the room changing. Plan for it and you will stop reading it as a failure.

So go early, when the talking is good. Decide your leaving time before you arrive, and leave at it without an announcement or an apology. Arrange your own way home so that staying is never the easier option.

Saying good night is the one thing people do not question.

Expect your own discomfort and let it be there

Even with every line prepared, the first few events are uncomfortable, and that is not a sign you are doing it wrong.

You may feel on the outside of something, or boring, or that you are being watched. Some of that is accurate: you are doing something unusual in a room built around one thing. Most of it fades after a handful of occasions, as it stops being new to you and to everyone else.

It helps to notice what you get. Remembering the conversation. Driving home. Waking up clear. No morning of combing back through what you said.

And it helps to have one person in the room who knows. Not an announcement, just somebody you can stand next to who will not offer you a drink.

When to get help

Please talk to your doctor if you are stopping after drinking heavily or daily, because coming off alcohol can need medical support and stopping suddenly on your own is not always safe. If you are not drinking because drinking had become a problem, a therapist or a local alcohol service will give you more than willpower will, and a group of people doing the same thing takes much of the loneliness out of it. If social events are becoming something you avoid altogether, or the anxiety beforehand is affecting your week, that is worth taking to a professional in its own right.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
