When People Stop Asking How You Are

The calls thin out around the second month, right when it gets harder. Why support fades, what to do when you still need it, and how to ask without feeling like a burden.

GriefLoss Visitor 824 words

For the first few weeks there were messages, food, people checking in. Then it thinned out. By the second month it had mostly stopped, and by now almost nobody mentions it at all.

Which would be fine, except that this is the part where it got harder, not easier.

Why support fades when it does

Very little of this is people not caring. The reasons are more ordinary than that, and knowing them makes the fading hurt slightly less.

People respond to events. A death is an event, so there is a wave of support around it. The months afterward are not events, so nothing prompts anyone.

Most people also believe they would be intruding. They imagine you want to move on and that mentioning it would drag you back, which is almost exactly backward for most grieving people, who are relieved when somebody says the name.

Some are afraid of saying the wrong thing, so they say nothing, which feels to you like indifference and feels to them like care.

And people return to their own lives, which continued. That is not betrayal. It is just the shape of things, and it lands hard when yours has not.

The timing is particularly unfortunate

The early weeks are often strange and busy. There is a lot to organize, you may be numb, and the sheer activity carries you.

Then the arrangements end, everyone goes home, and the actual absence starts. Month two to month six is when many people find it heaviest, which is precisely when the outside world has decided you are through the worst.

So you end up least supported at the point you need it most, and often concluding from that there is something wrong with how long this is taking. There is not. The mismatch is in other people's calendars, not in your grief.

Asking without feeling like a burden

The fading will not reverse on its own, because nothing is prompting anyone. Which means the ask falls to you, unfairly, and it is worth doing anyway.

Be specific rather than general. People freeze at let me know if you need anything and respond well to a concrete request. Can you call me on Sunday evenings for a while. Can we walk on Thursday.

Say what you actually want from the contact, because most people do not know. I do not need advice, I just want to be able to mention him without everyone going quiet.

Ask different people for different things. One person for company, one for practical help, one you can be a mess with. Expecting all of it from one person is how that person burns out and starts avoiding you.

And tell them it is still happening. A lot of people genuinely believe you are fine now. It is still very hard, more than people realize is new information to almost everyone who hears it.

Building something that does not depend on being asked

Where you can, make contact that does not require anyone to remember.

Standing arrangements work better than intentions. The same walk, the same call, the same evening each week, agreed once so nobody has to initiate again.

Grief groups exist for exactly this gap, and people who expect them to be grim are often surprised. The relief of being with people who do not need it explained is considerable.

Anniversaries and birthdays are worth telling someone about in advance. Most people would mark them if they knew, and will not think of it otherwise.

If someone asks what they can do, have an answer ready. People want a task.

What to do with the anger about it

Many people feel let down, and it is worth separating two things.

Some of it is fair. Some people did disappear, including ones who should not have, and that is a real loss on top of the first one.

Some of it is the impossible wish, which is for people to understand without being told. That wish is completely understandable and it will not be granted, and holding onto it mostly costs you the help that is still available.

You can be angry and still ask. The asking is not a concession.

When to bring in more than friends

If months are going by without it easing at all, if you cannot do the ordinary parts of your day, if you are drinking more or sleeping badly most nights, or if the loneliness has turned into something flatter and heavier, that deserves more than the support of people who are busy.

A therapist who works with grief is not for people who are failing at it. It is a reasonable thing to arrange when the people around you have gone back to their lives and yours has not restarted yet. Your doctor can point you to local grief services, and many are free. In the US you can call or text 988 at any time.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
