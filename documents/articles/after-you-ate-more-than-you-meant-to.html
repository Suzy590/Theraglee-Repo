After You Ate More Than You Meant To

The eating is over and the hard part has started: the self-attack and the plan to make up for it. What actually helps in the next hour, and how to stop the cycle restarting.

FoodDBT Visitor 870 words

It is done. You are sitting there feeling uncomfortable and slightly unreal, and the voice has already started: what is wrong with you, you have no control, you have ruined it.

Then comes the plan. Nothing tomorrow. Skip breakfast. Extra exercise. Start again Monday and be strict this time.

That plan is the problem, and it is worth understanding why before you act on it.

The restriction is usually what set it up

For most people this is a cycle rather than an event, and it runs in a predictable order.

You decide to be strict. You eat too little, or skip meals, or cut out whole categories. Your body gets genuinely hungry and your mind gets preoccupied with food. Eventually that pressure breaks, usually in the evening, usually fast and not very enjoyably. Then shame arrives, and shame produces a stricter plan, which builds the next one.

So the resolution to make up for it tomorrow is not the cure. It is the first step of the next round.

Do the next hour differently

The hour after is where the cycle is either fed or broken, and almost nothing you do with food in that hour matters as much as what you do with the self-attack.

Stop eating and do not purge, over-exercise or fast to compensate. Those make the physical discomfort worse and strengthen the pattern.

Make yourself physically comfortable. Looser clothes, a glass of water, a short slow walk if that feels all right, somewhere to sit that is not where it happened.

Then let the discomfort pass without commentary. It does pass, usually within a couple of hours, and it is not an emergency.

Say something to yourself that is both kind and true

Not it is fine, because you do not believe that. Something accurate.

That was more than I wanted. I was hungry and tired and had a bad day. Eating too much in one evening does not undo anything. My next meal is normal.

That last sentence is the whole of it. The single most useful thing is to eat your next ordinary meal at the ordinary time.

Eat normally tomorrow, deliberately

This feels backward and it is what breaks the cycle. Breakfast, lunch, dinner, something in between if you are hungry.

Skipping meals after a big night guarantees the same evening again within a few days, because you arrive at six o'clock underfed and the pressure is back. Regular eating is the single most effective change people make here, and it works better than any amount of resolve.

Find out what it was actually about

Food is sometimes about food, and often about something else. In the next day or two, when you can think, ask what was going on beforehand.

Were you underfed that day. Over-tired. Did something happen: a conversation, a rejection, a dull lonely evening, a feeling you did not want to be in.

Most people find two or three reliable triggers. That list is worth more than any rule about what you eat, because it tells you what the eating is doing and what else might do part of the same job.

Watch for the all-or-nothing word

The thinking that drives this is nearly always absolute. I have ruined it. I have no willpower. I might as well finish it now and start again Monday.

That is the thought that turns a single evening into a week. The accurate version: one evening of eating more than I planned, in a month where most evenings were fine.

If you can catch it mid-way, the same applies. Stopping after more than you meant to is a better outcome than deciding the day is lost, and it is allowed.

Take the moral weight out of it

Food is not virtue. You were not good last Tuesday and bad last night. The language of being good with food is where a lot of this pain comes from, and dropping it makes the whole subject quieter.

Notice the signs this is bigger than a habit

Some things point to an eating difficulty that deserves real help rather than another plan.

Eating large amounts with a sense of being out of control, regularly. Making yourself sick, using laxatives, or exercising to cancel food. Eating in secret and hiding it. Food and your body taking up most of your thinking. Fasting for long stretches. Feeling that your worth depends on what you eat or what you weigh.

Those are common, they are not a character problem, and they respond well to treatment. People wait years before mentioning them, usually out of shame, and almost everyone says afterward that saying it out loud was the hardest part.

When to get help

Please talk to your doctor if this happens regularly, if you are compensating afterward in any way, or if food has become the loudest thing in your head. Ask specifically about eating disorder services or a dietitian who works with this, and say the plain truth rather than the tidier version, since what is available often depends on it. A therapist can help with the shame and the all-or-nothing thinking that keeps the cycle turning, and that part is as treatable as the eating itself.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
