Feeling Nothing at All

Not sad, not anxious, just blank. Numbness is one of the least talked-about ways of struggling, and it can feel like there is nothing to fix. What it usually means, and how to start feeling again.

DepressionTrauma Visitor 903 words

Something happens that should matter. Good news, bad news, a friend crying, a song you used to love. And you feel nothing. Not calm. Nothing. Like watching your own life through a window. You go through the day, say the right things, and inside there is a flat gray quiet where the feelings used to be.

People rarely ask for help with numbness, because it does not feel like a problem so much as an absence of one. There is no pain to point to. But if you are reading this and recognizing yourself, that absence is worth taking seriously. Numbness is one of the most common ways a person can be struggling without looking like it.

Where the numbness comes from

Numbness is usually protection. When feelings get too big, too painful, or go on too long, the mind has a way of turning the volume down. It is a survival move, and it often works, for a while.

Some common sources:

Overwhelm. Weeks or months of stress, grief, or fear that never let up. At some point the system stops responding, the way a muscle stops responding when it has been clenched too long.

Loss. After a death, a breakup, or a major change, many people expect grief to feel like sadness and are frightened when it feels like nothing at all. That flatness is often grief in its first form.

Trauma. After something frightening or violating, the mind can shut the feeling part down to get you through. Sometimes it stays shut long after the danger is over.

Depression. Low mood does not always feel low. For many people it feels like emptiness, as if the color has drained out of everything. Nothing is enjoyable, nothing is terrible, nothing is anything.

Burnout. Caring for others, working without rest, or holding it together for too long can leave a person hollowed out and unable to feel much of anything.

Why it is worth paying attention to

Numbness protects you from pain, but it does not know how to protect you only from pain. It turns down everything. Joy, love, interest, the taste of food, the pleasure of a friend's company. It also cuts you off from the signals that tell you what you need, which makes it hard to make decisions, connect with people, or even know what is wrong.

And numbness can hide things that matter. Because there is no loud feeling, the flatness can go on for months while a person tells themselves they are fine.

What helps

There is no switch to flip. But there are ways to start inviting feeling back, gently.

Go through the body

Feelings live in the body, and the body is often the easiest way back to them. Cold water on your face. A hot shower. A hard walk. Stretching until something aches a little. Holding something with texture. Eating something with a strong taste. You are not trying to feel good. You are trying to feel anything, and the senses are a door that is rarely locked.

Name what you notice, however small

A few times a day, stop and ask: what is here right now? The answer may be nothing. Say that. Nothing, flat, blank. Even naming the absence is a form of contact with yourself. Sometimes, underneath the nothing, there is a faint something: tired, heavy, a little irritated. Name that too. Feelings often return in tiny amounts before they return in full.

Do the things you used to love, without expecting to love them

Numbness tells you there is no point in the walk, the music, the call with your friend, because you will not enjoy it. Do it anyway, as an experiment rather than a pleasure. You may feel nothing the first five times. The sixth time there may be a flicker. Feeling tends to come back through doing, not through waiting to feel like doing.

Let people in a little

Numbness pulls you away from others, and isolation deepens it. You do not have to explain yourself. You can just be near people, or say the true thing: I have been feeling really flat lately, and I do not know why. Being seen, even slightly, often stirs something.

Be patient with what comes back

When feeling starts to return, it does not always return as joy. Often the first thing through the door is what the numbness was covering: sadness, anger, fear. That can be frightening and can make you want to shut back down. It is a sign the system is waking up. Let it come in small doses, and get support for it.

If you have thoughts of not wanting to be here

Numbness can sometimes come with a quiet sense that nothing matters, including whether you go on. If you notice thoughts like that, please tell someone today. In the US you can call or text 988 at any time.

When to get help

If the flatness has lasted more than a few weeks, if it followed something frightening or a major loss, if you cannot remember the last time you enjoyed anything, or if it is affecting your work or your relationships, please talk to your doctor or a therapist. Numbness responds well to treatment once someone understands what it is protecting you from, and you do not have to work that out alone. You were built to feel. It can come back.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
