Walking When You Do Not Want to Be in Your Head

Not exercise, not a step count. A walk taken specifically because thinking has stopped being useful. How to use one deliberately when you are stuck, spiraling, or flat.

MovementGrounding Visitor 897 words

There is a particular state where thinking has stopped helping. You have been over the problem eleven times. You are not analyzing anymore, you are just circling, and every lap leaves you slightly worse. Sitting still with it makes it louder.

A walk is unreasonably good for this, and not for the reasons walks usually get recommended. Forget fitness and step counts. This is about using movement as a tool for a mind that has gotten stuck inside itself.

Why it works when sitting does not

A few things happen at once. Your visual field starts moving, which occupies the part of your attention that was being spent on the loop. Your body has a rhythm to attend to. The world supplies input that is not you: weather, dogs, a delivery truck, a tree that has started turning.

Walking also changes the shape of your thoughts rather than only their volume. People often find that a problem they could not hold still enough to see becomes clearer at minute fifteen, and that it arrives sideways rather than as the result of effort.

And it gives a body full of stress hormones something to do with them. Worry is a physical state as much as a mental one, and standing up and moving is one of the few things that reliably discharges it.

Go without a destination

This is the main instruction. A walk to the store is an errand; your mind stays on the errand. A walk with no purpose lets your attention loosen, which is the whole point.

Pick a direction and go. Ten minutes out, ten minutes back. You do not need a route, a park, or good weather. A dull suburban loop works.

Leave the headphones out, at least sometimes

A podcast makes the walk pleasant and fills the space you were trying to open. If your mind is circling hard, try one walk with nothing in your ears. It will be uncomfortable for the first few minutes. That discomfort is the loop not getting what it wants, and it usually passes around the ten-minute mark.

On flat, heavy days, music is different. Something you like can lift a low mood in a way silence will not. Save the silence for the spiraling days.

Use your eyes on purpose

If the circling is loud, give your attention a job outside your head. Name what you see as you pass it, in the plainest terms. Red door. Cracked pavement. Two crows. Somebody's wet laundry.

This sounds childish and it is the single most effective thing in this article. Naming what is in front of you is not compatible with rehearsing an argument, and doing it for two minutes often breaks the loop entirely.

Let the pace match the state

If you are agitated, start fast. Walk it out, hard enough that your breathing changes, for the first five minutes, then let it settle. Trying to stroll calmly while your body is full of adrenaline rarely works; meeting the energy and then letting it fall is easier.

If you are flat and heavy, the opposite. Slow, short, no standard to meet. Getting to the end of the street counts as the whole walk.

Take the same walk repeatedly

There is a benefit to a route you know by heart: you do not have to navigate, so more attention is free. A familiar loop also becomes a place your mind associates with settling, which means it starts working faster over time. Many people build up one walk they do most days, in all weather, and come to rely on it.

Do it before the decision, not after

If something needs deciding, walk first and decide after, rather than walking to talk yourself into something. Thinking done while moving tends to be less rigid and less fearful, and you will often find your actual preference somewhere in the second half of the walk.

If you have a phone in your pocket, that is fine; leaving it in your pocket is what matters. A walk spent scrolling is a different activity.

Bring someone, sometimes

A walk with a friend is one of the best-designed conversations there is: side by side rather than face to face, with something to look at, no bill arriving, and a natural end. Hard things get said on walks that would not get said across a table. If you have something you have been avoiding telling someone, try telling them while walking.

And take the dog, if there is one. The requirement to go out regardless of your mood is doing more for you than the dog knows.

When a walk is not enough

Movement helps a great deal and it is not treatment. If your mind is circling constantly, if worry is running your days, if you are flat for weeks rather than an afternoon, or if you cannot get out of the house at all, please talk to your doctor or a therapist. A walk is a good tool and a poor substitute for help with something that has settled in. And if you ever find yourself hopeless or thinking of harming yourself, reach out that day. In the US you can call or text 988 at any time. Meanwhile, on an ordinary bad afternoon, putting your shoes on and going outside is still one of the most reliable things available to you.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
