Being Bad at Resting

You sit down and last four minutes before finding something to do. Why rest feels so uncomfortable for some people, and how to get better at it without turning it into a project.

PerfectionismSelf-Care Visitor 861 words

You have an afternoon with nothing in it. By quarter past two you have cleaned something, answered two emails and looked at the garden assessingly, and the sitting down has not happened.

When you do stop, you feel vaguely guilty, slightly itchy, and strangely tired without being rested.

Why stopping is uncomfortable

For a lot of people, being busy is not a schedule. It is a way of feeling all right about themselves.

If your sense of being worth something is tied to being useful, then an hour of doing nothing is an hour with no evidence in it, and the discomfort that follows is not laziness avoided; it is the absence of something you rely on.

There is often a history: a house where rest had to be earned, a parent who was always doing, years of work where only output counted. That teaches a rule nobody ever says out loud, which is that you are allowed to stop once everything is finished — and everything is never finished.

And stopping lets things catch up with you. Grief, dread, the conversation you have not had. Busy is an extremely effective way of not feeling something, which is why the first quiet hour is often the hardest.

Notice what you do instead

Most people in this position do not rest, they switch tasks.

Scrolling, which is stimulation rather than rest. Tidying. Admin. A podcast while cleaning while planning the week. Working out hard enough that it is another item on the list.

None of those are bad. They are not rest, though, and a day full of them will leave you depleted while looking productive.

The test is simple: afterward, do you feel more capable or less?

Define what rest means for you

Rest is not one thing, and the version everyone recommends may not be yours.

Some people need stillness: lying down, staring out of a window, a bath, sitting in the garden.

Some need absorption: a book, a jigsaw, cooking something slow, a long walk, playing an instrument badly. The mind rests when it is occupied by something undemanding.

Some need company, and some need nobody at all. Some need to be outside.

Write down the three things that genuinely leave you feeling better afterward. That list is more useful than any general advice, and it is probably shorter than you expect.

Give it a start and an end

This is the trick that makes it possible for people who cannot simply relax.

Open-ended rest is uncomfortable, so put edges on it. Twenty minutes. Until the kettle boils twice. One episode. Until three o'clock. A timer, oddly, makes it feel permitted.

And schedule it like a commitment rather than waiting for a gap, because the gap does not come. Two half-hours in the diary with a name beats a vague intention to take it easy at the weekend.

Expect the first ten minutes to be unpleasant

The itchiness is the part people take as evidence that rest does not suit them.

It is not. It is what happens when an activated system slows down, plus a bit of withdrawal from the usefulness you were running on. It passes, usually somewhere in the second ten minutes.

So decide in advance that you will stay put through the fidgeting. Put the phone in another room, because that is what the itch reaches for.

And if something underneath comes up when you stop — a feeling, a problem you have been outrunning — that is information rather than a reason to get up. Note it and look at it later, on purpose.

Deal with the guilt out loud

The guilt arrives with sentences, so answer them with plainer ones.

I should be doing something. Rest is what makes the something possible. I have not earned this. Nobody earns rest; it is maintenance, like sleep. Other people manage more. Other people are not doing my week.

It also helps enormously to see other people rest without apology. If everyone around you is busy at all times, notice that and be the person who sits down.

Do not optimize it

The final trap, especially for perfectionists: turning rest into a performance with a routine, an app and a morning schedule.

If your recovery has milestones, it is another project. Keep it unimpressive. Nothing to track, nothing to be good at, no before-and-after.

The measure of a restful hour is simply that the next hour went better.

When to get help

Please talk to a doctor if you are exhausted in a way that rest does not touch, if you have been running on empty for months, if you are not sleeping, or if you cannot stop without feeling panicky.

A therapist is the right place for the part underneath — the belief that you are only all right while you are useful, which is common, understandable and very workable. And if being still has become unbearable because of what comes up when you are quiet, say that plainly to a counselor; that is a specific, treatable thing rather than a personality trait, and it is one of the better reasons to make an appointment.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
