Napping Without Wrecking Your Night

Naps have a bad reputation they half deserve. Why twenty minutes or ninety work and forty-five does not, when in the day to take one, and who should not be napping at all.

SleepHabits Visitor 882 words

You are wiped out at three in the afternoon. There is a sofa. And somewhere in your head is a rule that says napping will ruin tonight, so you push through instead, and then you are useless until six and still not sleeping well.

Naps have a bad reputation they half deserve. Done badly they do wreck the night. Done with a little thought they are one of the most useful things available to a tired person.

What actually makes a nap backfire

Two things, and they are both about timing rather than the nap itself.

The first is length. Sleep runs in cycles, and surfacing from the deep middle of one leaves you groggy, heavy and worse than before. That fog is where the nap gets blamed, and it is a landing problem rather than a sleep problem.

The second is lateness. The sleepiness that builds through the day is what gets you off at bedtime. A long nap at six in the evening spends that, and then you lie there at eleven wide awake, and conclude that naps are not for you.

Keep it to twenty minutes, or go long on purpose

Those are the two versions that work, and the middle ground is the one to avoid.

Twenty minutes, or anything up to about twenty-five, keeps you in light sleep. You wake quickly, you feel better within a few minutes, and your night is almost untouched. This is the one to use on an ordinary tired afternoon.

Ninety minutes gets you through a full cycle and out the other side, which also lands cleanly, but it is a real chunk of sleep and it will affect your night. Save that for a genuine deficit, like after a bad night with a baby or a night shift.

Forty-five to sixty minutes is the version that leaves people feeling terrible, and it is the length most accidental naps turn out to be.

Set an alarm. Nobody wakes themselves at twenty minutes reliably, and the fear of oversleeping is itself enough to stop you dropping off.

Do it early enough

The general rule is before the middle of your afternoon, and well before seven hours ahead of your bedtime.

For most people that means between about one and three in the afternoon, which happens to be when the natural dip in alertness arrives anyway. You are not fighting your body at that hour, which is why those naps come easily.

After about four, you are borrowing from tonight. If you are desperate later than that, keep it to ten or fifteen minutes.

Make the twenty minutes work

A short nap is more about letting go than sleeping deeply, and a few small things help.

Somewhere dim, an eye mask, something over you, because body temperature drops as you fall asleep and being slightly cold keeps you up.

Not your bed, if you have insomnia at night. Keeping the bed for nighttime sleep matters, and a chair or a sofa is better for you.

A coffee immediately before, if you need to be sharp afterward, which sounds backward and works, because caffeine takes about twenty minutes to arrive and lands as you wake.

And do not chase it. If sleep does not come, lying still with your eyes shut for twenty minutes does more for you than another hour of pushing on. Rest counts even without sleep.

Know whether you should be napping at all

This is the honest caveat, because for one group of people naps make things worse.

If you lie awake at night, if getting to sleep is the problem, then daytime sleep is working against you, and the standard advice for insomnia is to stop napping while you get the nights back. That is hard and it is the right order to do it in.

If you sleep fine at night and are still exhausted all day, that is worth a doctor rather than a nap. Needing to sleep in the afternoon every day, falling asleep without meaning to, or waking unrefreshed however long you sleep are all things with causes worth finding.

Stop treating it as a moral question

A lot of the trouble here is not physiological. It is the belief that a nap is laziness, which means people either refuse one they need or take one and feel bad for the rest of the day.

A twenty-minute nap is a tool, in the same category as a glass of water. It makes the afternoon better, it makes you less irritable with the people around you, and it is cheaper than the three coffees that are the usual alternative.

When to get help

Please see your doctor if you are sleepy every day no matter how much you sleep, if you fall asleep without intending to, if you snore heavily or wake gasping, or if you need naps to get through a day that used to be fine without them, because those point to things that are diagnosable and treatable rather than to a character flaw. Say it plainly if your mood has been low alongside the tiredness, since the two travel together often. And if the nights are the real problem, ask specifically about treatment for insomnia, which works well and is not primarily about medication.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
