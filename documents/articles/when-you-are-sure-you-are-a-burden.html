When You Are Sure You Are a Burden

The thought that people would be better off without you feels like a plain fact. Where that belief comes from, why it is so convincing, and what to do with it tonight.

DepressionSelf-Compassion Visitor 901 words

It does not arrive as a feeling. It arrives as a conclusion, already settled: I am a drain on the people around me, they are being polite, their lives would be easier without me in them.

And because it feels like a fact rather than a mood, you do not argue with it. You act on it. You cancel things, you stop asking, you reply later and shorter, you take up less room.

Where the belief comes from

Almost nobody arrives at this out of nowhere. It usually has a source, and naming the source loosens it a little.

Low mood produces it directly. Depression does not only lower how you feel, it alters what seems true, and one of the most common distortions is reading yourself as a burden. When mood lifts, that thought goes quiet, which tells you something about where it came from.

Illness, pain or disability can bring it, especially where you need practical help.

Being told it, by someone who was angry or struggling or cruel, once or for years.

Growing up having to be no trouble, where needing things was unwelcome.

Being genuinely in a hard stretch, needing more than usual, and having nothing to give back yet.

Why it is so convincing

It has the shape of evidence. You can list the things people do for you, the times you canceled, the money, the lifts, the conversations about your problems. The list is real.

What is missing is everything else: what you give that you do not count, what people actually feel about helping you, and the plain fact that needing help is a condition every human passes through rather than a defect in you.

Depression also removes your ability to see yourself from outside. It is genuinely hard to notice that you are being read by the people around you as a person they love and not as a cost.

Do not act on it tonight

This is the most useful rule. The belief wants action: withdraw, cancel, apologize, go quiet, take up less space. Each of those makes it stronger tomorrow, because distance confirms what it told you.

So treat it as a thought to be carried rather than obeyed. I am having the burden thought again. I am not going to make any decisions about my relationships while it is this loud.

Check it, rather than argue with it

Arguing with yourself about your worth is unwinnable. What can be checked is the prediction.

Write down what you believe will happen: if I ask my sister for a lift, she will sigh and I will see it. Then ask, and write down what actually happened.

Try asking for something small on purpose, once a week, specifically to gather evidence. Most people find the answers are warm and unremarkable, and that unremarkableness is the most convincing thing available.

Let people answer for themselves

You are currently making a decision on behalf of other people, about how much they mind, without asking them.

So ask one person, plainly: I have been worried I am too much for you lately. They will usually be surprised, and sometimes hurt that you thought it. If there is something, you will hear a version you can actually work with, which is far better than the version you invented.

Notice what you do give

Keep a note for two weeks of things you did for other people, however small: a message, listening, a lift, remembering something, being funny, being there. This list is the one you never make, and it is as real as the other one.

If you are in a stretch of needing more than you give, that is a stretch rather than an identity. Relationships are settled over years, not weeks, and almost everyone has years where they take more.

Reciprocity is not a ledger

The belief treats closeness as an account that has to balance. That is not how it works for anybody. People help the people they love and do not run the arithmetic, and when they are the ones struggling, they will want the same from you.

Being needed by someone you love is not a cost. Most people would rather be asked than protected from it.

The dangerous version of this thought

Sometimes this does not stop at I am too much. It becomes they would be better off without me, or a sense that your absence would be a relief.

That is not a conclusion to sit with alone, and it is not the truth. It is one of the clearest signals that the illness is doing the thinking.

If you are there, please tell somebody today: your doctor, an urgent service, a person in your house. In the US you can call or text 988 at any time.

When to get help

Please talk to your doctor or a therapist if this belief is around most days, if it has lasted more than a couple of weeks, or if it is making you pull away from people. It is one of the most recognizable features of low mood, it responds well to treatment, and a therapist can help you test it rather than just arguing with it. If it began after an illness or alongside needing care, say that too, since there is practical support that lowers how much you have to ask of the people close to you.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
