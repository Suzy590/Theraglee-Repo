Loving Someone Who Is Depressed

They have withdrawn, and nothing you try reaches them. What actually helps a partner or friend in a depression, what quietly makes it worse, and how to stay well yourself.

DepressionRelationships Visitor 829 words

The person you love has gone somewhere you cannot follow. They are in the house and not really there. They cancel things, sleep badly or endlessly, and answer in single words. You have suggested a walk, fresh air, seeing friends, and each suggestion has landed like another small failure for both of you.

You feel helpless, and probably also frustrated, and then guilty about the frustration. All of that is normal, and none of it means you are doing this badly.

Understand what you are dealing with

Depression is not sadness and it does not respond to cheering up. It flattens the machinery that produces motivation, interest, and hope. So when they cannot get out of bed, it is not that they will not. When they say nothing will help, that is the illness talking with total conviction.

This matters practically, because most of what does not work comes from treating it as a mood rather than an illness.

What tends not to help

Suggesting solutions. Have you tried exercise, sunlight, gratitude. They have heard it, they may even agree, and hearing it again mostly communicates that you do not understand how heavy things are.

Reasoning them out of it. But you have so much to be grateful for is true and useless. Depression is not a failure of perspective.

Taking the withdrawal personally. They are not pulling away because of you. The illness pulls people away from everyone, including themselves.

Constant reassurance-seeking. Asking repeatedly whether they still love you or whether you are doing enough puts your distress on top of theirs.

Waiting for them to ask. People in a depression usually cannot initiate. Waiting for a request is waiting for the one thing the illness takes away.

What does help

Show up without an agenda. Sit with them. Watch something. Be in the room. Presence with no expectation of improvement is worth more than any suggestion.

Say what you see, kindly. I have noticed you have not been yourself for a while, and I am not going anywhere. Naming it gently is a relief; most people in it are convinced they are hiding it badly and being judged for it.

Do the concrete things. Make food, do a load of laundry, drive them somewhere, sit with them while they make the phone call. Practical help is the most usable kind, because it does not require energy they do not have.

Make invitations easy and keep making them. I am walking to the corner at four, come if you want, no problem if not. Then keep asking even after many nos. The asking itself says they are still wanted, and one day they will say yes.

Help them get treatment, practically. Depression makes finding a therapist or booking a doctor's appointment nearly impossible. Offer to look up names, make the call, sit in the waiting room, drive. That is often the single most useful thing a person on the outside can do.

Ask about safety, directly

If you are worried, ask plainly: are you having thoughts of hurting yourself, or of not wanting to be here? Asking does not plant the idea. It is a relief to be asked, and it is the only way to know.

If the answer is yes, take it seriously, stay with them, help them contact their doctor or a crisis line, and remove easy means if you can. In the US you can call or text 988 at any time, and you can call it for advice about someone else too.

Keep your own life going

You cannot pour from an empty cup, and loving someone through a depression is a long stretch, not a weekend. Keep seeing your own friends. Keep the things that restore you. Tell at least one person what is happening so you are not carrying it alone.

This is not disloyalty. A person who is well is far more use than one who has gone down with them.

Let yourself have the hard feelings

You will feel frustrated, lonely, resentful, and sometimes you will want to shake them. Those feelings do not make you unkind; they make you a person doing something difficult. Find somewhere to put them that is not the depressed person: a friend, a journal, your own therapist.

Know what is not yours

You cannot cure it. You cannot love someone out of a depression, and if it does not lift, that is not evidence that you failed. What you can do is stay, keep things practical, and help them reach the people whose job this is.

Get support for yourself

Please consider talking to a therapist of your own, or finding a support group for families. And encourage the person you love toward treatment, gently and repeatedly. Depression is one of the most treatable things there is, which is worth saying out loud in a house where it currently feels permanent. It will not always be like this, for them or for you.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
