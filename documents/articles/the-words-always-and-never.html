The Words Always and Never

You never get it right, they always do this, nothing ever changes. Why absolute words make everything feel permanent, and how a smaller, truer sentence changes what you do next.

Thinking TrapsCBT Visitor 829 words

Listen to what you say when you are upset and the same two words keep turning up. I never get anything right. You always do this. Nothing ever works out. I will always be like this.

They sound like description. They behave like a verdict, and the verdict is what flattens you.

What the absolutes actually do

Three things, all of them unhelpful.

They make the thing permanent. A mistake is something you can fix. Always getting it wrong is a fact about you, and facts cannot be addressed, only endured.

They make it total. I had a bad meeting leaves the rest of the week standing. I am hopeless at this takes the whole job down with it.

They end the conversation. If someone always does this, there is nothing to discuss, no exception to notice, and no request to make. People who say always in an argument almost never get the change they wanted, because the other person is now defending their character rather than hearing about Tuesday.

And the words are almost never accurate, which is the useful part. Nothing in a human life happens always.

Catch them rather than argue with yourself

You do not need to talk yourself out of the feeling. You only need to notice the word, which is far easier.

Keep an ear out for always, never, nothing, everyone, nobody, completely, ruined, and the ones in your own particular style. When one arrives, say to yourself: that is the absolute. Not that is wrong, since that invites a debate you will lose. Just notice it, and then make the sentence smaller.

Put a number or a time on it

The fastest repair is to make the claim specific enough to check.

Instead of I never finish anything: I have not finished the last two things I started, and I finished the kitchen in March.

Instead of nothing ever changes: this has not changed in the four months since I asked.

Instead of everyone thinks I am useless: two people in that meeting looked unimpressed.

Notice that none of these are reassuring. They are not supposed to be. They are the same facts, stated at a size you can do something about, which is the entire trick.

Look for the one exception

Absolutes are destroyed by a single counterexample, which is why your mind works hard not to find one.

When was a time this did not happen. When did you handle it. When did they do the thing you wanted. There is almost always one, and once there is one, always is gone and you are left with a frequency, which is a solvable problem.

Change what you say out loud, not just what you think

This matters most in arguments, and it is the quickest way to improve them.

Swap you always for a specific event and a request. Rather than you always leave me to deal with it, try: I handled bedtime on my own three nights this week and I am tired. Can you take Tuesday and Thursday.

It is less satisfying to say. It is far more likely to get you what you asked for, because there is nothing in it to deny.

Watch the ones aimed at the future

Always and never do their worst work on what is coming. I will never get over this. It will always be like this. I am never going to find anyone.

Nobody knows the future, and you especially do not know it while you are in the middle of the thing. The honest version is: right now I cannot see how this changes, which is true, bearable, and leaves the door open.

Where the habit comes from

If you grew up around sweeping judgments, or being told you always or never did something, that way of speaking about yourself is borrowed rather than chosen. It usually takes a while to hear in your own voice, and hearing it is most of the work.

Expect it to take weeks of noticing

This is a habit of language, so it changes the way habits change: slowly, with a lot of catching yourself afterward. The first win is noticing an absolute an hour later. Then the same day. Then as it leaves your mouth. That is progress, not failure, and the payoff is that your conclusions stop being so much worse than your situation.

When to get help

If your thinking runs in absolutes most of the time, if the conclusions are mostly about your own worth, or if low mood or anxiety is making every setback feel permanent, this is core ground for cognitive behavioral therapy and it responds well. A therapist can hear the patterns you cannot hear yet, which is the part that is genuinely hard to do alone. Please talk to your doctor too if the flatness has lasted more than a couple of weeks, since the thinking often lifts once the mood underneath it is treated.
