When People Keep Telling You How Strong You Are

Meant as the highest compliment and one of the loneliest things to hear. Why it closes the conversation, what you quietly stop saying, and how to answer it next time.

ResilienceLoneliness Visitor 873 words

You are so strong. I do not know how you do it. I could never cope with what you are coping with.

People mean it kindly. It is meant as the highest compliment available, and it is one of the loneliest things to be told, because of what it quietly does: it makes your coping the thing about you, and it closes a door you might have wanted to walk through.

Why it lands the way it does

It turns a survival into an identity. You were managing because managing was the only option, and now it has become a quality you possess.

It also sets a standard. Once you are the strong one, having a bad week risks letting people down, and that makes the bad week harder to mention.

And it often ends the conversation rather than opening it. Being told you are coping well closes the subject. There is no obvious reply except thank you, and then nobody asks how it actually is.

The worst version is when it is said with relief. Some people say it because they would rather not hear the detail, and you can hear that in it.

Notice what you have stopped saying

The cost shows up as a slow narrowing of what you report.

You answer fine automatically. You skip the bad days when somebody asks how the week was. You tell the version of the story where you handled it. You stop mentioning the practical help you need, because strong people are assumed not to need any.

Then you end up genuinely alone in it, surrounded by people who think you are doing well, and the gap between the two is its own particular weight.

Say one true thing to one person

The way out is not announcing that you are not coping. It is much smaller.

Pick one person and give them one honest sentence. Honestly, this week has been bad. I am managing and I am very tired. I need somebody to know that it is hard.

Say it once and see what happens. Most people are relieved rather than shocked, and some will say it is about time.

If it goes badly with that person, that is information about them rather than about whether you should have said it.

Have an answer ready for the compliment

It helps to have a line, because in the moment you will say thank you and change the subject.

Something short and true. I do not have much choice, to be honest. It looks better from outside than it feels. I am doing it, and I would not say I am doing it well.

Or ask for what you want instead. Thank you. What would help most is if you called me on a Sunday.

That last one works because people who say you are so strong are often willing to help and have no idea what to offer.

Take the help that is actually offered

The flip side of being seen as strong is that offers come vaguely and you refuse them automatically.

So say yes to something specific this week. Let them bring food. Let them take an afternoon. Let them sit in the waiting room. Let the practical thing be done by somebody else.

Being helped is a skill, and for people in this position it is usually the rustiest one. It is also what keeps the strength from being the only thing holding you up.

Separate what you have done from what you have to keep doing

You can be proud of having got through something and still want it to stop. Those are not in conflict, and the compliment tends to blur them.

So say both to yourself. I have handled a great deal. I am also running low and I need some of this to change.

Strength is not a renewable resource that grows with praise. It runs down, and what refills it is rest, help and somebody knowing the truth.

Watch the long version

If you have been the strong one for years, this becomes hard to see from inside. The usual signs are exhaustion that sleep does not fix, a short fuse at home, feeling nothing much about anything, and a quiet resentment of people who are allowed to struggle.

None of that means you have failed at being strong. It means a person has been carrying something for a long time without enough help, which is what would happen to anyone.

When to get help

Please talk to a therapist if you have been coping with something heavy and have nobody who knows how it actually is, because having one place where you do not have to perform is most of what therapy offers and it is worth a great deal. Tell your doctor if you are exhausted, not sleeping, flat for weeks, or drinking more to get through the evenings, and say the whole thing rather than the managed version. If you are caring for somebody, ask a professional specifically about support for carers, which exists more often than people realize. And if a day comes when you do not want to keep going, please tell somebody rather than holding it on your own.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
