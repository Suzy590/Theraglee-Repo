The Check-Up You Have Been Avoiding

The letter came months ago and the appointment still has not been made. Why avoidance grows the longer it goes on, and how to get the call made without a lecture from yourself.

MotivationShame Visitor 894 words

There is a letter somewhere in the house. A screening invitation, a dental reminder, a note saying you are due for bloods, a referral you never followed up. It has been there long enough that you have stopped seeing it, and every so often you remember and feel a small drop in your stomach.

Nothing about this is a mystery to you. You know it should be done. Knowing is not the part that is missing.

Why it gets harder rather than easier

Avoidance works in the short run, which is exactly the problem. Each time you decide not to deal with it, you get a small immediate relief, and that relief teaches your brain that not dealing with it is the move that helps.

Then the delay itself becomes a second reason to avoid. Now there is the appointment to face and the awkwardness of how long you left it. People go years on that loop, and the longer it runs the more certain they become that the reception desk will judge them, which nobody there will.

There is often a third layer: the fear of what might be found. If you never go, you never get told, and a part of you treats that as safety. It is worth naming plainly, because it is usually the real reason, and it is the one piece of this that avoidance makes genuinely worse.

Take the shame out of the way first

The story people carry is some version of: a person who looks after themselves would have done this ages ago, so what does it say about me.

It says you are a person who finds a particular thing frightening or boring, and who has been putting it off, which is most people. Dentists, nurses and doctors see long gaps constantly. The gap is not what they are interested in. What happens next is.

That matters practically, not just emotionally, because shame keeps the avoidance running. Someone who feels bad about themselves for not having called is more likely to keep not calling, so softening the story is part of the mechanics, not a nicety.

Shrink the step until it is nearly nothing

Make the appointment is not one task. It is find the letter, find the number, work out the opening hours, be free when they are, get through, speak to a person, find a date, write it down. No wonder the day goes by.

So pick the smallest piece and do only that. Find the letter today and leave it on the counter. Save the number in your phone. Open the booking page and close it again. Book online if that is possible, since a form is far easier than a conversation when dread is the obstacle.

Attach it to something you are already doing. Phone it on the way home from the store. Book it while the kettle boils. People who wait to feel ready wait a long time, and the readiness usually arrives a minute after starting, not before.

Borrow somebody

Say out loud to one person that you are going to call tomorrow. Ask them to ask you about it. If the appointment itself is the frightening part, ask them to come and sit in the waiting room.

Being frightened of a medical appointment at forty or seventy is not childish. Taking someone with you is a practical step, not a confession.

Say the awkward thing when you get there

Worrying about having to explain the gap keeps a lot of people away, so prepare one short sentence and use it. I have not been for years and I was nervous about coming. Or: I am frightened of the dentist and that is why it has been so long.

Said once, it is over, and in almost every case the answer is a version of I am glad you came. It also gets you better care, because they will go slower and tell you what they are doing.

Decide what you will do about the fear of bad news

If the real obstacle is what they might find, it helps to look straight at it. Most of these appointments find nothing. Where something is found, finding it earlier is the whole point of going, and the things screening looks for are far more treatable caught early.

That is not a comforting slogan. It is the actual arithmetic, and it is the opposite of what avoidance assumes.

Then write down the date and treat it as fixed

Once it is booked, put it in the calendar, tell the person who is coming with you, and stop renegotiating it with yourself. The deciding is done. All that is left is going.

When to get help

Please talk to your doctor if there is a symptom you have been avoiding having looked at, and say how long it has been without apologizing for it. If fear of needles, dentists, enclosed spaces or examinations is strong enough that you cannot get through the door, tell them that too, since there are good treatments for that specific fear and a therapist can help with it in a small number of sessions. If avoiding things has spread across your life, or you have lost interest in looking after yourself at all, that is worth mentioning to a doctor or a counselor as its own thing.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
