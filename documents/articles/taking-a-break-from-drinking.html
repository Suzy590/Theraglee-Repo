Taking a Break From Drinking

You are not asking whether to quit forever, only what a few weeks without it would be like. How to give a break a fair chance, and what the first two weeks usually tell you.

MotivationGoals Visitor 896 words

You are not in crisis. You are drinking most evenings, more than you planned on some of them, and you have started wondering what you would feel like without it for a while.

That is a good question and it deserves a real test rather than a vague intention to cut down, which is the version that quietly becomes nothing.

Pick a length and write it down

Cutting down is not a plan, because every evening becomes a negotiation. A defined break takes the deciding out of each night, which is where the effort actually goes.

Two weeks tells you something. Four tells you more, because the sleep and the mood changes mostly show up in the second half. Pick a start date in the next few days rather than next month, and tell one person.

Before you start, check one thing with a doctor

This part matters. If you drink heavily and daily, stopping suddenly can be dangerous, with shaking, sweating, sickness, hallucinations or seizures.

If you drink every day and more than a little, if you have ever had withdrawal symptoms, if you have a drink in the morning or to steady yourself, or if you are physically unwell, talk to a doctor before you stop rather than during. A managed reduction is safer and they will not lecture you.

Work out what the drink is doing for you

A break works much better when you know what you are replacing, because the function is the thing that defeats willpower.

For most people it is one of a few jobs. Marking the end of the day. Taking the edge off anxiety. Getting to sleep. Being able to talk at social things. Rewarding yourself. Numbing something specific.

Write down which it is for you, honestly, and then plan a substitute for that particular job rather than a general resolve to be better. An end-of-day ritual, a walk, a shower, a non-alcoholic drink you actually like in a proper glass, a call with someone.

Expect the first week to be unimpressive

People start a break expecting to feel fantastic and give up on day five when they feel worse. Early on you may sleep badly, feel irritable, and want it more in the evenings than you expected.

The good parts tend to arrive in week two and three: sleeping through, waking without the low-grade dread, a clearer head in the mornings, and often a surprising amount of extra time.

Plan the hard evenings in advance

Decide now what happens at the three predictable pressure points.

The first evening at home when the urge arrives at six o'clock. Have the substitute ready and in the house.

The first social event. Decide what you will drink, get it in your hand early, and have one short line ready: I am off it for a few weeks. No reason needed and no debate entered.

The first bad day, which is where most breaks end. Know in advance what you will do instead, and that one drink does not have to end the experiment.

Keep a line a day

A note each evening: did I drink, how I slept, mood out of ten, anything notable. It takes ten seconds and it is the whole point of the exercise, because at the end you will have evidence rather than an impression.

What the break is actually telling you

At the end, read your notes and ask three plain questions.

Was it easier or harder than you expected. Harder than expected is useful information rather than a failure.

What got better. Sleep, mood, mornings, money, weight of dread.

What happened when you could not have one. Relief at the end, irritation, planning around it, or genuinely not minding.

If you could not get through more than a day or two, if you felt physically unwell, or if the break was so difficult that you stopped early more than once, that is worth a conversation with a doctor rather than another try on your own.

Decide what happens next, deliberately

Most people land in one of three places. Back to as before, which at least you now know is a choice rather than a default. A lower, more deliberate amount, with some days off each week. Or realizing they would rather not, which a surprising number of people find once the first few weeks are behind them.

Any of those is a result. The thing you have gained either way is a decision you are making on purpose.

Do not let one evening end it

If you drink on day nine, you have not failed the whole thing. Write it down, notice what was happening, and carry on tomorrow. The all-or-nothing version of this is what makes people give up for years.

When to get help

Please talk to your doctor before stopping if you drink daily or heavily, since withdrawal can be medically serious and is manageable with support. Talk to them too if you have tried to cut back several times without it holding, if you drink to steady yourself, if it is affecting your work or your relationships, or if you are drinking to get away from something heavy like grief or a frightening memory. There is a great deal of effective help, including counseling and medication, and asking about a break is a completely reasonable way into that conversation.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
