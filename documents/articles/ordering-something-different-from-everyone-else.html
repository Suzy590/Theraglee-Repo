Ordering Something Different From Everyone Else

Saying no to the wine, the shared platter or the dessert can feel like making a scene. How to ask for what you actually need at the table without a speech or an apology.

AssertivenessFood Visitor 877 words

Everyone is ordering. There is a bottle on its way and a plan to share plates, and you need something else: no alcohol, no dairy, no gluten, smaller, plainer, separate, or just not that. And in the half second before you speak, it feels enormous.

So people go along with it. They have the wine they did not want, pick around the thing they cannot eat, say they are not hungry, and spend the evening managing a small problem they could have solved in one sentence at the start.

Why such a small thing feels so loud

Eating together is social glue, and anything that looks like stepping out of the shared thing can feel like a rejection of the group. The fear is not really about the food. It is about being difficult, drawing attention, making the host anxious, or being asked why.

The why is often the worst part. If the reason is private, a medical thing, sobriety, an eating difficulty you are working on, religion, or simply a preference you are tired of defending, you may dread one casual question more than the whole meal.

Short and flat works better than explained

The size of the explanation sets the size of the moment. A long reason invites questions. A short, unbothered sentence closes it.

I will stick with soda water tonight, thanks.

Could I get mine without cheese.

I am going to order my own rather than share, if that is all right.

None for me, thanks. Yes, I am sure.

Say it once, in the same tone you would use to ask for tap water, and then move the conversation on. People follow the lead you give them. If you treat it as unremarkable, it almost always becomes unremarkable.

You do not owe anyone the reason

This is worth saying plainly, because a lot of people believe otherwise. A reason is a courtesy, not a requirement, and no reason is better than a made-up one you will have to keep consistent.

If someone presses, repeat the same thing rather than escalating. Just not tonight, thanks. I am good. It is a long story and I am fine. Repetition is more effective than a better argument, and it is harder to argue with.

Say it earlier than feels necessary

Almost all of the difficulty lives in the first moment. Once the order is placed, the evening is just an evening.

So speak at the start. Tell the host when you accept the invitation, not when you arrive at the table. Tell the server when they take the order rather than waiting to see what arrives. Say it to the group before the bottle is poured rather than letting a full glass sit in front of you all night.

Where it matters medically, be clear rather than polite

A preference can be soft. An allergy, a celiac diagnosis or a serious intolerance cannot, and in a restaurant this is the one place to be plainly direct, because the kitchen needs to know.

I have a serious allergy to nuts, not a preference. Can you check what is in the sauce and whether it is cooked in the same pan.

That is not being difficult. It is giving people the information they need to do their job. Any decent kitchen would rather be told twice than told afterward.

Expect some friction from a few people, and plan the sentence

Most people do not care at all. A few will push, usually because they are uneasy about their own choices, and a couple will take your no personally, especially about alcohol.

Have one line ready for that and do not elaborate. Honestly, I am happier like this. Or: I know, I am no fun. Pass me the bread. Humor ends more of these than firmness does, and you are not obliged to win the exchange, only to finish it.

Sobriety deserves a special mention

If you are not drinking and would rather not discuss it, get a drink in your hand early, since an empty hand draws offers. Order first, so the table takes its lead from you. If pressure is likely, bring a line you have already decided on and tell one person at the table in advance so you are not the only one managing it.

Notice how much easier the next one is

The reason this is worth practicing on small things is that it is the same skill as everything else: asking for a change at work, telling someone what you need, declining an invitation. A table is a low-stakes place to find out that a plain request usually lands without incident, and that evidence transfers.

When to get help

If food in company brings real dread, if you are avoiding meals out, hiding what you eat, or finding that eating in front of people has become one of the harder parts of your week, please talk to your doctor or a therapist, since difficulty around eating responds well to support and is far more common than the silence around it suggests. If not drinking is hard to hold when you are around other people, that is worth saying to a professional too, and there is no shortage of good help for it.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
