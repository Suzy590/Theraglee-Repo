When an Urge Feels Impossible to Sit With

You want to send the message, open the bottle, quit on the spot, or do the thing you will regret. What to do in the twenty minutes when the pull is at its strongest.

DBTEmotion Regulation Visitor 916 words

The urge has arrived and it is enormous. Send the message that will end it. Open the bottle. Say the cruel thing you have ready. Quit, walk out, delete everything. Whatever your particular version is, it feels less like a choice than like a pressure that has to be released.

You know from experience that acting on it will make things worse. You also know that in this state, knowing that does not help much.

Here is what actually works in the twenty minutes that matter.

The urge has a shape

The most useful fact about urges is that they rise, peak, and fall. They do not increase forever, though they always feel as though they will. Most peak within about twenty minutes and then drop noticeably, whether or not you do anything about them.

That means your only job is to get to the other side of the peak without acting. Not to resist forever. Not to stop wanting it. Just to outlast one wave.

Say the wave out loud

Name it as it happens. This is an urge. It is at about a nine. It will come down. I do not have to do anything yet.

Naming an urge as a wave rather than an instruction changes what you are dealing with. You are no longer in an argument about whether to send the message; you are waiting out a weather event.

Put time between you and the action

Everything else in this article is a way of buying twenty minutes.

Make it physically harder. Put the phone in another room. Hand your keys to someone. Pour the drink down the sink. Leave the house. Close the laptop and take it somewhere else. The friction only has to hold for a short while.

Write it instead of sending it. Type the whole message, every word of it, in a notes app. Do not put it in the thread. Most people find the writing discharges a surprising amount, and the unsent version reads very differently an hour later.

Set a timer. Twenty minutes. Tell yourself you can revisit it when the timer goes, which is true. Almost nobody wants to, once it does.

Change your body, hard

Urges live in an activated body, and the fastest way to bring the intensity down is physical rather than mental.

Cold works better than almost anything. Cold water on your face, a cold shower, an ice cube held in your hand, stepping outside without a coat. It produces a genuine physiological shift within a minute or two.

So does intense exertion. Sprint to the end of the street. Twenty press-ups. Take the stairs fast. The goal is to spend the charge, not to exercise.

Slow breathing helps once the peak has passed, and is often too subtle during it. Out-breath longer than the in-breath, for a minute.

Occupy your hands and your attention

A fully occupied mind cannot hold the urge at full volume. It needs to be something demanding, though: scrolling is not enough, because it leaves plenty of room.

Something with your hands and some difficulty in it: cooking something that needs following, a puzzle, an instrument, cleaning a specific thing thoroughly, a video game that requires attention, counting backward from a hundred in sevens. Make it absorbing rather than pleasant.

Tell one person, in the moment

Text someone: I am about to do something stupid, talk to me for ten minutes. You do not need advice; you need company while the wave passes. Anyone who has ever done this for you will do it again.

If you have nobody available at that hour, a helpline serves the same purpose, and that is a completely legitimate use of one.

Afterward, look at what it was about

Once the wave has passed, and not during, ask what the urge was trying to do. Urges are usually trying to end an unbearable feeling: loneliness, shame, rage, fear, boredom that has something underneath it.

That feeling is the actual thing to work on, and it is much easier to see from the far side. Write down what happened, what set it off, and what you did instead. Over time that record shows you your own pattern, which is what lets you catch the next one earlier, when it is a four rather than a nine.

Plan for the next one now

The worst time to invent a strategy is mid-urge. So write your plan on a card while you are calm: the three physical things that work for you, the person you will text, where the phone goes. Then follow the card rather than deciding.

Count the ones you got through

Every wave you outlast makes the next one slightly less convincing, because you accumulate evidence that it passes. That evidence is the real mechanism here. Keep a tally if it helps.

When to get help

Please talk to a therapist if the urges are frequent, if they involve harming yourself, drinking or using, or actions that keep damaging your relationships or your work. There are therapies built specifically for this, and they teach a fuller version of what is above, with someone helping you practice. And if the urge is ever to hurt yourself, please treat it as the emergency it is and reach out immediately. In the US you can call or text 988 at any time. You do not have to win an argument with the urge. You only have to be slower than it is.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
