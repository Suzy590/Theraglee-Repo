Saying You Are Angry Before You Are Furious

You say nothing at two out of ten, nothing at five, and then everything at nine. How to raise an annoyance while it is still small enough to be heard.

AngerAssertiveness Visitor 878 words

They left the dishes again. You said nothing. They were late again. You said nothing, slightly louder in your head. Then on Thursday they made a joke about the shopping and you heard yourself say something about the last four years.

They look genuinely startled, because from where they are standing this came out of nowhere. And now the conversation is about your reaction rather than about the dishes.

The cost of waiting for nine

Most people have one speed for anger: silence, then eruption. The middle is empty.

Waiting has real costs. Nobody can act on something they do not know about, so the thing keeps happening. The resentment compounds, which means by the time you speak, the subject is no longer the dishes; it is everything.

And at nine out of ten you are not able to be clear. You generalize, you bring up history, you say the thing you will regret. Which gives the other person an easy exit: they get to be upset about how you spoke instead of looking at what you raised.

Speaking at three out of ten is uncomfortable and far more effective.

Why the middle feels impossible

Usually because of what you learned about anger.

If anger in your house meant shouting, slammed doors or somebody frightening, then any level of it feels dangerous, and silence feels like the responsible choice.

If you were the easy one, the peacemaker, the child who did not add to anybody's load, then mentioning an annoyance feels like being difficult.

And many people genuinely believe small things are not worth raising. The irony is that small things raised small stay small; small things swallowed become large.

What a three out of ten sounds like

Short, specific, about the thing rather than the person, and without a verdict attached.

Hey, the dishes were left again last night, can you do them before bed? Not: you never lift a finger.

I need to tell you something small before it gets big: when you are late and do not message, I spend the evening annoyed.

I did not like that joke. Said once, plainly, and then on with the evening.

Notice how little drama there is in those. That is the point. A small complaint said in a normal voice is not a confrontation; it is maintenance.

Say it close to the event

The longer the gap, the harder it is to raise and the less likely it is to land.

Same day, or next morning if the evening is not the moment. Not three weeks later, when they genuinely do not remember and you have rehearsed the case for the prosecution.

If you are too hot to speak well, take twenty minutes and come back. Too hot means raised heart rate, a speech forming, the urge to say something cutting. Twenty minutes of walking changes what you are capable of saying.

And pick the time deliberately: not mid-task, not in front of other people, not in the car on the way somewhere.

Name the level

This is a small trick that works unreasonably well: tell them how big it is.

This is a two out of ten, I just want to flag it. Or: this one actually matters to me, it is a seven.

People calibrate badly otherwise. They assume anything raised at all is serious, or that nothing is until you are shouting. Giving the number lets them respond proportionately, and it stops a two becoming an argument.

Expect it to be awkward at first

If you have been silent for years, the first few attempts will feel enormous and come out stiff. That is normal.

Start with the low-stakes ones: the wrong coffee, the radio volume, the meeting that always overruns. Practicing on small things builds the skill you will need for the big one.

And accept a mixed response. Some people will say thank you for telling me. Some will be defensive the first time and fine the second. What matters is that you said it at three rather than storing it for nine.

What to do with the anger you cannot raise

Some of it genuinely cannot be said: a boss who would punish it, a person who twists everything, someone who has died.

That still needs somewhere to go. Write it, walk it off, tell a friend, say it out loud in the car. Anger with no outlet does not evaporate; it leaks into the people who happen to be nearby.

When to get help with it

Please talk to a doctor or a therapist if your anger is frightening you or anyone else, if you have broken things or made contact with someone in anger, if you are drinking when you are angry, or if the swing from silence to fury is a pattern you have tried and failed to change.

If speaking up at all feels impossible, that is equally worth taking to a counselor; it usually has a history, and it is one of the most workable things in therapy. Both ends of this — too much and none at all — respond well to help, and asking for it is not an admission of anything except that you would like the next ten years to go differently.
