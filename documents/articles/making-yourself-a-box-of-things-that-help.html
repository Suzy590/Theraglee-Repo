Making Yourself a Box of Things That Help

In a bad hour you cannot think of a single thing that helps. So decide while you are calm and put the answers in a box your worst hour can find, built around your senses.

DBTGrounding Visitor 815 words

When you are in the middle of a bad hour, you cannot think of a single thing that helps. That is not a memory problem. A distressed brain stops being able to generate options, which is exactly when you need them most.

So the trick is to decide while you are calm and put the answers somewhere your worst hour can find them. A physical box, a drawer, a bag in the car. Not a plan in your head, which is the thing that goes offline.

Why a physical box beats a list

A list requires you to read, decide and then go and find things, which is three steps too many when you are shaking or numb.

A box requires you to open it and pick something up. That is it. People consistently find that they use the box and do not use the list, even when the list is better written.

It also works as a signal. Getting the box out is itself a small act of looking after yourself, and that act often shifts something before you have used anything inside it.

Build it around your senses

The reason senses are the organizing idea is that they land in the body rather than in thinking, and thinking is the part that is not cooperating.

Touch. Something with a texture you like. A smooth stone, a soft piece of fabric, hand cream, a hot water bottle, a weighted thing to put on your lap.

Smell. The strongest of them, and the one people skip. A particular soap, coffee beans, a bottle of something from a place you liked, mint, eucalyptus.

Taste. Strong and specific rather than comforting. Mints, sour candies, a tea bag of something distinctive, a square of very dark chocolate to eat slowly.

Sound. Headphones and a short list of three or four tracks chosen in advance, because choosing music while distressed rarely goes well.

Sight. Photographs on paper, so the phone stays out of it. Somewhere you felt well. A person or an animal you love.

Then add the practical things

Beyond the senses, a few items earn their place.

A card in your own handwriting with three sentences on it. Not affirmations. Something true and plain that you tend to forget: this has passed before, it will pass this time, do not decide anything tonight.

One phone number written down, so you do not have to scroll.

Something for your hands that takes a bit of attention. A puzzle, a pack of cards, modeling clay, a pencil and paper.

A pair of thick socks, which sounds absurd and is one of the most used items people report.

Make versions that live where you are

One box at home is good. Two or three smaller ones is better, because bad hours do not wait until you are in the right room.

A small bag for your work drawer or your backpack. A tin in the car. Something in your coat pocket, even just the card and a mint.

Keep the home one somewhere you can reach without standing on anything, and tell one person it exists, so somebody can hand it to you.

Test it, then change it

The first version will be wrong in places, and that is the normal course of this.

So use it, and afterward make a note of what you reached for and what you ignored. Take the ignored things out. People are often surprised: the thoughtful items go unused and the socks and the sour candies do the work.

Refill it. A box with an empty wrapper and a dead pen in it is discouraging exactly when you cannot afford to be discouraged.

Keep expectations honest

This does not stop distress. It is not supposed to. What it does is get you through the stretch where you would otherwise do something that makes tomorrow worse.

That is a real and modest job: twenty minutes of being held rather than making a decision, sending a message, or hurting yourself.

So judge it by that. Did I get through the hour without adding to the problem. If yes, the box worked, even if you still felt terrible the whole time.

When to get help

Please talk to a therapist if the bad hours are frequent, if you have urges to hurt yourself, or if you find you cannot get through them without doing something you regret, because that is exactly what therapies like this one were built for and they work. Skills like these come from dialectical behavior therapy, and learning them properly with a professional, rather than from an article, makes a considerable difference. Please see your doctor as well if your mood has been low for weeks or if you are not sleeping. And if there are times you do not want to be here, tell somebody today rather than keeping it to yourself.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
