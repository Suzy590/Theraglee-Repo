Steadying Yourself When Nerves Hit

For the twenty minutes before something that matters — an interview, a scan, a hard conversation. It walks you through settling your body first, then your head, so the nerves have somewhere to go besides your chest.

8 items AnxietyGrounding Free
  • Name it plainlySay “I am nervous” rather than “something is wrong with me.”
  • Slow the out-breathIn for four, out for six, six rounds; the long exhale is the part that settles you.
  • Put both feet flat on the floorPress down and let the ground take your weight for a moment.
  • Find five things you can seeNaming them moves your attention out of your head and into the room.
  • Drop your shoulders, unclench your jawNerves gather there first, and letting go tells your body the danger is not here.
  • Drink some waterA dry mouth and a racing heart both ease a little with a few slow sips.
  • Ask what you are preparing forNerves usually mean something matters to you, not that it will go badly.
  • Plan the first thirty secondsKnow your opening line or first step so you are not deciding it while shaking.

Notes

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
