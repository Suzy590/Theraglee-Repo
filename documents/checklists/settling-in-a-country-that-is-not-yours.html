Settling in a Country That Is Not Yours

For the first year or two in a new country, when everything takes longer and nothing is automatic. It keeps the paperwork in one place, learns the sentences you actually use, finds the food you grew up on, and gives the clumsy stage the time it needs.

9 items Life TransitionsLonelinessResilience Free
  • Keep your documents in one place you can grab fastpaperwork panic is its own kind of exhausting.
  • Learn ten sentences you will actually use this weekthe bakery and the bus matter more than grammar.
  • Find one shop that sells the food you grew up ona familiar taste is not a weakness.
  • Say yes to one invitation a week even when tiredbelonging gets built out of small attendances.
  • Set a fixed hour to call homeopen-ended longing is heavier than a standing date.
  • Ask someone to explain one thing you pretended to follownobody grades you for asking twice.
  • Find out who helps new arrivals near youa library, a church, a community center, a group chat.
  • Give yourself two years rather than two monthsthe clumsy stage is long and it is not failure.
  • Write down one thing that went well each weekearly on, the hard parts are much louder than the wins.
