Telling Your Child Something Hard

For the news you have to give a child — a separation, a death, a move, a diagnosis in the family — and the hour before you say it. It helps you decide what to tell them, keep it in their language, and leave room for whatever comes back.

8 items ParentingCommunication Free
  • Decide the one sentence they need firstchildren hear the first line and little else.
  • Pick a time that is not bedtime or a school morningthey need somewhere to put it afterward.
  • Say it in words they already usea softened version gets misunderstood.
  • Tell them plainly that it is not their faultchildren assume they caused things.
  • Say what will stay the same for themthe practical questions are their real worry.
  • Let the silence sit without filling itprocessing often looks like nothing happening.
  • Answer the question asked, not the whole thingthey will come back for more in their own time.
  • Tell their teacher or another adult tomorrowa child does the rest of their day elsewhere.

Notes
