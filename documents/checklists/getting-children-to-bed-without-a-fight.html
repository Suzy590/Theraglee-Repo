Getting Children to Bed Without a Fight

For the hour before a child's bedtime, when it turns into a negotiation every night. It starts the wind-down earlier, keeps the same order, takes screens out of the last hour, and moves the hard conversation to the morning.

9 items ParentingHabitsBoundaries Free
  • Start the wind-down half an hour before you thinkbedtime runs late mostly because it starts late.
  • Keep the same order every nightthe sequence does the work, not the argument.
  • Give the warning in minutes they can seea timer argues far less well than you do.
  • Take screens out of the last hourthe fight you have at nine usually began at eight.
  • Offer two choices that are both bedtimepajamas first or teeth first is still bed.
  • Answer the fourth request for water once, then stopevery extra answer buys another request.
  • Keep your voice flat when theirs goes upone calm person in the room is the whole technique.
  • Put the hard conversation in the morningnothing gets resolved at bedtime by anybody.
  • Expect two weeks before it settlesa new routine gets worse before it starts working.
