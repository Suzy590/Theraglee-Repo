The Months Before a Baby Arrives

For the months before a baby comes, when advice arrives faster than answers. It gets your questions written down for short appointments, settles who is told what, packs the bag early, and checks what your leave and pay actually are.

9 items ParentingBodyLife Transitions Free
  • Write your questions down as they occur to youappointments are short and memory is worse than you think.
  • Decide together who gets told what, and whenone agreed answer saves repeating yourself all month.
  • Say the frightening thought out loud to one personunspoken fear gets very loud at night.
  • Pack the bag four weeks before you think you need toearly is calm and late is frantic.
  • Work out what the first two weeks at home needmeals, a sleeping plan, who comes and when.
  • Ask what your leave and pay actually are, in writingassumptions here turn out expensive.
  • Keep one thing that is entirely yoursa baby takes the calendar, not the whole person.
  • Tell your midwife or doctor about low mood nowthe weeks before count, not only the weeks after.
  • Let the birth plan be a preference, not a promisethe day rarely reads the document.

Notes

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
