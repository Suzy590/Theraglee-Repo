When Someone's Memory Is Going

Open this when a person you love is forgetting things that matter, and every visit asks something new of you. It offers ways to make the time together easier for both of you, and room for the grief that comes with it.

8 items CaregivingFamilyResilience Free
  • Go with what they said, not the factsCorrecting a wrong year wins nothing and costs the mood.
  • Ask one thing at a timeTwo questions in one sentence is two too many.
  • Bring something to do with your handsFolding or sorting together beats hunting for conversation.
  • Put the same things in the same placesA predictable room does some of the remembering for them.
  • Write what you did on a shared noteA line by the door tells the next visitor where to pick up.
  • Let the same story be new againThey are not repeating it at you, they are telling it to you.
  • Say the goodbye warmly and brieflyA long leaving is harder on both of you than a short one.
  • Grieve the person while they are hereMissing who they were is not disloyal, and it needs somewhere to go.

Notes

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
