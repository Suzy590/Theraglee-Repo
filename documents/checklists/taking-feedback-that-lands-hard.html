Taking Feedback That Lands Hard

Open this after a review, a critique or a note from someone whose opinion matters, when the words keep replaying. It helps you sit with what was said, sort the useful part from the sting, and decide what, if anything, to change.

8 items WorkSelf-EsteemPerfectionism Free
  • Write down what was actually saidGetting the words out of your head and onto paper stops them growing in the retelling.
  • Wait a day before you replyA first reaction is usually about the sting, not the substance.
  • Underline the one line that stung mostNaming the sharpest part shrinks it to a single sentence you can look at.
  • Separate the facts from the toneSomeone can be right about the work and clumsy about how they said it.
  • Ask about the part you do not understandA short question turns a verdict back into a conversation.
  • Name the part you would keepFeedback can be useful in part without being true in whole.
  • Pick one thing to change this weekOne small change is a response; a full overhaul is a punishment.
  • Tell one person you trust about itSaying it out loud to someone kind takes the private weight off.

Notes

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
