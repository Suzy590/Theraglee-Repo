Taking a Compliment Without Deflecting

Open this if praise makes you squirm and you hear yourself brushing it off before the sentence is finished. It gives you something to say instead, and a way to let the good thing land rather than bounce.

8 items Self-EsteemSelf-CompassionRelationships Free
  • Say thank you and stop thereThe whole sentence is two words, and anything after it tends to undo them.
  • Hold off on returning it instantlyTrading compliments is polite, but it closes the moment too fast.
  • Notice the squirm without acting on itThe discomfort passes in seconds if you do not fill it with words.
  • Repeat the compliment to yourself laterHearing it in your own voice is how it gets past the filter.
  • Ask what they noticed, if you want moreA question turns vague praise into something specific you can keep.
  • Write down the ones that surprised youA short list is useful on the days you cannot find anything good.
  • Give one honest compliment this weekBeing on the other side shows you how easy it is to mean it.
  • Let a thank-you be enough for the giverArguing with praise quietly tells someone their judgment is wrong.
