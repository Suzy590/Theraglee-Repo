Asking for Help When You Hate To

Open this when something has got too big to carry alone and you keep deciding to manage anyway. It breaks the ask into small steps, so reaching out to one person feels like a specific request rather than an admission.

8 items BoundariesCommunicationShame Free
  • Name the one thing you needA specific ask is far easier to say and to answer than a general one.
  • Pick the person nearby, not the ideal oneSomeone close who likes you beats the perfect helper you never call.
  • Work out how long it would take themKnowing it is twenty minutes makes the request smaller for both of you.
  • Draft the message before you send itWriting it and sending it are two jobs, and splitting them helps.
  • Say what you have already triedIt shows where the real gap is and tells them where to start.
  • Give them an easy way to say noAn out makes a yes mean more and keeps the friendship clean.
  • Ask early rather than at the edgeHelp arrives better before the deadline than during the crisis.
  • Thank them in a way they will noticeBeing thanked well makes people glad to be asked again.

Notes

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
