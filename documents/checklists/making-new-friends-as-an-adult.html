Making New Friends as an Adult

Open this when your days are full but your weekends are quiet, and the friendships you have are far away or faded. It gives you a few repeatable steps for meeting people and, harder than that, turning a pleasant stranger into someone you actually see.

8 items LonelinessSocial AnxietyRelationships Free
  • Go back to the same place twiceFamiliar faces come from repetition, not from one good night out.
  • Pick something that meets every weekA standing time does the remembering for you.
  • Ask one question past the small talkMoving from the weather to what someone cares about is where a friend starts.
  • Name a day, not just an intentionAn invitation with a date attached is the one that actually happens.
  • Follow up within a weekThe gap after a first meeting is where most new friendships quietly end.
  • Offer something small and low effortA walk or a coffee is easier to say yes to than a whole evening out.
  • Let it be dull at firstEarly friendship is mostly logistics and small talk; depth arrives later.
  • Count the near misses as practiceNot everyone you like will have room for a new friend, and that is not about you.
