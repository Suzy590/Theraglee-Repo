Motivation Challenge

Getting Unstuck, One Tiny Step at a Time

For the task you keep putting off and the guilt that grows around it. Each day asks for one tiny, specific step on something you have been avoiding, so momentum comes back without needing a burst of willpower.

30 daily to-dos. Run it for a week, a month or a year: one to-do a day, and when you reach the end of the list, start again at the top.

MotivationPerfectionism Free

Your days

  1. 1

    Write down the one thing you have been avoiding most. Just the name of it.

  2. 2

    Spend two minutes on that thing, then stop. Two minutes counts.

  3. 3

    Break the thing you are avoiding into five steps, each small enough to do in ten minutes.

  4. 4

    Do the first of those steps today.

  5. 5

    Set a timer for ten minutes and work on the avoided task until it rings.

  6. 6

    Tell someone what you are going to do today, and then do it.

  7. 7

    Do the easiest version of the task: a rough draft, a quick call, an ugly first attempt.

  8. 8

    Clear the space you need to do the task, and lay out what you need for tomorrow.

  9. 9

    Write down what you are afraid will happen if you start. Then start anyway for five minutes.

  10. 10

    Do one thing today at "good enough" and leave it there.

  11. 11

    Pair the task with something you like: a coffee, a playlist, a comfortable spot.

  12. 12

    Make one decision that has been holding the task up, in under a minute.

  13. 13

    Do the next step before you check your phone this morning.

  14. 14

    Ask someone for help with the part you are stuck on.

  15. 15

    Write down what you will do first tomorrow, specifically, and where.

  16. 16

    Work on the task for fifteen minutes and then reward yourself with something small.

  17. 17

    Finish one tiny, unrelated thing today, a dish, an email, a bill, to feel the click of "done."

  18. 18

    Do a step you have been calling boring and notice it was not as bad as the dread.

  19. 19

    Lower the bar: what is the smallest possible version of the next step? Do that.

  20. 20

    Sit with the task open for five minutes, even if you do nothing else. Just be near it.

  21. 21

    Say out loud, "I don't have to feel like it to do it," and do five minutes.

  22. 22

    Cross off anything on your list that no longer matters. Fewer things means less stuck.

  23. 23

    Do the step you are dreading at the time of day when you have the most energy.

  24. 24

    Show someone a piece of the unfinished work, whatever state it is in.

  25. 25

    Write down what finishing the task would let you do or feel.

  26. 26

    Work on the task next to someone else who is working, in person or on a call.

  27. 27

    Do the next step even if the last one was imperfect. Forward beats perfect.

  28. 28

    Spend ten minutes on the task standing up.

  29. 29

    Write down one thing you learned about how you get stuck, and one thing that got you moving.

  30. 30

    Look at everything you have done during this challenge and decide the next single step. Write it on a card for tomorrow.
