Anger Challenge

Meeting Anger Early

For anger that arrives fast and leaves a mess behind. Each day gives you one way to notice it sooner, cool it a little, and say what you mean without the heat, one practice at a time.

30 daily to-dos. Run it for a week, a month or a year: one to-do a day, and when you reach the end of the list, start again at the top.

Your days

  1. 1

    Write down the last three things that made you angry. Look for what they have in common.

  2. 2

    Notice where anger shows up in your body first today: jaw, chest, hands, stomach.

  3. 3

    When you feel a flash of anger, breathe out slowly for a count of six before you say anything.

  4. 4

    Practice the phrase "I need a minute" and use it once today if you need it.

  5. 5

    Write down what you were really feeling underneath your last angry moment. Hurt? Scared? Tired?

  6. 6

    Unclench your hands and jaw on purpose five times today.

  7. 7

    Take a ten-minute walk when something irritates you, before responding.

  8. 8

    Say "I feel frustrated when..." instead of "You always..." once today.

  9. 9

    Rate your anger from zero to ten when you notice it. Respond only when it is under five.

  10. 10

    Splash cold water on your face or hold something cold when your temper rises.

  11. 11

    Write down what you would want to say in the heat of the moment. Then wait an hour and reread it.

  12. 12

    Notice one small annoyance today and let it go without commenting.

  13. 13

    Ask yourself, "What do I need right now?" when you feel angry, and try to give it to yourself.

  14. 14

    Move your body hard for two minutes, stairs, a fast walk, when anger builds.

  15. 15

    Get enough to eat and drink today. Hunger and thirst make everything sharper.

  16. 16

    Write a list of your early warning signs of anger and keep it on your phone.

  17. 17

    Delay one reaction by ten minutes today and see if the same response still feels right.

  18. 18

    Say sorry for something small today, cleanly, without explaining why you did it.

  19. 19

    Notice one thought that fuels your anger, like "they did that on purpose," and write down another possibility.

  20. 20

    Tell someone calmly what you would like them to do differently, in one sentence.

  21. 21

    Take three slow breaths before you open a message or email that might annoy you.

  22. 22

    Spend ten minutes doing something physical and productive, cleaning, chopping, digging, when tension builds.

  23. 23

    Write down what anger has cost you recently and what it has protected.

  24. 24

    Practice lowering your voice on purpose in one tense moment today.

  25. 25

    Ask a question instead of making an accusation once today.

  26. 26

    Leave a conversation that is getting heated and come back to it later, saying that you will.

  27. 27

    Notice what you are tired of today and say it in plain words to someone who can help.

  28. 28

    Do something soothing for your body tonight: a stretch, a warm shower, an early night.

  29. 29

    Write down one thing you handled well today when you could have gotten angry.

  30. 30

    Review your notes from this challenge and pick the one early sign of anger you can now spot. Watch for it.
