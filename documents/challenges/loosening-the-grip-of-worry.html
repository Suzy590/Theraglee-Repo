Worry Challenge

Loosening the Grip of Worry

For a mind that spends its days rehearsing what could go wrong. Each day gives you one way to set worry down for a while, so it takes up less of your day.

30 daily to-dos. Run it for a week, a month or a year: one to-do a day, and when you reach the end of the list, start again at the top.

WorryAnxiety Free

Your days

  1. 1

    Set a fifteen-minute "worry window" this evening. When worries appear earlier, write them down and save them for then.

  2. 2

    Write down one worry and sort it: something you can act on, or something you cannot. Act on the first kind, breathe through the second.

  3. 3

    Notice one "what if" today and answer it with "even if that happened, I would..."

  4. 4

    Take a worry and write down the most likely outcome, not the worst one.

  5. 5

    Ask yourself: "Is this worry solving anything right now?" If not, put it down for an hour.

  6. 6

    Do something with your hands for ten minutes, cooking, gardening, drawing, and let your mind follow.

  7. 7

    Write down a worry from last month. Did it come true? What actually happened?

  8. 8

    When you catch yourself worrying, name what you can see, hear, and feel right now.

  9. 9

    Pick one small step on the thing you are worried about and do it today.

  10. 10

    Limit checking the news to two set times today.

  11. 11

    Tell someone one worry out loud. Notice how it changes when it is said.

  12. 12

    Breathe out for longer than you breathe in, ten times, when a worry spikes.

  13. 13

    Write a worry on paper, fold it, and put it in a drawer until tomorrow.

  14. 14

    Notice a worry about someone else and ask whether there is something kind you could do for them instead.

  15. 15

    Spend ten minutes on something that fully absorbs you, a puzzle, a game, a craft.

  16. 16

    Ask: "What would I tell a friend who was worrying about this?" and take your own advice.

  17. 17

    Rate a worry from zero to ten in the morning and again at night. Notice the difference.

  18. 18

    Replace one "I can't stop thinking about it" with "I am choosing to think about something else for now."

  19. 19

    Make a short list of what is going fine today. Worry tends to leave those off.

  20. 20

    Delay checking for a reply or result by thirty minutes today and see what happens in the gap.

  21. 21

    Write down the thing you are worried about and next to it the date you will actually know the answer.

  22. 22

    Do a quick body check: unclench your jaw, drop your shoulders, loosen your hands.

  23. 23

    Write down what you would do if the worst case happened, in three steps. Having a plan shrinks it.

  24. 24

    Spend fifteen minutes outside and leave the worry indoors.

  25. 25

    Notice a worry that is really about wanting certainty. Say, "I can handle not knowing for now."

  26. 26

    Choose one worry to stop researching online today.

  27. 27

    Say to a worry: "Thanks for trying to protect me. I have got this."

  28. 28

    Make a small decision you have been putting off, in under two minutes.

  29. 29

    Look at your worry notes from this challenge and count how many worries came true. Let the number speak.

  30. 30

    Write down one thing you want to do this week that worry has been talking you out of, and do the first step.
