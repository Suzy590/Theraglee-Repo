Thinking Challenge

Redirecting Unhelpful Thoughts

For thoughts that run in circles, jump to the worst case, or talk you down. Each day offers one practical way to catch an unhelpful thought and steer it somewhere more useful.

30 daily to-dos. Run it for a week, a month or a year: one to-do a day, and when you reach the end of the list, start again at the top.

Your days

  1. 1

    Catch one unhelpful thought today and write it down word for word.

  2. 2

    Take a thought that upset you and ask: what is the evidence for it, and what is the evidence against it?

  3. 3

    Notice one "always" or "never" in your thinking today and swap it for "sometimes."

  4. 4

    When a thought says the worst will happen, write down the most likely outcome next to it.

  5. 5

    Ask yourself what you would say to a friend who had the thought you just had. Say that to yourself.

  6. 6

    Notice one time today you assumed you knew what someone else was thinking. Write down two other possibilities.

  7. 7

    Turn one "I should" into "I could" or "I would like to" and see how it changes the pressure.

  8. 8

    Rate a thought's power from zero to ten now, then again in an hour. Notice whether it moved.

  9. 9

    Catch a thought that blames you for everything and list two other factors that played a part.

  10. 10

    Write an unhelpful thought on a sticky note, then write a fairer version underneath it.

  11. 11

    When you notice a harsh label like "lazy" or "stupid," replace it with a description of what actually happened.

  12. 12

    Ask one thought: "Is this a fact or a feeling?" Sort it into the right pile.

  13. 13

    Take a thought that feels certain and add the words "I am having the thought that" in front of it.

  14. 14

    Spot one prediction you made today and check later whether it came true.

  15. 15

    Write down a thought that ignores the good parts, then add the parts it left out.

  16. 16

    Notice a thought that starts with "what if" and answer it with "then I would."

  17. 17

    Give a repeating thought a silly voice in your head and hear how it sounds.

  18. 18

    Pick one thought that is not helping and ask: "Will this matter in a month?"

  19. 19

    Write down the thought you have most often about yourself and one piece of evidence that does not fit it.

  20. 20

    When you catch yourself mind-reading, ask the person directly instead, if you can.

  21. 21

    Take a thought about the future and list one thing that is within your control today.

  22. 22

    Notice a thought that compares you to someone else and write down what you are comparing against, their highlight reel or their whole life.

  23. 23

    Write down one thought that you have been treating as an order, then ask whether you agree with it.

  24. 24

    Spend two minutes listing three explanations for something that went wrong today, not just the one that blames you.

  25. 25

    Rewrite one all-or-nothing thought on a scale from zero to one hundred.

  26. 26

    Notice a thought that predicts embarrassment and write down what would actually happen if it came true.

  27. 27

    Catch a "here we go again" thought and write down one way today is different from last time.

  28. 28

    Ask a recurring thought: "What would I need to know to let this go?" and go find it, if you can.

  29. 29

    Write a thought that says you cannot cope, then list three hard things you have already gotten through.

  30. 30

    Review your notes from this challenge and circle the thinking pattern that showed up most. Name it so you can spot it faster.
