Worksheet

A Transcript of Your Inner Critic

The voice that tells you you’re not enough is easier to answer once you’ve written down exactly what it says. This worksheet helps you catch it word for word, work out whose voice it borrowed, and write a reply.

12 fields Self-CompassionSelf-Esteem Basic
This is not a test, and there are no right answers. The inner critic is not proof of anything about you — it is a habit of speech, usually learned early, that believes it is keeping you safe. Writing its lines down takes some of the power out of them. You will finish by answering it in your own voice, which may feel strange at first. That is fine.
1

Catch it talking

A recent moment when the critic got loud — a mistake, a message you sent, a mirror, a meeting
Write down what it said, as close to word for word as you can, in the second person: “You always…”, “Nobody…”
Its tone. Is it sneering, tired, panicky, cold? Does it shout or mutter?
2

Whose voice it is

Whose voice does it sound like — a parent, a teacher, a coach, an ex, a version of yourself at a certain age?
The earliest time you can remember hearing something like this said to you or about you
3

What it is trying to prevent

Finish this: “If I stopped listening to the critic, it is afraid that I would…”
What it thinks will happen if it goes quiet — that you’ll get lazy, get hurt, get found out, get left?
Is there a grain of something useful under the cruelty — a real wish to do well, to be liked, to be safe?
4

Your reply

Now write a reply, in your own voice, as you would to a frightened person who is being rude to you. Firm, not cruel.
One sentence from that reply short enough to say under your breath next time it starts
Read the transcript and the reply aloud. Which one sounds more like a grown-up? Which one would you rather listen to?
5

Next time

Next time you hear the critic this week, what will you notice first — the words, the tone, or the feeling in your body?
