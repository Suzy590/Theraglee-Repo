Mapping a Decision You Keep Circling

A decision you keep circling usually has more in it than the two options you argue about. This map lays out what each option costs and gives, hears what fear is voting for, and borrows your own advice.

11 fields Decision Making Basic
There is no right answer waiting at the bottom of this page, and nothing here will decide for you. Going round and round on a choice is not indecisiveness; it usually means something in it matters and you have not yet seen all of it laid out in one place. This map is for getting it out of your head and onto a table, where the costs, the gains and the fears can be looked at one at a time.
1

The choice

The decision, in one plain sentence — what you are choosing between, and by when it has to be made, if it does
How long you've been circling it, and what happens each time you get close to deciding
2

The options laid out

Each option gets a row, including “do nothing for now” — what it costs, what it gives, and who else it touches

What fear is voting for

If fear got a vote, which option would it choose, and what is it afraid will happen otherwise?
What the fear is trying to protect — your safety, your reputation, someone else's feelings, a version of yourself you're attached to
Set fear aside for a moment. Which option would you pick if you knew you could handle whatever followed?
4

Advice from outside

A friend describes exactly this situation to you. What do you say to them — the actual words, not the tactful version
Imagine looking back at this a year from now. What do you suspect you'll wish you had known or trusted today?
5

A next move

One thing you'd need to find out, try or say before you could decide — a single thing, not a research project
The date you'll come back to this page and decide, or decide to wait on purpose
