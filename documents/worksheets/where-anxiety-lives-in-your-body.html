Where Anxiety Lives in Your Body

Anxiety is felt before it is thought. This helps you map where it sits — jaw, chest, stomach, hands — what each part is doing, and what it might be asking for right now.

10 fields Anxiety Basic
Most of us try to think our way out of anxiety, but the body is usually where it shows up first and where it eases first too. This is a slow, curious look at what your body is doing when anxiety is present, one area at a time. It is not a test and there are no right answers. If a part of you is tight or restless, that is information, and often it is asking for something quite simple.
1

Start where you are

How anxious you feel right now (1–10), and where you first noticed it in your body when you read that question
2

Head and face

The jaw, forehead, eyes and scalp — what they are doing right now, and what they tend to do when anxiety is high
If your jaw or forehead could ask for one thing, what would it be — to unclench, to look away, to stop scanning?
3

Chest and breath

The chest, shoulders and breath — tight, fast, shallow, held, heavy — describe what you find without trying to fix it
What the chest and breath seem to be bracing for, and what they might be asking for instead — a longer out-breath, a hand resting there, room
4

Stomach, hands and legs

The stomach, hands and legs — churning, buzzing, cold, restless, weak — and what they do when anxiety spikes
What these parts want to do that you usually stop them doing — run, shake, curl up, move — and whether you could allow any of it for a minute
5

What it is asking for

Reading back over the map, the one area that seems to carry the most, and what it is asking for in one line
Something small you can offer that part today — warmth, stillness, a stretch, permission to rest
How anxious you feel now (1–10), and what you would like to notice in your body the next time it starts to rise
