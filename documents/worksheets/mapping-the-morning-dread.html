Mapping the Morning Dread

For some people the hardest part of the day is the first hour. This maps what happens between waking and leaving the house, where the dread peaks, and which small changes in the routine might ease it.

10 fields AnxietyWorry Basic
Waking with a knot already in your stomach is common, and it is not laziness or a bad attitude. Morning dread usually has a shape — a particular moment, a particular thought — and shapes can be worked with. This is not a test and there are no right answers. You will map an ordinary morning step by step, find where the dread rises and falls, and try one small change to the sequence.
1

Wake up

What you first notice on waking — before you open your eyes, before you check your phone — thought, feeling, and where it sits in the body
What your first thoughts are usually about: the day ahead, something from yesterday, a general sense that something is wrong
2

Map the routine

Your morning from waking to leaving the house (or starting the day), one step per row — alarm, phone, shower, coffee, dressing, the door

3

Where it peaks

The step where the dread is highest, and what is happening in your mind and body at that moment
The step, if any, where it eases even slightly, and what is different about that one
Which parts of the routine feed the dread — checking messages first, rushing, silence, skipping breakfast — and which parts help
4

One small change

One change to the sequence you will try for a week — phone after coffee, curtains open first, the same song, five minutes outside
What you expect to happen to the peak (1–10) if you make that change, and how sure you are
5

Watch the week

After a week, the actual peak on most mornings (1–10), and what surprised you about it
What you want to keep, what you will drop, and one thing you will say to yourself tomorrow morning at the hardest step
