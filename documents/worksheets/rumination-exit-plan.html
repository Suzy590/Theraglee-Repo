Getting Out of the Loop

Going over the same thing again and again feels like problem-solving, but it rarely solves anything. This helps you see what the loop promises, what it delivers, and how to step out of it.

12 fields Thinking TrapsDepressionAnxiety Basic
Rumination is the thought that keeps circling — replaying the argument, rehearsing what you should have said, asking “why am I like this” late at night. It feels like work, which is why it is hard to put down. This is not a test and there is no right answer. You are stepping outside the loop to check what it really gives you. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

The loop

The thought you keep circling, in the words it uses — “why did I say that”, “what if I'd left earlier”, “what's wrong with me”
When it tends to start — in bed, in the shower, on the drive home — and how long a typical round lasts
What it feels like in your body while the loop is running, and afterwards
2

What it promises

What the loop seems to be offering — an answer, a way to undo it, certainty, a guarantee you'll never do it again
Finish this: “If I just think about it hard enough, I'll…”
3

What it delivers

Honestly, what the last hour of looping actually produced — a decision, a plan, or the same three sentences and a headache
What you didn't do while the loop had you — sleep, the call, dinner, the thing you'd been looking forward to
Real problem-solving ends in a next step. What does the loop end in, and how is that different
4

The next ten minutes

Something you can do with your hands or feet for ten minutes — wash up, walk to the corner, water the plants, text one person
A sentence to mark the exit — for example, “I've noticed the loop. I'll give it ten minutes at six if it still wants attention”
If there is a real problem inside the loop, the one concrete step it needs, and when you'll take it
5

Next time

The earliest sign you'll watch for that the loop is starting — the phrase, the posture, the time of night
