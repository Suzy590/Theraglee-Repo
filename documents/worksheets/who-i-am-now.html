Worksheet

Who I Am Now

Loss changes who you get to be. This reflection sorts the roles that ended, the ones that remain and the ones quietly beginning, so you can see yourself clearly without pretending nothing changed.

11 fields LossGrief Basic
When a person, a job or a home is gone, part of your identity goes quiet too — you were a partner, a son, the one on that street. This is not a test of resilience and it will not tell you who to become. It is an honest look at the roles you have, had, and might be growing into. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

Roles that ended

The roles that ended with this loss — partner, caregiver, employee, neighbor, the person who always drove. Name each one
Which of those roles you were proud of, and which you were tired of, even if it feels disloyal to say so
What each ended role used to fill in an ordinary week — hours, purpose, being needed, a reason to get dressed
2

Roles that remain

The roles you still hold — friend, parent, colleague, the one who knows the dog's schedule. Include the small ones
Which remaining role has been carrying you lately, and which has felt like too much
One role you have been neglecting since the loss that you would like to return to, gently
3

Roles beginning

Anything new you have found yourself doing since — handling the bills, cooking for one, being the person others check on
A role you can feel the edges of but cannot name yet. Describe it rather than labeling it
Something about the person you are becoming that the person you lost would recognize, and something they would be surprised by
4

For now

Finish this sentence for today, not forever: “Right now, I am someone who…”
One small thing you could do this week that belongs to who you are now, not who you were
