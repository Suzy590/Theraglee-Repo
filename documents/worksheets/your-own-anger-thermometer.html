Your Own Anger Thermometer

Everyone's anger climbs differently. You build a 1–10 scale in your own words, with a body sign and a concrete move at 3, 6 and 9, so you can act while you can still choose.

11 fields AngerEmotion Regulation Basic
A thermometer is only useful if it is calibrated to you, so this one uses your body, your words and your history rather than anyone else's. There are no right numbers here and no target to hit. The aim is simply to know where you are on the scale early enough that a move is still possible, because by 9 most of us have very few moves left.
1

Calibrate it

What 1 feels like — the you that is genuinely at ease. What is your body doing, what is your voice like?
What 10 looks like, based on the worst it has actually been rather than a guess. What did you do, say or nearly do?
Roughly how long it takes you to climb from 1 to 8 on a bad day — seconds, minutes, or does it build over hours?
2

At 3, the first warning

The body sign that tells you you're at 3 — a slight tightness, a change in your breathing, a sharper tone you can just hear in yourself
The move that fits at 3, when you still have all your options — name it, ask for a minute, slow your speech, check whether you are hungry or tired
3

At 6, the fork

The body sign at 6 — hot face, clenched hands, the urge to interrupt, a feeling that words are speeding up
The move at 6, when you can still leave cleanly — a sentence you can say to buy space, and where you go with that space
4

At 9, the last exit

The body sign at 9 — the point where you know from experience that the next thing out of your mouth will not be one you choose
The one move you can still make at 9, kept deliberately simple: leave the room, close your mouth, put the phone down, walk
5

Put it somewhere

Who else could know your scale, so they can say “you look like a 6” without it being an accusation? What would you want them to say?
Where you'll keep a copy of your 3, 6 and 9 moves so they are there when you need them, and the number you'll check in with tonight

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
