A Recovery Day That Is Not a To-Do List

A day off often turns into chores, errands and catching up, and you end it as tired as you started. This helps you plan a day that actually restores you, and notice afterward what worked.

11 fields Self-CareBurnout Basic
This is not a schedule and there is nothing to complete. A recovery day is not a productive day in disguise, and it is not a reward you have to earn. It is a day whose only job is to leave you with slightly more than you started with. Plan it loosely before, notice how it goes during, and look back gently afterward. If you spend it doing nothing at all, that counts.
1

What you need rest from

What has worn you down lately — the kind of tired it is, and where you feel it
What you usually do with a free day, and how you feel at the end of it. Be honest about the laundry.
The things you are tempted to sneak in “because you have the time” — write them down here so they stay here
2

Shaping the day

What actually restores you, as opposed to what merely distracts you — quiet, movement, people, water, making something, being outdoors
The one or two things you would genuinely like the day to include, held lightly rather than scheduled
What you will say to yourself, or to others, when the pull toward getting things done shows up
The date, and what you will do about your phone, your inbox and anyone likely to need you
3

During the day

Jot down as you go: what you did, whether it left you fuller or emptier, and any guilt that showed up
4

Looking back

How you felt at the end of it compared with the end of a normal free day
What surprised you — what restored you more than expected, and what turned out to be a chore in disguise
The one thing from this day you could give yourself in a smaller dose during an ordinary week, and when
