The Toasts at Your 80th Birthday

Imagining what three people might say about you at your 80th birthday is a quick way to hear what you actually value. You then bring one piece of it back into this month.

11 fields ValuesGoals Basic
This is an exercise in imagination, not a prediction and not a report card. Nobody is grading the life you have had so far, and there are no right answers about who should stand up to speak. Let it be as ordinary or as grand as you like. The useful part comes at the end, when you look at what the speakers said and notice what it implies about the next thirty days rather than the next thirty years.
1

The room

Picture the room at your 80th birthday. Who is there, and where is it — a kitchen, a garden, a hall? As ordinary or grand as you like
Three people who stand up to speak. Name them, or name their place in your life — a child, an old friend, someone you haven't met yet
2

What they say

What the first person says about you. Two or three sentences, written in their voice rather than yours
What the second person says about you — a different side of you than the first speaker saw, if you can
What the third person says. Let this be the person who knows you least well, or the one whose words you'd most want to hear
3

Read it back

Read the three toasts back. What do they have in common — what kind of person do they describe?
What none of them mention. Notice what is missing — the job title, the tidy house, the things you worry about most
Which sentence would be hardest to earn from where you are right now, and what makes it hard
4

This month

One thing you could do in the next thirty days that a speaker might mention — small counts, like calling your brother back or showing up to the thing
When you'll do it, and who might notice that you did
A line from one of the toasts to keep somewhere you'll see it — a mirror, a wallet, the lock screen
