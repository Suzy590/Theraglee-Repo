The Rule That Got Broken

Behind most anger is a rule about how things should go. You find the rule, check whether the other person ever knew it, and decide if it is one you want to keep, say out loud, or loosen.

11 fields AngerRelationships Basic
Anger usually means a line was crossed. The trouble is that many of our lines are invisible, even to us, until someone steps over one. This is not a test of whether your rules are reasonable, and it is not about whether you were entitled to be angry. It is a look at the rulebook you carry, so that fewer of its pages are secret from the people you live and work with.
1

The flash

The thing that made you angry, described plainly and without the adjectives you'd use to a friend
Finish this: “People should…” or “They should never…” — the rule that was broken, in one sentence
2

The rule itself

Where the rule came from — a parent, a hard experience, a household you grew up in, a job that punished mistakes
How strictly you hold yourself to it, honestly. Are you ever the one who breaks it?
3

Did they know?

Had you ever said this rule out loud to the person, in plain words? Or was it something they were meant to just know?
What rule do you suspect they were following instead? Most people are keeping to a rule of their own, not breaking yours on purpose
If they had known your rule, what do you think they would actually have done differently, if anything?

What to do with it

For the rule that got broken: keep it and say it out loud, keep it just for yourself, or loosen it? What would loosening it look like?
If you're keeping it, the sentence you'll use to tell the person — a request, not a verdict. Write it exactly as you'd say it
