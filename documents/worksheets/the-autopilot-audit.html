The Autopilot Audit

A look at the stretches of an ordinary day you don't remember living — the commute, the shower, the scroll. You map where you were absent and pick one stretch to be present for.

10 fields Mindfulness Basic
Most of a day happens without us. We arrive at work and cannot recall the drive; we finish a meal and do not remember eating it. That is not a flaw — autopilot is how the mind saves effort. But it is worth seeing where it runs, because those stretches add up to a life. This is an audit, not a judgment. There is nothing to fix. You are simply finding out where you actually are during your own day.
1

Yesterday, in stretches

Walk back through yesterday in six or seven chunks — waking, commute, morning work, lunch, afternoon, evening, bed. Be honest about what is blank
Stretch of the dayWhat I was doingWhat I actually rememberPresent or autopilot?
2

Where autopilot runs

The stretches you couldn't remember at all, and roughly how many hours of the day they add up to
What autopilot was doing for you in those stretches — resting, hiding from boredom, getting through something unpleasant
The stretch where you were most present yesterday, and what made that one different from the rest
3

What you missed

Something that probably happened in a blank stretch that you would have liked to notice — a conversation, the weather, a taste
A person you were with during autopilot, and what they might have noticed about you
4

One stretch, on purpose

The single stretch you'll choose to be present for tomorrow — the shower, the first coffee, the walk from the car. Keep it short
What being present in that stretch will actually look like — no phone, one sense to follow, a question to hold
Afterward: what you noticed in that stretch that you would normally have slept through
Whether you want to keep that one stretch for the rest of the week, or try a different one next
