From Goal to First Step

A goal stays a wish until it has a done-when definition and a first action with a date. This breaks one goal into sitting-sized next actions and gets the first one done this week.

10 fields GoalsMotivation Basic
This is not a productivity system and there is no score for how much you get done. A goal that has been sitting untouched is not evidence of laziness; usually it is just too big to pick up in one hand. The work here is to make it smaller and more specific until there is a first action so clear that the only thing left is to do it. Pick one goal, not five.
1

The goal

The goal, as concrete as you can make it — not “get healthier”, but “walk to work three days a week by November”
Why this one, and why now. What would you have then that you don't have today?
Done when: the plain test that tells you it's finished — what you'd see, hold, or be able to do that you can't now
2

Break it down

The next actions, each one small enough to finish in a single sitting — “find three routes”, not “sort out the walking thing”
Next actionDone whenNeeds (time, money, help)Target date
The action you're least sure how to do. Who or what could show you, and how would you ask?
3

What gets in the way

The most likely reason this stalls — the excuse you have used before, said honestly
If that happens, what you'll do instead of quietly dropping the whole thing
4

This week

The very first action from the table, and the day and time you'll do it this week
Who you'll tell that you did it, and what you'll say to them
Afterwards: what happened when you did the first step, and what the next one is now
