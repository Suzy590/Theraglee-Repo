Adjusting to a New Family Member

A new baby, a stepchild, a foster placement, or a parent moving in changes everything at once. This sheet checks in on sleep, identity, partnership, and support so the strain has somewhere to go.

13 fields FamilyParentingRelationships Basic
There are no right answers here and no verdict on how you are adjusting. Loving someone new and being flattened by their arrival happen at the same time, and feeling the second does not cancel the first. Most families do not talk about how hard the first months are until they are over. This sheet is somewhere to say it now, before it turns into something you carry alone.
1

Who arrived

Who has joined your family, when, and what the arrival was like — planned, sudden, longed for, complicated
What you imagined this stage would feel like, and how the real thing compares
2

Sleep and body

How much sleep you are actually getting, in hours, and how it is affecting your patience, your mood, and your memory
One thing that could give you more rest this week — a swapped night, a nap, a limit on visitors — and what stops you asking for it
3

Who you are now

What has fallen away from your life since they arrived — work, friends, your body, time alone, a version of yourself
What you miss most, and whether you have said so out loud to anyone
Something that is still yours — a habit, a friendship, a place — and how you will keep it this month
4

Your partnership

How you and your partner, if you have one, are talking to each other now — as a team, as shift workers, as strangers passing
One thing you appreciate that they have done, and one thing you need from them that you have not asked for
The sentence you will use to ask for it, and when you will say it
5

Support and next week

Who has actually shown up, and who you expected to show up and hasn't. What has that felt like?
One specific ask you will make of one person this week — a meal, an hour, a ride, a phone call at a bad time
One thing you will notice next week about how you are doing, so you know whether it is getting lighter or heavier

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
