Planning a Hard Conversation

Before a conversation you have been dreading, get clear on what you want the other person to know, what you want to happen, and how you will open — so nerves don't decide for you.

12 fields CommunicationRelationships Basic
Some conversations get rehearsed in the shower for weeks and never happen. This is not a test of courage and there is no right way to do it. It is a place to think slowly before you talk, so that when the moment comes you are not improvising from fear. You can write the whole thing out and still decide the timing isn't right. That is allowed.
1

The conversation

Who it's with, and what it's about — in one plain sentence, the way you'd tell a friend
How long you've been putting it off, and what has happened each time you nearly started it
What you are afraid will happen if you say it, and what you're afraid will happen if you never do
2

What they should know

The one thing you most want them to understand by the end. Not everything — the one thing.
What you want them to know about how you feel, in plain words — hurt, worried, tired, unsure
Anything you want to own from your side, said the way you'd want to hear it
3

What you want to happen

The outcome you are hoping for — a change, an apology, an answer, or simply being heard
What would count as a good enough result, even if it's not the whole thing you hoped for
4

Your opening

Your first sentence, word for word. Short, warm, and about you — “I've been wanting to talk about something that's been on my mind.”
When and where — a time with no rush and no audience — and how you'll ask whether it's a good moment
5

If it goes sideways

The signs it's going wrong — raised voices, silence, old arguments creeping in — and what you'll say to pause it
What you will do afterwards no matter how it went — a walk, a call to someone kind, a note about what you learned

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
