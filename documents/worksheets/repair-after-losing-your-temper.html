Repairing It After You Lost Your Temper

Every parent shouts, snaps, or slams a door sometimes. What matters most is what happens next. This sheet helps you look at what happened, work out what your child saw, and plan a repair that is honest and short.

12 fields ParentingStress Basic
This is not a test of whether you are a good parent, and there are no right answers. Losing it is information about how much you were carrying, not a verdict on you. Children do not need parents who never get it wrong. They need parents who come back afterward. Take your time here, and be as honest about what you needed as you are about what you did.
1

What happened

The moment itself, described plainly — what your child did, what you did, what you said. No softening, and no piling on either.
What the hour before looked like — how tired, rushed, hungry, or already stretched you were when it started
The exact point it tipped. What was the last small thing before you lost it?
2

What your child saw

Picture it from their height and their age. What did they see on your face and hear in your voice?
What they might have made it mean — about themselves, about you, or about whether they are safe with you
How they have been with you since — clingy, quiet, angry, or acting like nothing happened
3

What you needed

Underneath the anger, what were you actually needing in that moment — quiet, help, to be listened to, five minutes alone?
Who or what could have met that need earlier, and what got in the way of asking for it
4

The repair

What you will say, in your child's language. Short, no excuses, no lecture — for example, “I shouted. That was not your fault. I'm sorry.”
What you will not say — the “but you made me” or “if you had just listened” you can feel wanting to come out
When and where you will say it — at bedtime, in the car, on the floor next to them
Afterward: how it went, what they said or did, and one thing you will do differently the next time you feel it tipping

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
