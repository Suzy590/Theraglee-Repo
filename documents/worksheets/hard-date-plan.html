Planning for a Hard Date

Anniversaries, birthdays and other marked days can loom for weeks. This helps you plan the next one — before, during and after — so the day is shaped by you rather than dreaded.

11 fields GriefLoss Basic
A hard date does not have to be survived by accident. Some people want to mark it; some want to be somewhere else entirely; many want both by the afternoon. None of those is the right way. This is a plan you can change, and the point is to walk into the day with a few decisions already made. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

The date

The date that is coming, what it marks, and how far away it is
What you notice as it gets closer — sleep, irritability, an urge to fill the calendar, or a numbness you cannot name
2

What happened last time

If you have been through this date before, what the day was actually like — not what you feared, what happened
What helped last time, and what you would not repeat
3

The days before

What you will tell the people around you, and by when — “the 14th is my mother's anniversary, I may be quiet”
Anything to arrange ahead — time off, a place booked, a person on standby, food in the fridge, the phone on silent
4

The day itself

How you would like to mark it, if at all — a visit, a candle, their favorite meal, a walk, doing nothing on purpose
One person you could be with for part of the day, and one hour you might prefer to be alone
A backup plan if the morning is worse than expected — something small, kind and already decided
5

The day after

How you will let yourself land on the next day — a slower start, a check-in call, no big decisions
A note to your future self to read the morning of the date, written now

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
