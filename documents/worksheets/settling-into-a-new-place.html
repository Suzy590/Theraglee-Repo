Settling Into a New Place

Moving house or city uproots the small routines that made life feel like yours. This helps you notice what the move took, rebuild the anchors one at a time, and keep a record of the place becoming home.

11 fields Life TransitionsStress Basic
A move is counted as one event, but it is dozens of small losses at once: the route you walked without thinking, the barista who knew your order, the friend across town. Feeling unmoored months later is not failing at the move. This is not a checklist to complete, and there are no right answers. It is a way to notice what is missing and start putting anchors down, one at a time.
1

What the move took

The small, ordinary things you lost in the move — the walk, the store, the neighbor, the light in the old kitchen
The person or people you miss most, and what specifically you did with them that you have no replacement for yet
What you were hoping the move would fix or bring, and how that hope is holding up so far
2

How it feels now

The moment in the week when the new place feels least like home — Sunday afternoon, the commute, walking into an empty apartment
What you have been doing with that feeling — calling home constantly, not unpacking, saying yes to everything, saying no to everything
3

New anchors

For each routine or place you lost, the closest new version you could try and when — it doesn't have to be as good, just a start
Old anchorWhat it gave meNew version to tryWhen I'll try itHow it went
4

The people part

Where people who'd be your kind of people might already be gathering — a class, a run club, a volunteer shift, a place of worship, the dog park
The one line you'll use to turn a nod into a conversation, e.g. “I just moved here — is this place always this busy?”
Who from the old place you'll keep on a regular call, and how often, so those friendships don't depend on you remembering
5

A place that's yours

One spot in the new place — a bench, a cafe, a corner of the apartment — you'll claim as yours this week, and when you'll go
A month from now: what feels more like home than it did today, and what still doesn't
