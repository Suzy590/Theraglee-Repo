The Comparison Detox

Comparing yourself to other people is automatic, and it almost always uses a picture with half the details missing. This worksheet helps you name who you compare yourself to, on what, and what you never get to see.

11 fields Self-EsteemPerfectionism Basic
There are no right answers here, and comparing is not a flaw — every human mind does it. The trouble is that it compares your whole life, including the parts only you can see, against the edited version of someone else’s. This is a chance to lay the comparisons out in daylight, fill in what is missing from the picture, and decide what you would rather do with that attention.
1

Who you measure against

The people you find yourself measuring against most often — a sibling, a colleague, a friend, someone online you have never met
Where you usually see them: in person, on a screen, in your memory, in what other people tell you about them
2

Laying it out

One row per comparison. Be specific about what you compare, and honest about what you cannot see.

What’s missing from the picture

Pick the comparison that stings most. What do you actually know about that person’s week — sleep, money, health, worries, what they gave up?
What you are comparing against them — your unfiltered inside, or the version of you that other people see?
Something in your life that someone else might be comparing themselves to right now, without you knowing
4

What the comparing costs

What the comparing gives you — a push to try harder, a reason to stay small, a way to feel bad that at least feels familiar?
What it costs you in an ordinary week: time, mood, the ability to enjoy what you have
5

A week with less of it

One feed, habit, or situation you will reduce or step away from for the next seven days
What you’ll do with the attention you get back — something you want to get better at for its own sake, not to catch up with anyone
