Five Ways to Say No

Most of us have a clear no inside and a padded one that comes out. This script sheet lets you write both for five real situations, then look at the gap between them and what it costs you.

10 fields AssertivenessCommunication Basic
There is no right way to say no, and a soft no is not a failure. Sometimes the padded version is the kind one, and sometimes it is the one that leaves you doing the thing anyway. This sheet is a place to write both versions side by side, without judging either, so you can see how far apart they are and decide which one you'd like to move toward.
1

Five situations

Five requests you find hard to refuse — a favor, extra work, a visit, a loan, a plan you don't want to join

Looking at the gap

What the softer no has cost you before — the thing you ended up doing, the resentment, the time
3

Where the padding came from

Who in your life said no plainly, and how it landed on you as a child or younger adult
Who taught you that a bare no was rude, dangerous, or selfish, and how they taught it
4

A middle version

Pick one situation and write a no that is honest but still kind — shorter than your padded one, warmer than your blunt one
Say it out loud three times. What changed in your voice or body by the third?
5

Try one

The one situation you'll answer with the middle version next time it comes up, and roughly when that might be
Afterward: what you said, how they responded, and whether the thing you feared actually happened
