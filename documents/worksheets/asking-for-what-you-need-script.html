Asking for What You Need Without Losing Yourself

Asking for something clearly, while keeping the relationship and your self-respect intact, is a skill rather than a personality trait. You'll build a script for one real request, one line at a time.

13 fields RelationshipsDBT Basic
This is a script, not a test, and you're allowed to change every word of it. Most of us either ask too softly and get nothing, or ask too hard and get a fight. There is a middle way: describe what's happening, say how you feel, ask for what you want, and give the other person a reason to say yes — all while staying calm, looking like you mean it, and being willing to meet partway. Write it here first so you're not improvising in the moment.
1

The request

Who you're asking, what you're asking for, and what usually happens when you try to bring this up
What matters most here: getting the yes, keeping the relationship warm, or keeping your self-respect? Rank all three honestly
2

The script

Describe: the situation in plain facts the other person couldn't argue with — “The last three weekends I've done the school run alone”
Express: how it makes you feel, using “I” and a feeling word, without blame — “I feel worn out and a bit invisible”
Assert: the ask itself, in one clear sentence. Not a hint, not a question that's really a complaint
Reinforce: what's in it for them, or what saying yes would mean for both of you — the reason it's worth their while
3

How you'll carry yourself

Stay mindful: the thing they might say that would knock you off track, and the sentence you'll return to when it does
Appear confident: what your voice, posture, and eyes will do, even if inside you're shaking
Negotiate: what you'd accept instead of the full ask, and the line you won't go below
4

Keeping yourself

How you'll stay gentle with them even if they say no — an easy tone, curiosity about their side, no old scores
How you'll stay fair to yourself — no over-apologizing, no pretending it doesn't matter, no giving in just to end the discomfort
5

The conversation

When and where you'll have it, and what you'll do for the ten minutes before to settle yourself
Afterwards: what they said, what you managed, and what you'd keep or change the next time you ask for something
