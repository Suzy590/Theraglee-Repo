Asking for Help This Month

Grief makes ordinary life heavier and asking for help harder. This turns what you need this month into a short, practical list, matched to people who could give it, with the words to ask.

11 fields GriefRelationships Basic
People around a loss often say “let me know if you need anything” and mean it, then wait for instructions that never come. This is not a test of how independent you are. It is a list — rides, meals, paperwork, someone to sit with you — and a way to hand it to the people who have already offered. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

What this month needs

The practical things that have piled up or gone undone since the loss — bills, forms, the yard, groceries, school pickups, the dog
The things you are managing but at a cost — the ones that leave you wrung out or in tears in the car
The non-practical help you need too — company on a Sunday, someone to say their name, a person who will let you not talk
2

Who could help

Match each need to a person. One row per need; a person can appear more than once, and “nobody yet” is an honest answer
What I needWho could do itHow I'll askBy when
People who have said “anything you need” and whom you have not taken up on it. What has stopped you
Anyone outside your circle who could carry a piece — a neighbor, a coworker, a service, a faith community, a grief group
3

The asking

What makes asking hard for you right now — not wanting to be a burden, not knowing what to say, pride, exhaustion
Write the actual words for one ask, as a text or a sentence — “Could you take the kids Thursday after school? I have the lawyer at four”
How you will let someone help imperfectly — the wrong brand, a slightly late ride — without taking the task back
4

The first ask

The one request you will make in the next three days, who you will ask, and when
What you will notice about how it felt to be helped, so you can decide whether to ask again

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
