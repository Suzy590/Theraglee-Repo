Your Stress Early-Warning Signals

Stress rarely arrives all at once; it sends signals first. This helps you name the ones your body, mood and behavior send, and draw a personal yellow line and red line so you can act sooner.

12 fields StressSelf-Care Basic
This is not a test, and there is no score at the end. Most of us only notice stress once it has already taken over the week. The idea here is simpler: get to know the small, early signs that are yours in particular, so that next time you can spot them a little sooner. There are no right answers. Your signals may look nothing like anyone else’s, and that is exactly the point.
1

Your body

The first places stress shows up physically — a tight jaw, shallow breathing, a knot in your stomach, headaches, waking at 4 a.m.
What changes in how you eat, sleep or move when the pressure is building, even slightly
2

Your mood

The feelings that creep in first — irritability, flatness, a short fuse, tearfulness, a dread you cannot pin on anything
What you start thinking about yourself or other people when you are getting stretched — “nobody helps”, “I can’t keep up”
3

Your behavior

What you do more of under stress — snapping, scrolling, skipping meals, saying yes to everything, working late
What you do less of — calling friends, cooking, exercise, the things you usually enjoy
4

What others notice

What a partner, friend or coworker would say they see in you before you admit anything is wrong
A time someone noticed before you did. What did they say, and how did you respond?
5

Your yellow and red lines

Your yellow line: the two or three signals that mean “pay attention now” — still manageable, but time to ease off something
Your red line: the signals that mean “stop and get help with this” — the point past which pushing on has never worked for you
What you will do the next time you cross yellow — one concrete move, like canceling one thing or telling one person
Who you will tell when you reach red, and the words you would use

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
