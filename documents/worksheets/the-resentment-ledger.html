The Resentment Ledger

Resentment is often a need that was never asked for out loud. This helps you write down what you've been keeping score of, and what it would sound like to ask for it directly.

9 fields RelationshipsCommunication Basic
Keeping score is exhausting and very human. This is not a test of your generosity and there is no verdict here. Resentment is information: usually a need you have been meeting silently, expecting the other person to notice. Writing the ledger out is not about presenting a bill. It is about finding the ask that has been hiding inside each entry and deciding what to do with it.
1

Who and where

Who this resentment is with — a partner, a parent, a roommate, a colleague, a friend — and how long it's been building
How it shows up — sharpness, silence, doing things with a sigh, mentally rehearsing speeches
2

The ledger

One entry per row. Be specific: not “they never help”, but “I've done every school pickup since March”
What I'm keeping score ofWhat I do about it nowWhat I actually needHow the ask would sound
3

Reading it back

Which entries they could reasonably know about, and which you have never actually said out loud
Which single entry, if it were resolved, would ease most of the rest
Anything on the list that is really about someone else, or about an older story, rather than this person
4

Making one ask

The one ask you'll make first, written the way you'll say it — plain, about your need, without the tally attached
What you'll do if the answer is no or not now, so that it doesn't just go back on the ledger
5

After the ask

When you'll make the ask, and what you'd like to notice in yourself in the days after
