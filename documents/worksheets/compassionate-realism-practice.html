Honest and Kind at the Same Time

Between the harsh thought and the falsely cheerful one there is a third voice, honest and kind. This helps you find it, sentence by sentence, for the things you're hard on yourself about.

10 fields CBTDepression Basic
When you are low, the harsh voice says “you ruined it.” The cheerful voice, trying to help, says “it was fine, don't worry about it” — and you don't believe a word. Neither is telling the truth. This is not a test and there are no right answers. You are practicing a third voice, one that admits what actually happened and still speaks to you the way you would speak to someone you love.
1

The situation

Something recent you've been hard on yourself about — a conversation, a mistake, a thing you didn't do
2

Three voices

The harsh thought, in the exact words it uses. Let it be as unkind as it really is
The falsely cheerful thought — the thing a well-meaning friend or a poster might say, that you don't quite buy
What is actually true here — the parts of the harsh voice that are fair, and the parts that are not
Now the honest and kind version. Admit what happened, then say what you'd say to a close friend in the same spot
3

Practice on more

Try the same three voices on five more things your mind has been hard on you about lately
The harsh thoughtThe cheerful thoughtThe honest, kind thought
4

How it lands

Read the honest, kind sentences aloud. What is different in your body compared with the harsh ones
What makes the kind voice hard to believe — does it feel like letting yourself off, or like a language you don't speak yet
5

Carry it

One honest, kind sentence you'll keep for the week, for the moment the harsh voice starts up
Someone whose voice already sounds like this, and what they'd say to you tonight
