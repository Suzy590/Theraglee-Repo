The Morning After a Big Feeling

After a night of tears, a blow-up, or a panic that ran its course, the next day has its own weather. This helps you tend to yourself, decide what to repair, and let the rest go.

12 fields Emotion RegulationRelationships Basic
There is no verdict here about last night. Big feelings leave a residue — a tiredness, a wince, a wish to hide — and it's tempting to spend the whole next day replaying it. This is a different way to spend the morning: notice what you need, sort what actually needs mending from what only shame says needs mending, and put the rest down. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

Taking stock

What happened, in a few plain sentences — the feeling, how big it got, roughly how long it lasted
How you feel this morning, in your body and your mood — heavy, raw, embarrassed, flat, relieved, all of the above
The thoughts that are circling. Write them down so they're on the page instead of on a loop
2

What you need

What your body needs today — water, a proper meal, a nap, a walk, fewer plans
What your mind needs — quiet, company, a distraction, someone to say it's okay
One thing you can do for yourself in the next hour, before you do anything about anyone else
3

What you'll repair

Anything you said or did that actually hurt someone or crossed a line you care about. Only real harm, not everything you feel bad about
The repair, sized to fit — a short apology, a text, a conversation later this week — and what you'll say in it
What you won't do in the name of repair: over-explaining, groveling, promising it'll never happen again
4

What you'll leave

The parts that shame wants you to keep chewing on but that don't need fixing — being seen crying, needing help, having feelings at all
A sentence that closes it — something like “That was a hard night, I got through it, and today is a different day” in your own words
Something from last night worth keeping — a thing you learned about what you need, or a person who showed up

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
