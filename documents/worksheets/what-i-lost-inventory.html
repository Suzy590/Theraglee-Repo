Everything That Went With It

A loss is rarely one thing. This helps you name the person or thing you lost, and then the routines, roles, futures and small daily details that quietly disappeared alongside it.

10 fields GriefLoss Basic
When something is gone, people grieve the obvious part and feel ambushed by the rest — the empty side of the sofa, the errand you no longer run. This is not a test and there are no right answers. It is a place to see the whole shape of what you lost, so none of it has to be grieved in secret. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

The center of it

Who or what you lost, in your own words — a name, a job title, a house, a friendship, a version of your health
When it ended, or when you understood it had. Some losses have a date; some arrive slowly
2

Routines that went too

Daily or weekly things you did with or because of them — the Sunday call, the walk, the shared commute, cooking for two
Which of those routines you have stopped, and which ones you still half-do out of habit
3

Roles and futures

Who you were to them, or in that place — partner, caregiver, colleague, the one who knew the way. Which of those roles has nowhere to go now
Plans and futures that quietly ended too — the trip, the retirement, the child's first day, the conversation you were saving
4

The small daily details

The details nobody else would think to grieve, and how each one lands when you bump into it
The detailWhen I notice itHow much it aches (1–10)What I do with it
5

Something to keep

Reading back over all of this, what surprises you about how much was tied to this one loss
One small part of what you lost that you could deliberately keep — a routine, a recipe, a phrase, a place you still go
One person you could show this page to, or one line from it you could say out loud this week
