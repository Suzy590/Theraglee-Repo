When the Anger Is at You

Anger pointed inward — at your own mistakes, your body, your past — rarely fixes anything, but it feels like discipline. You separate what you're angry about from the voice doing the shouting, and find a fairer verdict.

12 fields AngerEmotion Regulation Basic
This is for the anger you turn on yourself: the harshness after a mistake, the contempt for a habit, the fury at your own body or history. It is not a test of whether you deserve it. Self-directed anger often started as an attempt to keep you safe or in line, and it can be looked at like any other anger — what set it off, what it wants, what it costs. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

What set it off

The thing you are angry at yourself about right now, named plainly — the missed deadline, the thing said at dinner, the money, the years
What you actually did or didn't do, described as a neutral witness would, without the adjectives
2

The voice

What the anger says to you, word for word, including the names it calls you. Write it down exactly, however ugly
Whose voice does it sound like? Who spoke to you in that tone first, and when?
What the anger says it is trying to achieve — make you better, stop you doing it again, punish you before someone else does
3

What it costs

What happens to you after a round of it — do you fix the thing, or do you go flat, hide, avoid, or do the thing again?
How long you have been angry at yourself about this one, and whether the anger has changed anything in that time
4

A fairer verdict

What you would say to a friend who had done exactly the same thing and told you about it in the same words
What was actually true at the time — what you knew, how tired or scared you were, what you were trying to do. Not excuses, context
The fair sentence: what you did, what it cost, and what you're responsible for. No more, no less
5

Repair instead of punishment

If there is a real repair to make — an apology, a payment, a doctor's appointment — what is it, and when?
One sentence to say to yourself the next time the voice starts, written in the tone you'd use with someone you actually wanted to help

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
