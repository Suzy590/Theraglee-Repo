If Only: Guilt After a Loss

Grief often brings a loop of if-onlys. This helps you write each one down, next to what you actually knew at the time, and hear what a kind witness would say about it.

10 fields GriefSelf-Compassion Basic
If-only thoughts are grief trying to find a version of events where the loss could have been prevented — and where you had more power than you did. They are painful, common, and not proof of anything. This is not a trial, and it does not decide whether you are guilty. It puts each thought next to what you actually knew then. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.

The if-onlys

One row per if-only. Fill the middle columns with what you knew and could do at the time, not what you know now

Look at the pattern

What most of the if-onlys have in common — a single moment, a decision, a thing you said or did not say
How much of what you now know was actually available to you at the time. Be precise about hindsight
Whether any of these thoughts are doing a job — keeping you connected, keeping you busy, keeping you from feeling something else
4

A kind witness

Picture someone who saw everything you did and did not do, and who is on your side. What they say about the whole picture, not one moment
What you would say to a close friend who brought you the same if-only about their own loss
5

Carrying it differently

One if-only you might be able to set down, even a little, and what you would put in its place — a fact, a memory, a sentence
A sentence to say to yourself the next time the loop starts, written now
