A Boundary With One Difficult Person

Some people need a boundary written down before you can hold it in the room. This sheet helps you set one with a specific person: what you'll allow, what you won't, and what you'll do if it's crossed.

12 fields BoundariesRelationships Basic
This is not a plan to change the other person, and nobody is grading how much you allow. A boundary with a difficult person works best when it is specific: this topic, this tone, this hour, this amount. Write it as plainly as you can, including what you will do when it is crossed, because it probably will be. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

The person

Who this is, and the relationship — parent, ex, boss, sibling, neighbor, friend
The specific thing they do that you've had enough of — not “they're difficult”, but the comment, the drop-in, the request, the tone
What it costs you afterward — hours, sleep, a bad mood you take home, money
2

What you'll allow

What you're genuinely fine with from this person — the contact, topics, or help you're willing to keep giving
The conditions under which you'll allow it — daytime only, in public, with someone else there, once a month
3

What you won't

The line, in one sentence you could say out loud — “I won't discuss my marriage with you”, “I'm not lending money again”
How you'll tell them, if you're going to — the words, the setting, and whether it's said once or repeated each time
4

If it's crossed

The first thing you'll do the moment they cross it — end the call, change the subject, leave the room, say the sentence again
What you'll do if they cross it a second time in the same conversation, and a third
What you'll do with your own reaction afterward — who you'll call, where you'll go, how you'll get the adrenaline out
5

Keep it steady

Someone who knows this boundary exists and can remind you of it when you wobble
The first time you expect to need this, and the one sentence you'll have ready

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
