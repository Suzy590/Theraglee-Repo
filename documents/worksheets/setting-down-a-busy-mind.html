Setting Down a Busy Mind at Night

An end-of-day sheet for a mind that won't stop. You write out what you're carrying, decide what can wait until tomorrow, and give the rest a note so it doesn't have to be held all night.

12 fields StressMindfulnessSelf-Care Basic
A busy mind at bedtime is not a broken mind; it is a mind that has been handed too much to hold, with nowhere to put it down. This sheet gives it somewhere. You are not solving anything tonight, and there are no right answers. You are sorting what you are carrying into what can rest, what needs a note, and the small amount that truly needs you before morning — which is usually nothing.
1

What you're carrying

Everything on your mind right now, in whatever order it comes — tasks, worries, conversations, things you forgot, things you can't change. Don't tidy it
The one that keeps coming back most, the one your mind returns to when the others go quiet
What your body is doing while you hold all this — jaw, shoulders, stomach, breath
2

Sort it

The things on your list that cannot be done tonight no matter how much you think about them
The things that only need a note — a name, a time, a reminder — so tomorrow's you finds them. Write the notes here
Anything that genuinely needs doing in the next hour. Be strict — most nights this box is empty
The things that are not tasks at all, just feelings wearing a task's clothes — and what the feeling underneath is
3

Put it down

A sentence to say to the loudest worry, like “I've written you down. I'll come back to you at nine tomorrow.”
What is actually true about this moment — the bed, the dark, the quiet, the fact that nothing is required of you right now
One thing from today that went fine, or better than fine, that your busy mind skipped straight over
4

For tomorrow morning

Where you'll look for tonight's notes in the morning, and the time you'll actually deal with the first one
What you'll notice about how you slept, and whether writing it all down made any difference
