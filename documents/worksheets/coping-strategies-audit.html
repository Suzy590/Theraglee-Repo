Your Coping Strategies, Audited

Everything you reach for under stress does something for you, even the things you wish you did not do. This lays them side by side — what each gives, what it costs — and helps you choose one swap.

10 fields StressSelf-Care Basic
This is not a list of good habits and bad habits, and there is no score. Every coping strategy you have was learned because it worked at least once. The wine, the scrolling, the overworking, the long run, the phone call to your sister — each one gives you something. The only questions here are what it gives, what it costs, and whether a cheaper way to get the same thing might exist. There are no right answers.

Reading the audit

What the “gives” column has in common — relief, numbness, control, company, a break from thinking. Name the need underneath.
The costs you had been quietly paying without adding them up — sleep, money, mornings, trust, self-respect
The strategies that give a lot and cost little. Why do you not reach for those first?
4

One swap

One strategy you would like to swap, and what it is really giving you that the replacement will have to give as well
The replacement — something that meets the same need, is available at the same moment, and that you would actually do
How you will remember the swap in the moment — a note on the fridge, a word on your phone, something moved out of reach
Afterward: the first time you tried the swap, what happened, and whether the need underneath was met
