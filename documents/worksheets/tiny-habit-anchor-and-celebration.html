Design a Tiny Habit

Big habits fail because they ask too much on a bad day. This helps you shrink one habit until it is almost too small to skip, attach it to something you already do, and track a first week.

12 fields Habits Basic
This is not a test of willpower, and there is no right answer about which habit you pick. A tiny habit is one that takes under a minute and can survive a day when you are tired, late, or fed up. The point of the first week is not to be perfect. It is to find out where the habit fits, how it feels, and what gets in its way, so you can adjust it rather than abandon it.
1

The habit you want

The habit you would like to have, in plain words — “floss”, “stretch in the morning”, “write a few lines”
Why it matters to you right now, not why it should matter to anyone
Every version of this you have tried before, and roughly how long each one lasted
2

Shrink it

The smallest version that still counts — one tooth, one stretch, one sentence. Make it take under a minute
If that still feels like too much on a rough day, shrink it again. Write the even smaller version here
3

Anchor and celebration

Something you already do every day without deciding to — pouring coffee, sitting down at your desk, brushing your teeth
Your recipe, in one line: “After I [anchor], I will [tiny behavior]”
How you will mark it as done the moment it happens — a quiet “nice”, a fist pump, a tick on this sheet. Pick something that feels good, not silly
4

Seven days

Tick each day you did the tiny version. A note on what made it easy or hard is more useful than a perfect row
DayDid it? (yes / no)What made it easy or hardAnchor worked? (yes / no)
5

Look back

Which days it happened easily, and what those days had in common
If the anchor was wrong, a different daily moment that might fit better
The version you will carry into next week — same tiny size, or one notch bigger if it felt easy
