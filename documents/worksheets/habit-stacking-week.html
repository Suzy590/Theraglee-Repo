A Habit Stacking Week

You already have routines that happen without effort. This helps you attach one or two new habits to those routines, run the pairing for a week, and keep the stacks that fit.

11 fields Habits Basic
You do not need new discipline to add a habit; you need a slot. Your day already has fixed points — the kettle, the commute, the moment you sit down after work — and each one can carry a small extra behavior. This is not a test, and a stack that does not fit is not a failure. It is a pairing that needs a different slot. Aim small, keep the ones that stick, and let the rest go.
1

Routines you already have

Things you do every day without deciding to — coffee, unlocking the door, feeding the cat, plugging in your phone. List as many as you can
The two or three that happen at the same time and place almost every day
2

Pair them up

The new habit you want to add, shrunk to under two minutes
The routine it will follow, written as a sentence: “After I [routine], I will [habit]”
A second stack if you want one — keep it separate from the first so they do not compete
3

The week

Each day, note whether the stack happened, and what got in the way if it did not

What you learned

The days the routine itself did not happen. If it is not daily, it is not a good anchor
Whether the habit felt natural after its routine, or like an interruption. Describe the moment of switching
A better slot for anything that felt forced
5

Keep or move

The stack you will keep exactly as it is, and the one you will move or drop
The next routine you could stack onto once this one feels automatic — not yet, just noted
