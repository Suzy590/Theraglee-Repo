Rewriting Your Stress Story

Under pressure most of us repeat one sentence to ourselves — “I can’t cope”, “I have to do it all”. This helps you catch yours, check it against the evidence, and write a truer one to carry instead.

12 fields ResilienceStress Basic
This is not an exercise in correcting your thinking, and there is no right sentence at the end. The line you say to yourself under pressure was probably learned early and has probably kept you going more than once. It is not stupid and it is not the enemy. But it may be out of date, and it may be making the pressure heavier than it needs to be. The aim is a sentence that is truer, not just nicer.
1

Catch the sentence

A recent moment when the pressure spiked — the email, the school call, the deadline moving, the third thing going wrong before nine
The sentence that ran through your head, word for word if you can — “I can’t do this”, “it’s all on me”, “if I stop, everything falls apart”
How your body responded to that sentence, and what you did in the next ten minutes
2

Where it came from

Who else in your life says or said a sentence like this — a parent, a boss, a coach — and in what tone of voice
A time when the sentence was true, or nearly true. What was happening then, and how old were you?
3

What it costs and protects

What the sentence does for you under pressure — keeps you moving, stops you asking for help, spares you from disappointing anyone
What it costs you — sleep, the ability to stop, the chance to be helped, the way you speak to yourself at midnight
4

A truer sentence

The evidence the sentence leaves out — the times you did cope, the help that exists, the things that did not fall apart when you paused
A truer sentence. Not a slogan, and not the opposite of the old one — something you could say out loud without rolling your eyes.
Say both sentences slowly. What happens in your shoulders, your breathing and your urge to rush with each one?
5

Try it under pressure

Where you will keep the new sentence so it is there when the pressure spikes — a lock screen, a card in your wallet, a note on the monitor
The next time the old sentence shows up, what happened when you answered it with the new one — and what you would change about the wording
