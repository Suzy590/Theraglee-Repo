Sort Your Worries and Book a Worry Time

Worry spreads across the whole day unless it is given a place. This log helps you write each worry down, sort it into something you can act on or not, and save the rest for a set worry time.

10 fields WorryAnxietyCBT Basic
Worry is not a sign that something is wrong with you. It is the mind trying to get ahead of trouble, and it works better with a container. This is not a test and there are no right answers. Over the week you will note worries as they arrive, decide whether each one has an action attached, and keep the rest for a short daily worry time you choose. Nothing here needs to be tidy or complete.
1

Choose a worry time

The time of day you will give to worry — 15 to 20 minutes, not too close to bed, and the same time each day if you can
Where you will sit for it, and what you will do the moment it ends (make tea, walk to the mailbox, put music on)
2

Log worries as they arrive

One row for each worry as it arrives during the week. If there is a next action, name it; if there is not, save the worry for worry time
WhenThe worryAct on it? (yes/no)Next action, if yesSaved for worry time?
3

At worry time

Which saved worries still felt urgent when you sat down with them, and which had gone quiet by themselves
For the worries you cannot act on, what they seem to want from you — certainty, a promise it won't happen, someone to say it will be fine
One sentence you can say to a worry that has no action attached, in your own words — not a slogan, something you would actually say
4

Look back at the week

What you noticed about the split between worries with an action and worries without one — was it even, or leaning one way?
How the worry time itself felt by day five or six compared with day one
5

Next week

One worry from the “act on it” column that you will take the next action on, and the day you will do it
What you would like to notice next week when a worry shows up outside worry time
