Stay or Go: A Job Decision Map

A job decision rarely gets clearer by thinking harder in circles. This lays the two paths side by side — what you'd gain, lose, and fear on each — and asks what an ordinary day looks like a year on.

13 fields WorkDecision Making Basic
This will not tell you what to do, and there is no right answer waiting at the bottom of the page. Most job decisions get stuck because gain, loss and fear are all talking at once. Putting them in separate columns turns the noise into something you can look at. Take your time; you can come back to it more than once before you decide, and changing your mind halfway through is allowed.
1

The two paths

Path A — staying where you are, or the current situation — described in two or three plain sentences
Path B — the change: the new job, the leave, the move into something else — in two or three plain sentences
Compare the two paths across the things that matter to you — money, hours, people, learning, health, pride, commute — one area per row
AreaIf I stayIf I goHow much it matters (1–10)
2

The fear underneath

The fear that belongs to leaving, said plainly, e.g. “I'll fail somewhere new and won't be able to come back”
The fear that belongs to staying, said just as plainly, e.g. “I'll still be here in five years, wondering”
Which of those two fears you have been avoiding looking at, and why it is the louder one
3

A year from now

It is a year later and you stayed. Describe an ordinary Tuesday — the morning, the work, the evening, how you feel at the end of it
It is a year later and you went. The same ordinary Tuesday, in the same detail
Reading both Tuesdays back, which one made your shoulders drop, and which one made your stomach tighten
4

What you'd need to know

The information that would actually change the decision — and how you could get it this month: a conversation, a trial day, a real number
Who you'll talk to before deciding, and what you'll ask them that isn't “what should I do”
5

Next step

The smallest next move on either path — an application, a conversation with your manager, a look at the numbers
The date by which you'll decide, and what you'll do if that date comes and you still don't know
