Getting Ready for a Big Change

Any large transition is easier to walk into when you know what will stay the same, what you're leaving, what you're taking with you, and who is coming along. This maps all four, whatever the change is.

13 fields Life TransitionsDecision Making Basic
This works for any change big enough to make you nervous — a new baby, a separation, a career shift, a graduation, a change in your health. It is not a test of readiness, and there is no score at the end that says go or don't go. Big change feels as if everything is moving at once. Sorting it into what stays, what goes, what comes with you and who comes along makes it a size you can hold.
1

The change and what stays

The change, in a sentence, and roughly when it happens
How you feel about it today — it is fine if several feelings sit side by side, e.g. excited and dreading it
The parts of your life this change does not touch — the people, the habits, the places, the parts of you
Which of those you'll deliberately protect through the change, and how
2

What you're leaving

What ends with this change — name it plainly, even if you chose it
What you'll miss, and what you're relieved to put down
Something you'd like to do to mark the ending — a last visit, a note, a meal, a photograph
3

What you're carrying with you

What you learned from the last big change you went through — the thing that helped, and the thing you'd do differently
The strengths, habits and people who come with you no matter where the change lands you
Something you're carrying that you'd rather set down before you go — an old story about yourself, a grudge, a role you've outgrown
4

Who's coming along

The people who will be there on the other side, and what each can actually offer — a couch, a listening ear, a spare key, a laugh
One of them you'll tell this week that the change is coming and how you feel about it, and what you'll ask them for
5

The first day after

The first morning after the change: one small thing from your “stays the same” list that you'll do, so the day begins with something familiar

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
