Worksheet

What Was Normal in Your House

Every family has rules that were never said out loud. This sheet helps you name what counted as normal where you grew up, then choose deliberately what you are keeping and what ends with you.

12 fields FamilyRelationships Basic
There are no right answers here and no verdict on your family or on you. Every household has a way of doing things that felt like simply how life is, until you met people who did it differently. Some of what you learned is worth keeping. Some of it you may decide ends with you. Both are allowed, and you can change your mind. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

What was normal

How anger was handled in your house — shouting, silence, slammed doors, jokes, leaving the room, or something else
How affection and praise were shown, or not shown, and who in the house got them
What happened when someone was sick, sad, or struggling. Who noticed, and what were they told?
The things nobody talked about — money, a relative, a drinking habit, a death, a feeling
What you had to do or be to stay in good standing — be quiet, be funny, be the achiever, be the easy one
2

Seeing it from outside

The first time you noticed another family did it differently. What did you see, and what did you feel?
A pattern from your house that you have caught yourself repeating with a partner, a child, or a friend
3

Keeping and ending

What you are keeping — the traditions, phrases, and ways of caring that you want to carry on, and why they matter
What you are ending — the pattern, rule, or silence that stops with you. Name it as plainly as you can.
What ending it will cost you, and what it might cost the people who still live by it
4

The first small break

One moment next week where the old pattern would usually show up, and what you will do instead
Who you could tell about this — a sentence you could say to a partner, sibling, or friend

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
