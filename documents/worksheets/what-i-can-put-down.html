Worksheet

What I Can Put Down

Some of what is weighing on you is not really yours to carry, and some of it can be made smaller. This sorts your obligations into keep, shrink, delegate and drop, and gets you started on one of them.

10 fields BurnoutWorkSelf-Care Basic
This is an inventory, not a test of how much you can handle. When everything feels heavy, it is hard to see that the load is made of separate pieces, and that not all of them have to stay exactly as they are. Nothing you write here commits you to anything. You are just laying the pieces out on the table and looking at them one at a time. There are no right answers.
1

Everything you are carrying

The obligations on your plate right now, big and small — the job, the school run, the aging parent, the committee, the friend who always needs you
Which of these you chose, which were handed to you, and which you simply drifted into
2

Sorting the load

Take each obligation in turn. Be honest in the second column — “everyone” is rarely the real answer.
ObligationWho it really servesKeep, shrink, delegate, dropWhat makes it hard
3

Looking at the sort

The items you marked “keep”. What makes them worth keeping — do they matter to you, or would you just feel guilty without them?
The items you could shrink. What would a smaller version look like — fewer hours, a lower standard, once a month instead of every week?
The items someone else could carry, and what stops you from asking — pride, guilt, a belief that they would do it wrong
The items you could drop entirely. What is the worst that would actually happen, and who would honestly mind?
4

First thing to put down

The one item you will shrink, delegate or drop first — pick the easiest, not the most important
The exact words you will use to hand it over, scale it back or decline it, and who will hear them
When you will do it, and what you will do with the ten minutes or ten hours it gives back
