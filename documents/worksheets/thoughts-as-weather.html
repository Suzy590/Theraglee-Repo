Thoughts as Weather

A sheet for spotting the mind's habits — planning, replaying, judging, comparing — and naming them as passing weather rather than orders to follow. You practice noticing a thought without climbing aboard it.

11 fields MindfulnessStress Basic
Thoughts arrive the way weather does: you do not choose them, and you cannot stop them, but you also do not have to walk out into every storm. This sheet is not about clearing your mind, and there are no wrong thoughts to have. It is about learning to name the kind of thought that has shown up — planning, replaying, judging, comparing — and letting it pass while you stay where you are.
1

Today's forecast

A thought that is on top right now, or was earlier today, written down as it actually sounded in your head
Which family it belongs to — planning, replaying, judging, comparing, worrying, or something else you can name
How long it has been circling, and roughly how many times today it has come back around
2

The usual patterns

The kind of weather your mind makes most — replaying old conversations, planning three steps ahead, comparing yourself, judging what you just did
The time of day or situation when that pattern rolls in most reliably — in bed, in the shower, scrolling, after a meeting
What usually happens when you follow it — where it takes you and how you feel an hour later
3

Naming without following

Try saying it silently: “Planning is happening” or “Here is the comparing again.” Write what shifted, even slightly, when you named it
Where the thought sits in your body when you don't chase it — throat, chest, forehead, restless legs
What is actually here, in the room, right now — three things you can see, hear, or feel against your skin
4

For the next few days

The one pattern you'll practice naming this week, and the short phrase you'll use for it
Something you'll notice about the weather next time it comes — how long it lasts, whether it passes quicker once named
