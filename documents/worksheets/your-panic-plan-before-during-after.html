Your Panic Plan for Before, During and After

Panic is frightening and fast, which is why a plan written while you are calm helps. This guides you through what to prepare, what to do when it peaks, and how to recover afterward without adding shame.

11 fields PanicAnxiety Basic
A panic episode feels like an emergency, and the body behaves as if it is one, even when nothing dangerous is happening. Writing a plan now, in a quieter moment, means the calm part of you gets a say later. This is not a test and there is nothing to get right; the plan can be short and it can change. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

Before it starts

The early signs that panic may be on its way — a flutter in the chest, a change in breathing, a sudden need to leave
Places, times, or situations where it has tended to show up, and any that you have started to avoid because of it
What you want to remember about panic itself — for example, that it peaks and passes, usually within minutes, and has never actually harmed you
2

While it peaks

One thing you will do with your breath or your feet — slow the out-breath, press your heels into the floor, name five things you can see
A sentence you will say to yourself, written the way you actually talk, not the way a poster would
What you will not do, if you can manage it — for example, not leaving the room, not checking your pulse, not calling for reassurance
3

As it passes

How you will know it is easing, and what you will let your body do next — shake, sit, drink water, keep going
4

In the hours after

What you tend to tell yourself after an episode, and a kinder, more accurate version of the same sentence
Something ordinary you will do within the hour so the day does not become about the panic
A person who could know about this plan, and the one thing you would want them to do or not do
5

Keep it close

Where you will keep a short copy of this — phone notes, wallet, bedside — and the three lines from it you would want to read first

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
