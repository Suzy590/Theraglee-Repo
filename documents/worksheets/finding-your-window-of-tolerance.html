Finding Your Window of Tolerance

Helps you learn your own signs of being too revved up or too shut down, what a steady middle feels like for you, and what brings you back in each direction.

11 fields Emotion RegulationTraumaGrounding Basic
Everyone has a range where they can think, feel, and choose at the same time. Above it, you're revved — racing, braced, on guard. Below it, you're shut down — foggy, far away, flat. Neither is a failing; both are your body trying to protect you. This sheet helps you learn your own signs and your own way back to the middle. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

Too revved up

What too much looks like for you — racing thoughts, snapping, can't sit still, heart pounding, scanning the room
The earliest sign, the one that shows up before anyone else would notice
2

Too shut down

What shutdown looks like for you — foggy, far away, heavy, can't answer, can't feel much of anything
The earliest sign of that — the one you could catch if you were looking for it
3

Inside the window

A recent moment when you felt steady enough to think, feel, and choose. Where were you and what was happening?
How your body feels when you're in the window — breathing, shoulders, jaw, how the room sounds
4

The way back

What settles you when you're revved — slower things: a long exhale, cold water, walking, a weighted blanket, a quiet room
What wakes you when you're shut down — brighter things: standing up, music with a beat, a strong taste, talking to someone, stepping outside
Something that helps in one direction but makes the other worse — for example coffee, lying down, being alone
5

Widening the window

What a person who cares about you could do or say that helps, and what they should not do
One thing to notice this week — the earliest sign in either direction — and what you'll try the moment you see it
