Replaying an Argument in Slow Motion

After a fight, go back through it one line at a time — what was said, what you meant, what you heard, and what you actually needed — so the next one can go differently.

12 fields CommunicationRelationships Basic
Arguments move fast, and most of what matters gets lost in the speed. This is not a way to work out who was right, and there are no correct answers here. It is a slow replay, done alone and afterwards, to notice the gap between what was said and what was meant. Be as honest about your side as you would like them to be about theirs.
1

The argument

Who it was with, when, and what it was supposedly about — the surface topic
How it started. The first sentence or look that tipped it from a conversation into a fight
How it ended — someone left, someone went quiet, someone gave in, it fizzled out
2

What was said

The line of yours that landed worst, as close to word for word as you can remember
The line of theirs that landed worst for you, as close to word for word as you can remember
3

What was meant and heard

What you actually meant by your line — the worry or hurt underneath the words
What you heard in their line — the message you took from it, which may not be what they meant
What they might have meant instead, if you give them the same benefit of the doubt you'd want
4

What you needed

What you needed in that moment — to be taken seriously, to be left alone, to feel like you were on the same side
What you think they needed, going by what they did rather than what they said
5

Next time

One sentence you could have said at the tipping point that named the need instead of the complaint
Something you'd like to tell them about this argument now that it has cooled, if anything
