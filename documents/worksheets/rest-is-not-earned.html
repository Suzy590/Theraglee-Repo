Rest Is Not Earned

If you only rest once everything is done, you never rest, because everything is never done. This sheet looks at where that rule came from and helps you take one rest before it's earned.

13 fields Self-CareSelf-Esteem Basic
This is for people who sit down only when the last dish is washed and the last email answered, and who feel a small guilt even then. There is nothing wrong with you for doing this. Somewhere you learned that rest has to be paid for. This sheet is not a lecture about self-care; it is a look at that rule, what it costs, and one unearned rest this week.
1

The rule

Finish this sentence honestly: “I'm allowed to rest when…”
What actually happens when you try to rest before that — the guilt, the list running, getting up again
How often the condition is actually met. When was the last time everything was done?
2

Where it came from

Who you watched rest, and who you watched never rest, growing up
What rest was called in your house — lazy, idle, deserved, a reward, a weakness
What you're afraid would happen, or what people would think, if you rested without earning it
3

What it costs

What your body and mood are like by the time you finally do stop
Something you've missed, cut short, or resented because rest had to wait
4

Checking the rule

Someone you know who rests without earning it. Do you think less of them?
What you'd say to a friend who told you they were only allowed to sit down once everything was finished
5

One unearned rest

One rest you'll take this week before the list is done — when, for how long, and what you'll do
What you'll say to the guilt when it shows up, in one sentence
Afterwards: what actually happened, and whether the undone things were still there and still fine
