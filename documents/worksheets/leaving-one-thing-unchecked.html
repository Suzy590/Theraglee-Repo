Leaving One Thing Unchecked

Not knowing is often more uncomfortable than bad news. This experiment helps you deliberately leave one thing unchecked, unplanned or unresolved, predict what will happen, and then see what actually did.

11 fields AnxietyWorryCBT Basic
A lot of anxiety is not about danger but about not knowing, and the usual answer — check again, plan every step, settle it tonight — brings relief that does not last. This is an experiment, not a test. You choose one small thing to leave open on purpose, write down what you expect, sit with the discomfort for a while, and then compare. Whatever happens, the result is useful information, not a grade.
1

Pick the experiment

The one thing you will leave unchecked, unplanned, or unresolved this week — an email you won't reread, a trip you won't map out, a question you won't settle tonight
Why this one: small enough to try, uncomfortable enough to count
How long you will leave it — an hour, a day, until Friday — and how you will stop yourself quietly checking anyway
2

Make a prediction

What your anxiety says will happen if you leave it, in its own words, and how sure it is (0–100%)
How uncomfortable you expect to feel at the start (1–10), and how long you expect the discomfort to last
3

Sit with it

What you noticed in the first ten minutes — urges, thoughts, where it sat in your body
What you did with the urge each time it came back, and what happened to it when you did not act
How uncomfortable it actually got at its peak (1–10), and roughly when it began to ease
4

Compare the two

What actually happened to the thing you left open, set beside what anxiety predicted
What you learned about not knowing that you could only have learned by doing it, not by thinking about it
5

Next time

A slightly larger thing you could leave open next, and when you will try it
