Keeping Friendships Alive

Friendships drift when nobody means them to. This helps you name who you'd like to be closer to, what has kept you from reaching out, and one small, specific message for each of them.

9 fields RelationshipsLoneliness Basic
Adult friendships rarely end; they thin out. This is not a test of how good a friend you have been and there are no right answers. It is a plan for tending a few relationships you'd like to keep, in steps small enough to actually happen on a busy week. Reaching out after a long silence is almost always more welcome than it feels.
1

The drift

The friendships that have gone quieter than you'd like, and roughly how long since you last properly talked
What has gotten in the way — time, distance, awkwardness about the gap, waiting for them to go first
2

Who you'd like closer

One row per person. Make the reach-out small enough to send today — a photo, a question, a “thought of you”
NameWhat I miss about themThe reach-out, word for wordBy when
3

Past the awkwardness

What you're afraid a message might land as — needy, out of the blue, too late — and what you'd think if a friend sent you the same
A sentence that names the gap lightly if you need one: “It's been ages and that's on me — how are you?”
4

Making it regular

One small habit that would keep this going — a monthly call, a standing walk, a message on the commute
How you'll remember to do it — a calendar reminder, a note on the fridge, one friend-day each week
5

This week

The first message you'll send, to whom, and when. Then come back and write what happened
What you'd like to notice about yourself the next time a friend goes quiet on you
