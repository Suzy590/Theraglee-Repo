New Place, New People

Just moved or started a new job? Building a life from scratch takes deliberate steps. This helps you plan where you'll go, who you'll say hello to, and how you'll get through the awkward first weeks.

11 fields LonelinessSocial AnxietyRelationships Basic
Arriving somewhere new means losing your background hum of familiar faces all at once, and it is normal to feel it for months. This is not a test of how outgoing you are and there is no right pace. It is a practical plan for putting yourself in the same rooms as the same people often enough that strangers turn into faces, and faces into names.
1

Where you are

What changed — the move, the job, the ending — and how many weeks in you are
What you left behind that you miss most — not just people, but the ease of knowing where things are and being known
Who you still have, even at a distance, and how often you are actually in touch
2

The same rooms, repeatedly

Three places you could go every week where the same people would also be — a class, a run club, a coffee shop, a volunteer shift
The one you'll start with, and the day and time you'll go for the first four weeks
3

The first hello

What you'll say to one new person the first time — something small and about the place, not about you
What you expect to feel just before you say it, and how you'll get yourself through those ten seconds
4

When it feels pointless

What you'll tell yourself in week three, when you've shown up and nothing much has happened yet
Small comforts that make a new place feel like yours — a route you like, a shop where they know your order, a corner set up your way
5

Looking back

Three months from now — one thing you'd like to be true about your week here that isn't yet
Someone from your old life you'll tell how it's going, and when you'll do that
