Your Digital Availability Plan

Being reachable at all hours is a choice most of us never made on purpose. This plan helps you decide when you're available, on which channels, and who gets through when you're not.

13 fields BoundariesWork Basic
This is not about going offline or being hard to reach, and there are no right hours. It is about choosing your availability rather than defaulting to always. Most of us answer work messages in bed and family messages in meetings because nobody ever asked us where the edges were. This sheet asks. You'll set hours, sort channels, and decide who can reach you outside those hours, then try it for a week and see what breaks.
1

How it is now

The first thing you check when you wake and the last thing before sleep, and roughly how many minutes each
Which channels ping you — work email, work chat, texts, group chats, social apps, calls — and which one you resent most
The last time a message outside working hours changed your evening, and what it was
2

Your hours

The hours you'll be reachable for work on a weekday — a start and an end, for example 8:30 to 6
The hours you'll be reachable for friends and family, if different, and what weekends look like
One stretch each day that is nobody's — no phone at all — even if it's twenty minutes
3

Your channels

For each channel, what you'll do with it outside your hours — muted, off, notifications silenced, phone in another room. Be specific about the settings
Which channel you'll turn off entirely, or delete, and what you'll lose by doing so
4

Who gets through

The short list of people who can reach you at any hour, and how they'll do it — a starred contact, a particular ringtone, a call not a text
What you'll tell everyone else, in one sentence — an email signature, a status, or just something you say when asked
5

Try it for a week

The day you'll start, and the two or three settings you'll change that morning
What you expect to be hardest — the urge to check, someone's reaction, feeling out of the loop
At the end of the week: what actually happened when a message waited, and what you'd adjust
