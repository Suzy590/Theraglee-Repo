Working With Intrusive Thoughts

A thought that arrives uninvited is not a plan, a wish, or a confession. This worksheet helps you notice one, step back from it, and decide what you do next.

12 fields CBTThinking Traps Premium
Intrusive thoughts are common — most people have them. They tend to run opposite to what you actually want, which is exactly why they alarm you. The aim here is not to work out what a thought means, or to argue it away. It is to let it be there without letting it steer. If a thought involves hurting yourself or someone else, or you cannot put it down, please talk to someone — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

Notice it

What you were doing when it arrived (time of day, where you were, who was around)
The thought, in a few words — a short phrase is enough, you do not need to write it out in full
How hard it landed, 0–100, and where you felt it in your body
2

Step back from it

Write it again starting with “I'm having the thought that…” — then read both versions and notice the difference
Name the shape of it without arguing with it (a doubt, a what-if, a worst-case image, an urge to check)
What it would cost you to leave this one unanswered
3

What you did next

What you felt pulled to do — check, replay it, avoid something, ask someone whether it's true
What that gave you in the moment, and what it cost an hour later
What you could try instead the next time it shows up
4

Come back to what matters

What you were heading toward before the thought interrupted
One small thing you'll do now, whether or not the thought is still there
5

Worth telling someone

If this keeps happening: who you'd tell, and the one sentence you'd open with

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
