Your Own Crisis Kit for Riding Out a Hard Hour

When a feeling is too big to solve right now, the job is to get through the next hour without making it worse. You'll build a kit of things that distract, soothe, and steady you, then test it.

10 fields DBT Basic
Some moments are not for fixing. They are for getting through. This kit is not a cure and there is no right answer for what belongs in it — only what actually works for you, which you'll find out by trying things. Build it on a calm day so it's ready on a hard one. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

Know your hard hour

What a crisis hour looks like for you — the feeling, the urge, and the thing you're most likely to do that you'd regret later
2

Distract and self-soothe

Things that pull your attention somewhere else for a while — a show, a task with your hands, counting backwards, a walk to a specific place
One person you could contact just to talk about something ordinary, and what you'd say to open
Something for each sense that settles you: what to look at, listen to, smell, taste, and touch. Be specific — the gray blanket, not “a blanket”
3

Improve the moment

A way to change the room, your body, or the thought — cold water on your face, a different chair, one sentence you'll repeat, a prayer, a breath count
A picture in your mind you can go to — a real place, a remembered day, a made-up one — described with enough detail to actually get there
4

Pros and cons

Acting on the urge: what it gives you in the moment, and what it costs you tomorrow
Riding it out instead: what it costs you in the moment, and what it gives you tomorrow
5

What you tested

Each time you use something from the kit, note what it was and how it went
