Small Joys, Savored

A seven-entry log for the small good moments that usually slide past — the first sip, the sun on the back of your neck — and how long you managed to stay with each one before moving on.

9 fields MindfulnessSelf-Care Basic
Good moments happen more often than we register, and pass faster than they need to. Savoring is the simple practice of staying with a pleasant thing a few seconds longer than usual — not manufacturing happiness, just not rushing past what is already there. This is not a happiness score and there is nothing to achieve. Small is the point: a warm mug, a song, a patch of sun, a dog on the street.
1

Setting out

The kinds of small good things that already happen in your ordinary day, even if you barely register them — coffee, a joke at work, the walk home
What usually cuts them short — reaching for the phone, the next task, a habit of thinking “yes, but”
2

The log

Seven small joys, one line each. Under “How long I stayed”, be honest — three seconds is a fine answer. Under “What I noticed”, use your senses
DayThe momentHow long I stayedWhat I noticed
3

What the log shows

The kind of moment that appeared most often — taste, warmth, a person, something outside, something funny
The longest you stayed with any of them, and what let you stay that long
The moment you cut short fastest, and what pulled you away from it
Any change across the week in how quickly you spotted a good moment while it was still happening
4

Staying a little longer

One small joy you can count on tomorrow, and the extra few seconds you'll give it — a second breath before the first sip, eyes closed for the chorus
A short phrase to remind yourself to stay — “this, now”, “one more second”, or your own
