The Good Enough Experiment

Perfectionism promises that extra effort will keep you safe, but rarely gets tested. This worksheet helps you do one ordinary task at about 80 percent, predict the fallout, and then record what actually happened.

12 fields Perfectionism Basic
This is an experiment, not an exam. Nobody is going to grade the result, including you. The idea is simple: pick one low-stakes task, do it deliberately less than perfectly, and find out what really follows. Some people discover that no one notices. Some discover that they feel worse than expected, which is also useful information. Either way, you learn something you cannot learn by thinking about it.
1

Pick the task

The task you’ll do at about 80 percent — an email, a meal, a report, tidying a room. Pick something with low stakes.
What 100 percent would look like for this task, honestly: the rereads, the extra checks, the time it usually eats
What 80 percent will look like instead. Be concrete: one draft and send, twenty minutes and stop, no second coat.
2

Your prediction

Before you start, what you expect will go wrong, and how bad you think it will feel (1–10)
Who you imagine noticing the difference, and what you imagine them thinking
3

Doing it at 80 percent

Day and time you’ll do it. Write it down like an appointment.
While you were doing it: the moment you most wanted to go back and fix, polish, or check. What did you do instead?
4

What actually happened

Prediction versus result. Fill in the left column before, the right two after.
What I fearedPredicted (1–10)Actual (1–10)What really happened
Did anyone notice? If so, what did they actually say — not what you read into their face
How long the discomfort lasted, and what it was like an hour later
5

Next round

What the extra 20 percent usually buys you, now that you have seen what happens without it
The next task you’ll try at 80 percent, and one thing you’ll do differently this time
