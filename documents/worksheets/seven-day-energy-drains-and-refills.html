Seven Days of Energy Drains and Refills

A one-week log of what takes your energy and what gives it back. By day seven, you will see patterns you could not see from inside a single tiring day, and you can pick one refill to protect.

10 fields StressSelf-Care Basic
Tiredness is not a personal failing and it is not laziness; it is usually a ledger that has quietly gone into the red. This log is not about doing more or judging how you spend your time. For seven days, jot down what drained you and what put something back, however small. Then look at the week as a whole. There are no right answers, only your own pattern coming into view.
1

Before you start

How you would describe your energy right now, in a sentence — and what you think is draining it most
Your guess at what refills you. Write it down now so you can compare it with what the week actually shows.
2

The seven-day log

One line a day, at bedtime if you can. Small things count on both sides — a rude email drains, a good coffee refills.

Looking at the week

The drains that showed up more than once. Name them plainly — a person, a task, a time of day, a habit.
The refills that showed up, even tiny ones — a walk, a joke, ten minutes alone in the car
Which drains surprised you, and which refills you had been dismissing as not counting
How your guess from before the week compared with what you actually recorded
4

One thing to protect

One refill you will make room for next week, and the day and time it goes in
One drain you can shrink by a small amount — leave a meeting early, batch the emails, say no to one request
What you will notice at bedtime next week to tell whether the change made a difference
