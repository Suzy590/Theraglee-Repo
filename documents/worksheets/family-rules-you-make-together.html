Family Rules You Make Together

Rules that are handed down get argued with; rules that are built together get kept, mostly. This sheet plans a short family meeting about screens and the other things you fight over, and records what you agree.

11 fields ParentingFamily Basic
This is not a test, and there is no correct set of rules. Every family is different in what it can live with. The point of building rules together is not to give children the final say. It is to let them be heard, so the rules make sense to them and to you. Expect it to be imperfect. A rule that gets kept most of the time is doing its job.
1

Before the meeting

What you are fighting about right now — screens at the table, bedtime, homework, chores. Name the top two or three.
The rule you would impose if you could, and honestly, why that has not worked so far
What you think each child would say they want, if asked. Guess now, then check later.
Your own screen habits, honestly, and what your children see you doing with your phone
2

The meeting

When and where you will hold it, how long it will be, and what you will do to make it feel like a conversation rather than a lecture
The questions you will ask, in your children's words — for example, “What feels unfair right now?” or “What would you change?”
The things that are not up for discussion, said in advance so nobody feels tricked — safety, sleep, being kind to each other
3

What we agreed

Write the rules as the family agreed them, in plain words a child can read. “If it slips” is what happens next, not a punishment to be dreaded.
The ruleWho suggested itWhy it mattersIf it slipsReview date
4

Keeping it alive

Where the rules will live so everyone can see them — the fridge, a shared note, a drawing
The first rule to try, and what you will notice this week about whether it is working, for them and for you
One thing you will say when a rule slips, instead of the thing you usually say
