Worksheet

Why This Feeling Makes Sense

Telling yourself you shouldn't feel something rarely makes it go away. This helps you take one feeling seriously, trace why it makes sense, and speak to yourself the way you'd speak to a friend.

12 fields Emotion RegulationDBT Basic
There is no right answer here and no feeling that's off limits. Validating a feeling doesn't mean agreeing with everything it says or doing what it wants. It means acknowledging that it's there and that, given what happened and who you are, it makes sense. Most of us are far better at this for other people than for ourselves. This is practice at turning that same fairness inward, one feeling at a time.
1

The feeling

The feeling, named as precisely as you can — not just “bad”, but hurt, envious, ashamed, lonely, furious, flat
What you usually say to yourself about having this feeling — “get over it”, “you're being dramatic”, “other people have it worse”
What happens to the feeling when you say that. Does it leave, or does it just go quieter and heavier?
2

Why it makes sense

What happened, and what about it would make almost anyone feel some version of this
What you were hoping for or needing that didn't come — being seen, being safe, being chosen, being left alone
Anything from your history that makes this particular thing land harder for you than it might for someone else
3

Words for a friend

Imagine a friend told you all of the above. Write the first thing you'd say to them, word for word
Now the second thing — the part where you'd tell them why their feeling is reasonable, given everything
The tone you'd use with them: your pace, your face, whether you'd touch their arm. What of that could you give yourself?
4

Turning it inward

Rewrite what you'd say to the friend as a short note to yourself, using your own name and “you”
Read it back. What is it like to hear it, and where in your body do you feel any resistance to it?
The next time this feeling comes, the one sentence from the note you'll try to say to yourself first
