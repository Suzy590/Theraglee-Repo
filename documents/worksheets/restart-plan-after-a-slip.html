The Restart Plan

A missed day only ends a habit if you decide it does. This helps you replace “I blew it” with a plan for the next hour, so a slip stays a slip instead of becoming the end.

12 fields HabitsMotivation Basic
A slip is not a relapse and a relapse is not a verdict. Everyone who has ever kept a habit has also broken it, often more than once. What separates a habit that lasts from one that ends is not the missed day; it is what happens the day after. This is not a test and there are no right answers. Write it while you are steady, so that the version of you who has just slipped has something to read.
1

What happened

The slip, described plainly and without adjectives — what you did or did not do, when, and for how long
What was going on that day. Tiredness, a hard conversation, a party, a bad week, nothing in particular
2

The “I blew it” story

The sentence that shows up after a slip — “I've ruined it”, “I always do this”, “what's the point now”
What that sentence gives you permission to do next. Be honest — usually it is permission to stop
What you would say to a friend who had the same slip and said the same sentence
3

What a slip actually is

How many days you kept the habit before the slip, written as a number
Whether one missed day changes that number, or just adds a gap to it
4

The restart

The very next opportunity to do the habit — not Monday, not the first of the month, the next real chance
The smallest version you will do at that opportunity, so that restarting is easier than the original habit was
One thing to change so the same slip is less likely, if there is one. If there is not, say so and move on
5

Write it for next time

A short note to the you who has just slipped, to read before the story starts. Three or four sentences, kind and practical
Where you will keep this note so you can find it in the moment — phone notes, the fridge, a bookmark

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
