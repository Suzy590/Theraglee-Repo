After a Nightmare or a Bad Morning

A plan you make on an ordinary day for a bad night or a bad morning — what to do in the first minutes, how to get up, what to skip, and who to tell.

11 fields TraumaSelf-CareGrounding Basic
A nightmare or a bad morning can hijack a whole day. This is a plan you make on an ordinary day for a hard one: what you'll do in the first minutes, how you'll get up, what you'll skip, who you'll tell. You will never be asked what the dream was about, and there is no right way to do this. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

How it usually goes

What a bad night or morning looks like for you — a jolt awake, dread before you open your eyes, not wanting to move. No need to describe the dream.
What you usually do in the first hour afterward, and how that tends to go
2

In the first minutes

Three things you'll do before getting out of bed to tell your body it's now — lights on, feet on the floor, say the date aloud, touch the wall
A sentence to say to yourself, out loud if you can — for example, ‘That was a dream. I'm in my room. It's morning.’
What to do with the dream — write one line, tell no one, tell someone, let it go. Whatever you choose is allowed.
3

Getting up

Your bad-morning routine, shorter than your usual one — water, shower, something warm, open the blinds. List the steps in order.
What you'll skip or postpone on a bad morning without apology — the news, email, the hard conversation
4

The rest of the day

Who you could tell, and what you'd say — it can be as little as ‘rough night, go easy on me today’
Something kind and ordinary planned for the afternoon or evening — a walk, a show, food you like
5

Setting up tonight

What you'll put by the bed tonight so the plan is ready — water, a lamp within reach, a note with your sentence, warm socks
One thing to notice next time — did the plan shorten the bad part, even a little?

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
