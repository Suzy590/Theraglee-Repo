A Burnout Map: What Each Part Is Protecting

Burnout tends to show up in three ways: exhaustion, cynicism and feeling ineffective. This walks you through each one and asks what it might be protecting you from, so you can respond with understanding instead of more effort.

12 fields BurnoutWork Basic
This is not a checklist for whether you are “burned out”, and there is nothing to score. Burnout is usually a reasonable response to too much for too long, and each part of it is trying to do something useful: exhaustion asks you to stop, cynicism keeps you from caring about what keeps hurting, and feeling ineffective lowers the bar so you can survive. Mapping them is a way of listening, not a verdict.
1

The exhaustion

How tired you are, described honestly — not just short on sleep, but the kind of tired that rest does not seem to touch
What your exhaustion stops you from doing, and what it might be trying to stop you from doing
2

The cynicism

The things or people you have gone cold on — the work, the clients, the family, the cause you used to believe in
Finish this: “If I let myself care about that again, I would have to feel…”
3

Feeling ineffective

Where you feel you are no longer any good — and how that compares with what you actually got done this week
Finish this: “If I admitted I am still capable, then I would be expected to…”
4

What each is protecting

Exhaustion is a brake. If it were not there, what would you keep doing, and for how much longer?
Cynicism is armor. What hurt was it fitted for, and when did you put it on?
Feeling ineffective lowers the bar. Whose standard was the old bar, and what has it cost you to keep reaching for it?
5

A different response

What each part would need in order to stand down a little — real rest for the brake, some safety for the armor, a fair standard instead of an impossible one
One thing you will hand back, pause or ask for this week, and who you will say it to
A sentence to say to yourself the next time the tiredness or the cold feeling shows up, that treats it as a message rather than a flaw
