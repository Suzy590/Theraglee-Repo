The Fear of Not Sleeping

Set the fears about a bad night next to what bad nights have actually cost you, and find a calmer sentence to reach for when sleep will not come.

10 fields SleepAnxiety Basic
Worrying about not sleeping is one of the most reliable ways to stay awake, which is unfair. This sheet does not tell you the worry is silly. It asks you to write the fear down, then check it against your own record of bad nights and the days that followed. Most people find the days were harder than usual and still happened. There is no score, and no right amount of sleep here.
1

The fear

What you tell yourself when you cannot sleep — “I’ll be useless tomorrow”, “I can’t cope on less than seven”, “this is going to become a thing”
Where that worry shows up in your body once it starts — the jaw, the chest, the legs that will not settle
What you do to try to force sleep, and honestly how well that works
2

Check the record

Bad nights you remember. For each, note roughly how much you slept, what you feared for the next day, what the day was actually like, and the real cost.

Things you have done on very little sleep that turned out fine enough — a shift, a drive, a hard conversation, a whole first year of a child’s life
3

A softer sentence

Rewrite the sentence from the first box into something you could actually believe at 2 a.m. — not cheerful, just accurate
What a friend who sleeps badly and still manages would say to you about tomorrow
4

Resting instead of sleeping

What you will do when sleep will not come — rest still counts. Lying quietly in the dark is not the same as failing.
The next bad night: before you get up, predict tomorrow’s cost out of ten. At the end of that day, write the real number beside it.
