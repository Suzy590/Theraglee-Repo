Planning a Money Conversation

Talking about money with a partner or family member can go sideways fast. This helps you work out what you actually want to say, guess what they'll hear, and set things up so the talk stays a conversation.

13 fields MoneyStress Basic
This is not a script for winning. Money talks go wrong when they arrive as ambushes — one person has been rehearsing for weeks and the other has had ten seconds. This gives you somewhere to do the rehearsing where it belongs, on paper before the talk, so that what you bring to the table is clear, fair, and small enough to actually discuss. There are no right answers, and no one is being judged.
1

What this is really about

The money issue in one plain sentence — the credit card, the wedding cost, the loan to your brother, who pays for what
The feeling underneath the issue for you — fear of running out, feeling unseen, resentment, embarrassment, being the only one who worries
What you want to be different after this conversation. Not “for them to get it”, but a concrete change or decision
2

Their side of the table

What money likely meant in their home growing up, and how that might shape what they hear when you raise it
The version of this conversation they are probably dreading, and what they would need to hear early on to relax
One thing they are right about, even if you don't like it
3

The set-up

When and where you'll ask to talk — not late at night, not mid-argument, not when either of you is hungry or halfway out the door
How you'll open — one sentence that names the topic and says you want to work it out together, e.g. “I'm worried about the card and can't carry it alone”
The one request you'll make. Just one — a number, a decision, a shared look at the account together
4

If it gets heated

The signs, in you and in them, that the conversation is turning into a fight — volume, old grievances, silence, leaving the room
What you'll say to pause it without abandoning it, e.g. “Let's stop here and pick this up Sunday morning after coffee”
5

After the talk

The day and time you'll suggest for the talk, and the exact words you'll use to ask
After the conversation: what they said that surprised you, what got decided, and what still needs a second talk
