A Difficult Feeling, Allowed

A step-by-step sheet for staying with a hard feeling instead of pushing it away — recognize it, allow it, look at it with curiosity, and offer yourself some care. In plain words, at your own pace.

11 fields Emotion RegulationMindfulness Basic
This is for a feeling you would rather not have — the one you have been outrunning all week. You will not be asked to fix it or explain it away. The steps are simple: recognize what is here, allow it to stay, look at it with some curiosity, then be kind to the person feeling it. Go slowly. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

Recognize what is here

The feeling, named as best you can — not just “bad”, but sad, ashamed, furious, lonely, scared, or a mix of several
What set it off, or what you were doing when you first noticed it was there
What you have been doing to keep it at arm's length — busyness, scrolling, snapping at people, sleeping, staying late
2

Allow it to stay

Try saying to yourself, “This is here. It is allowed to be here for now.” Write down what happens when you do — resistance, relief, tears, nothing at all
What you are afraid would happen if you stopped pushing it away and just let it sit
3

Look at it closely

Where it lives in your body right now — chest, throat, stomach, shoulders — and whether it is heavy, tight, hot, or hollow
What the feeling believes. Finish this: “This feeling is telling me that…”
What it might be asking for — rest, to be heard, an apology, to grieve something, to be left alone for an hour
4

Be kind to yourself

What you would say to a close friend feeling exactly this. Write it to yourself, using your own name if that helps
One small kind thing you can do for yourself in the next hour — a hand on your chest, a warm drink, telling someone
What you'll say to yourself the next time this feeling shows up, before you reach for the usual escape

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
