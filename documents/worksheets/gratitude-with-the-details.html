Gratitude, With the Details

Not a list of things you are grateful for, but one of them looked at closely — the sounds, the light, the faces, the exact moment. Specifics are what make gratitude land instead of skimming past.

11 fields MindfulnessSelf-Care Basic
You may have tried writing three things you are grateful for and found it went flat after a week. That is because lists skim. This sheet asks you to pick just one thing and go slowly into it — what it looked like, sounded like, who was there, what your body did. There is no right thing to choose and nothing too small. A good cup of coffee described properly beats a vague “my family” every time.
1

Choose one thing

One thing from the last few days you were glad of. Not a category like “my health” — a moment, an object, a specific person doing a specific thing
Why this one, out of everything that happened — what made it stick with you
2

Go into the senses

What you could see — the light, the colors, the expression on someone's face, the exact place you were standing or sitting
What you could hear, smell, taste, or feel on your skin — a voice, rain, warmth, the weight of something in your hand
What your body was doing in that moment — shoulders dropping, a laugh, breath slowing, leaning in
3

The people in it

Who made this possible, including people you never met — the friend who called, whoever grew the tomatoes, whoever built the bench
What it might have cost them, or what they had to choose, for this to reach you
4

What it stirs

The feeling this brings up when you sit with it for a minute now — warmth, tenderness, an ache, missing someone
What this one moment tells you about what you actually value, beneath the busy version of your life
5

Carry it forward

One person you could tell about this, in a sentence or two, this week — and the words you might use
Something to look for tomorrow that might be like this — the same kind of light, the same kind of kindness
