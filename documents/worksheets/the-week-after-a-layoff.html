The Week After a Layoff

Losing a job knocks out income, routine, and a piece of how you introduce yourself, all at once. This helps you look at the story you're telling, plan a practical week, decide whom to tell, and keep one routine going.

12 fields WorkMoneyLife Transitions Basic
This is not a job-search plan or a pep talk. A layoff is a loss, and the first days are often shock, anger, relief and shame in any order. None of it says anything about your worth. This is a place to get the story straight, do the practical things that matter this week, and keep one piece of your day the same. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

The story you're telling

What happened, in the plainest version you can write — the date, who told you, and what they actually said
The version you have been telling yourself since, especially the sentences that start with “I should have” or “this proves”
What is actually known about why this happened, and what is guesswork filling in the gap
The version you would tell if it had happened to a friend. Notice the difference between the two
2

The practical week

Money: what you know and what you need to find out this week — severance, final paycheck, health coverage, unemployment filing, which bills can wait
The three or four practical things that genuinely need doing in the next seven days, and the day each one will happen
The things that feel urgent but can wait two weeks — updating every profile, telling everyone, deciding what comes next
3

The people to tell

Who needs to know soon, and one sentence you're comfortable saying to each — it can be short, e.g. “My role was cut. I'm okay, and I'll be looking”
Who you're dreading telling, and what you're afraid they will think
Who you'd like to be around this week, whether or not you talk about it
4

One act of routine

One ordinary thing you'll keep doing at the same time every day — dressed by nine, the walk, the gym, dinner at the table
At the end of the week: which day was hardest, what helped, and whether the story in the first section has shifted at all

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
