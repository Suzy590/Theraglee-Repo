What Your Body Needs Right Now

A slow head-to-feet check-in that asks each part of your body what it needs right now, and helps you offer one small thing in return. You never have to say what happened.

10 fields GroundingSelf-CareTrauma Basic
This check-in asks your body what it needs, not what happened to it. You move slowly from head to feet, notice what's there, and ask each area what it would want if it could speak. You can skip any area you'd rather not visit today, stop at any point, and there is nothing to get right. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

Settle in

Where you are right now, and one thing that makes this an okay place to do this — a locked door, a blanket, a window
Anything you want to set down before you begin — the phone, a task, a worry you'll come back to. Name it and set it aside for ten minutes.
2

The scan

Move slowly from the top down. For each area, what do you notice, and what would it ask for if it could? Skip any area you'd rather not visit today.
Area (head, jaw, shoulders, chest, belly, hands, legs and feet)What I noticeTight, numb, or easy?What it needsWhat I can offer now
3

Listen a little longer

The area that most wanted something. What did it ask for — warmth, stillness, movement, to be left alone, to be held?
An area that felt easy or neutral. Rest your attention there a moment. What is that like?
If an area wanted something you can't give right now, what's the nearest thing you can give?
4

Offer something

One thing you'll do in the next ten minutes for the area that needed it most — a stretch, a hand on your chest, a hot drink, socks
How your body feels after, in a sentence — it's fine if the answer is ‘the same’
5

Carry it

A time this week you'll check in like this again, and a two-word cue that will remind you — for example ‘feet first’
One thing your body asked for today that you'd like to give it more often

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
