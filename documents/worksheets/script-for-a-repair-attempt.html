A Script for Making Repair

After a rupture — a snapped reply, a missed moment, a bigger hurt — this helps you write what you'll say to reconnect: the apology, the ownership, and the ask, in your own words.

12 fields RelationshipsCommunication Basic
Every close relationship has ruptures. What matters more is whether they get repaired. This is not a test of how sorry you are and there is no perfect apology. It is a place to draft what you'll say before you say it, so that you go in with something to offer rather than hoping the right words arrive. Write it plainly. Then read it out loud.
1

What happened

The rupture, in a sentence or two — what you did or didn't do, without the reasons yet
What it cost them, as far as you can tell — how they looked, what they said, what changed afterwards
What was going on for you at the time — tired, scared, cornered. This is context, not an excuse.
2

What you own

The part that is yours, said without a “but” — “I snapped at you in front of your sister.”
The part that isn't yours, which you are choosing to leave out of this conversation for now
3

The script

Your opening line — how you'll ask for a few minutes, e.g. “I've been thinking about Tuesday and I'd like to make it right.”
The apology itself, in one or two sentences. Name the thing, name the effect, then stop.
What you'll do differently, small and specific enough that they could notice it happening
What you'll ask them — “Is there anything I've missed?” or “What do you need from me?”
4

If they're not ready

What you'll say if they aren't ready to talk or are still angry — and how you'll leave the door open
How you'll look after yourself if the repair doesn't land the first time
5

Doing it

When you'll say it, and the first four words you will actually open with
