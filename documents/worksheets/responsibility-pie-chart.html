The Responsibility Pie

After something goes wrong, it is easy to hand yourself the whole thing. This helps you list every cause that played a part and work out your honest share.

10 fields CBTThinking TrapsDepression Basic
When something goes badly, a common trap is to take one hundred percent of the responsibility and leave nothing for anyone or anything else. This is not a test and it is not a way of letting yourself off the hook. It is a way of drawing the picture accurately. Picture a pie. Every cause gets a slice, and you get yours last, once the others have been given their honest size.
1

The outcome

The bad outcome, described in one plain sentence — the missed deadline, the argument, the friendship that faded
How much of it feels like your fault right now, as a rough percentage, and the sentence you say to yourself about it
2

Every cause

List every cause that played a part, big or small, and give each a slice. Leave your own slice until the very last row
The causeBelongs toSlice of the pie (%)
Causes people often forget: timing, luck, other people's choices, tiredness, money, what nobody could have known at the time. Add any you missed
3

Your slice

Now that the other slices are drawn, the part that is honestly yours — a specific choice or action, not a character flaw
Whether that part was a mistake, a limit, or a reasonable decision that happened to go wrong
What you'd say to a friend who owned exactly this slice and no more
4

What to do with it

If your slice calls for a repair — an apology, a fix, a conversation — what is the smallest honest version
If it calls for nothing but learning, what you'd do differently in the same spot next time
5

Next week

A sentence to carry with you the next time your mind serves you the whole pie — for example, “I was one cause among several”
