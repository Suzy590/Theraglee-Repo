Anchor Your Mornings

Choose one wake time you can keep every day, plan where your morning light will come from, and track a week of it to see what steadier mornings do to your nights.

10 fields SleepHabits Basic
If you want to move your sleep, the morning is the easier end to hold. A wake time that stays the same and daylight soon after it are two of the steadiest signals your body listens to. This sheet helps you pick an anchor you can actually keep — including on weekends and after a bad night — and plan where the light comes from. It is a plan to try, not a rule to fail.

Your wake times this week, weekdays and weekend. How far apart are the earliest and the latest?

Pick an anchor

The wake time you could keep seven days a week, even after a rough night. Choose the one you can hold, not the one you admire.
What you would have to give up on weekends to keep it, and whether that trade seems worth a month’s trial
3

Find the light

Where the light will come from within an hour of waking — a walk, the porch with coffee, breakfast by the brightest window, the bus stop
How long, realistically. Ten minutes outside beats thirty minutes planned and skipped.

One week of it

Keep it going
