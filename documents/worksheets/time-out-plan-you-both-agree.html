A Time-Out Plan You Both Agree To

Walking away mid-argument only works if the other person knows you're coming back. You and a partner or housemate agree the signal, where you go, how long, and how you return, before you need it.

11 fields AngerRelationshipsCommunication Basic
This plan is written together, ideally on an ordinary evening when nobody is angry. A time-out is not storming off and it is not the silent treatment; it is a pause you have both agreed to in advance, with a return built in. There is no right length and no perfect signal. Whatever the two of you can actually remember at a 7 out of 10 is the right answer. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

Why you need one

What usually happens when an argument between you goes past the point of being useful. Describe the last one briefly, from both sides if you can
What each of you does now when it gets too hot — one leaves, one follows, one goes quiet, one keeps going. Say it without blame
2

The signal

The word, phrase or gesture either of you can use to call a time-out, agreed by both. Short, a bit neutral, and impossible to mistake for an insult
What the other person agrees to do when they hear it — for example, stop talking, not follow, not text during the break
3

Where and how long

Where each of you goes, separately, and what you'll do there that actually lowers the heat rather than rehearses the argument
How long the break lasts — twenty minutes is often the minimum to settle, so agree a time you can both bear and a maximum
What is off the table during the break: driving angry, drinking, calling people to take sides, leaving the house without saying so
4

How you come back

Who comes to find whom, and the first sentence spoken on return. Something like “I'm back, are you ready to try again?”
What happens if one of you is not ready yet — a second, longer break, or a set time to pick it up tomorrow
5

Practice and review

Each time the plan gets used, note how it went. Review together after five uses

Both of you, in your own words: what you're each agreeing to. Sign it, date it, and say where the plan will be kept
