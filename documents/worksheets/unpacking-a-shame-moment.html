Worksheet

Unpacking One Shame Moment

Shame makes you want to disappear, which is exactly why it stays hidden. This worksheet walks you slowly through one recent moment — what set it off, what you believed about yourself, who you hid from, and what you needed.

12 fields ShameSelf-Compassion Basic
Go gently here. Shame is the feeling that something is wrong with who you are, not just with what you did, and it thrives on silence. Writing it down is a way of breaking that silence in private, on your own terms. This is not a confession and there are no right answers. Pick one moment, recent enough to remember clearly, and small enough to look at without being flooded.
1

The moment

The moment, in a few plain sentences. Where you were, who was there, what happened.
What set it off — a look, a comment, a silence, a mistake, being seen doing something ordinary?
What shame felt like in your body: heat in the face, a drop in the stomach, wanting to shrink or leave
2

The belief underneath

Finish this: “In that moment, what I believed about myself was that I am…”
How old that belief feels. Is it new, or is it one you have carried for a long time?
3

Who you hid from

Who you hid from afterward — someone in the room, someone you would normally tell, or yourself
How you hid: went quiet, made a joke, changed the subject, left, replayed it alone at night
What you were afraid they would see if you hadn’t hidden
4

What you needed

What you actually needed in that moment — to be reassured, to be told it was human, to be left alone kindly, to laugh with someone
Who in your life could have given you that, if they had known
Imagine a friend describing this exact moment to you. Write what you would say to them, word for word.
5

Breaking the silence

One person you could tell a small version of this story to this week, and the first sentence you would use
