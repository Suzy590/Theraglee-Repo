The Should Audit

The word “should” carries a lot of quiet pressure. This helps you collect a week of your shoulds, notice who they came from, and decide which ones you actually want to keep.

11 fields CBTThinking Traps Basic
“I should have known better.” “They should have called.” “I should be over this by now.” Shoulds sound like standards, but they often work more like a rulebook nobody agreed to. This is not a test, and you are not being asked to drop your values. You are being asked to look at each rule in daylight, find out where it came from, and rewrite the ones that mostly produce guilt without producing anything else.
1

Collect them

For seven days, write down one should each day the moment you notice it — about yourself, other people, or how the world ought to be

Where they came from

Pick the should that carried the most pressure. Whose voice does it sound like — a parent, a teacher, a former boss, the internet
What that rule was originally for. Most shoulds started as protection or care, even if they no longer feel that way
What happens, honestly, when you break this rule — the real consequence, not the imagined one
3

Keep, soften, or retire

Shoulds you genuinely want to keep because they match what matters to you, rewritten as “I choose to” instead
Shoulds you want to soften. Try replacing “should” with “it would help if” or “I'd prefer to” and write the new version
Shoulds you're ready to retire, and a sentence thanking each one for the job it once did
4

Shoulds aimed at others

One should you aim at other people — “they should know”, “he should have asked”. What do you actually want from them, and could you ask for it plainly
5

This week

The one should you'll catch yourself saying this week, and the replacement sentence you'll say instead
What you'll notice about how you feel when the replacement lands, even if it feels awkward at first
