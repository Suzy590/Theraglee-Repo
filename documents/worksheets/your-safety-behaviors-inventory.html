The Safety Behaviors Inventory

Checking, asking for reassurance and avoiding all bring relief in the moment and feed anxiety over time. This helps you list what you lean on, see what it costs, and choose one to set down this week.

10 fields AnxietyCBT Basic
Safety behaviors are the small things you do to feel safer when anxiety is up: rereading the email four times, asking someone whether it will be fine, taking the long way around. They are not weaknesses; they are sensible responses to fear that have quietly started charging rent. There is no score here and no verdict. The point is to see the full list, notice what each one does for you, and pick just one to try living without for a week.

What they do for you

Pick the one you do most. What it seems to protect you from, and what your anxiety says would happen if you stopped
How long the relief tends to last, and what usually happens to the worry after that
What the behavior has quietly cost — time, closeness with someone, a belief that you could not have coped without it

Choose one

The one safety behavior you will set down this week — the easiest to drop, not the hardest; small counts
The prediction: what your anxiety says will happen without it, as specifically as you can, and how sure you are (0–100%)
What you will do instead in the moment — stay put, wait ten minutes, write the urge down — and who, if anyone, you will tell
4

During the week

Each day you managed without it, a line about what actually happened, and what the urge felt like as it passed
At the end of the week, compare what happened with the prediction. What was different, and what does that tell you about the behavior?
