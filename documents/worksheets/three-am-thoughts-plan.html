Worksheet

What Wakes You at 3 a.m.

Work out what tends to wake you in the small hours, what those thoughts look like by daylight, and a clear plan for what to do instead of lying there arguing with them.

12 fields SleepAnxiety Basic
Thoughts at 3 a.m. are not the thoughts you would have at 3 p.m., even when the words match. The night removes distraction, adds tiredness and makes everything feel urgent and unsolvable. This sheet is not about stopping the thoughts; it is about knowing what to do when they arrive so the night does not become a fight. There are no right answers. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

What the night brings

What tends to wake you — a thought, a full bladder, a sound, heat, or nothing you can name? And roughly what time?
The thoughts that show up once you are awake. Write a few of them down as they actually sound, not tidied up.
What you usually do next — check the phone, look at the clock, argue with the thought, plan, lie very still and hope
2

Daytime you and night you

Take one of those thoughts. How does it look at 3 in the afternoon, with coffee and other people around?
What does the night add to it — no distraction, tiredness, the feeling that this must be solved right now?
What can genuinely be done about it at 3 a.m.? Usually the honest answer is nothing until morning. Write yours.
3

A place to put it

A sentence to write on a notepad by the bed so the thought is kept for morning — “Email Dana about the invoice. Not now.”
A phrase to say to yourself, in your own words, that admits the worry is real and still lets it wait
4

The plan for next time

If you are still awake after what feels like twenty minutes, what you will get up and do — somewhere dim, something dull, no screens
What you will not do, and where the phone and clock will be so that choice is easy
What tells you it is time to go back to bed — heavy eyes, a yawn, the thought losing its grip
5

How it went

After the next 3 a.m. waking: what you did, how it went, and one thing you would keep
