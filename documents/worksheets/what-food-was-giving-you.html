What Food Was Giving You

Eating for comfort is not a failure of willpower; it is a way of meeting a need. This sheet helps you name the feeling, what the food offered, and what else might offer it too.

10 fields FoodSelf-Care Basic
Nothing here is about stopping, fixing, or controlling. Eating when you are sad, bored, anxious, or lonely is one of the most ordinary things a person can do, and it often works, at least for a while. This is a curious look at one recent moment: what you felt, what food gave you, and what else might sometimes give you the same thing. No verdicts, no rules, no right answers.
1

The moment

A recent time you ate when you weren't especially hungry — where you were, what time it was, what you ate
What had happened in the hour before — a conversation, a long stretch of nothing, a piece of news, a deadline
2

The feeling

The feeling that was there, as best you can name it — bored, flat, lonely, tense, wired, sad, angry, nothing at all
Where you felt it in your body, and whether you noticed it before eating or only afterwards
3

What the food gave

What eating actually did — a pause, a taste, warmth, something to do with your hands, a reason to stop working, company
How long that lasted, and what came next once it wore off
4

What else might

Other things that have ever given you the same thing — a walk, a call, a bath, lying down, a show, telling someone
What was in the way of those this time — too tired, too late, nobody around, didn't think of it
5

Being kind about it

What you would say to a friend who described this exact evening to you
Next time the feeling shows up, one thing you might try alongside food — not instead of it, alongside it
