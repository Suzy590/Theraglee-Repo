For Days You Don't Feel Like It

Motivation shows up after you start, not before. This helps you build a plan for the days it never arrives, with a minimum version of the habit and a sentence to get you moving anyway.

12 fields MotivationHabits Basic
There is nothing wrong with you on the days you do not feel like it. Nobody feels like it most of the time; people who keep habits have simply stopped waiting for the feeling. This is not a pep talk and there are no right answers. It is a plan you write while you feel calm, for a version of you who is tired and unconvinced. Make it easy for that person. They are the one who has to use it.
1

The not-today days

The habit this plan is for, and how often the “not today” feeling shows up around it
What “not feeling like it” is actually made of for you — tiredness, dread, resentment, wanting comfort, a blank flatness
What you usually do instead on those days, and how you feel about it by bedtime
2

Waiting for the feeling

A time the motivation did show up partway through, once you had already started
What waiting costs you over a month — the missed days add up to what?
3

The minimum version

The smallest version that still counts on a bad day — two minutes, one page, one street, putting on the shoes
The point at which you are allowed to stop with no guilt, and whether you will tell yourself that in advance
4

Lower the bar to starting

What has to be ready the night before so that starting takes no decisions — clothes out, file open, bag by the door
A person you could message with “doing it now” who will not mind, and might send one back
What you will do in the first thirty seconds, so precisely that you do not have to think
5

The sentence

A sentence to say to yourself in the moment — “Just the shoes”, “I don't have to want to”, “Start and see”. Choose one you can actually hear yourself saying
The next day you expect to not feel like it. Mark it, and plan to use the minimum version on purpose
