The Taking Up Space Log

Shrinking in a room happens in seconds and is easy to forget by evening. This one-week log catches those moments — meetings, groups, family tables — and pairs each one with a sentence for next time.

11 fields AssertivenessWorkRelationships Basic
This is not a log of failures and there is no right amount of space to take. Sometimes going quiet is the wise choice. The point is to notice the moments when you made yourself smaller without deciding to — laughed off your own idea, let someone finish your sentence, gave up your chair — and to write one thing you could say next time. Not a speech. One sentence.
1

Before the week

The rooms where you shrink most — a particular meeting, a friend group, the family table, a class, a chat thread
What shrinking looks like on you — talking faster, trailing off, “sorry, quick thought”, agreeing to end it, going to make the tea
2

One week of noticing

Each day, one moment you made yourself smaller. The last column is one plain sentence you could say next time, not a speech

What the log shows

Look at the “who was there” column. Is there a person, or a kind of person, in most of the rows?
What you believe would happen if you took the space — the interruption, the eye-roll, the silence. Write the fear specifically
A time someone else took that same space in that same room, and what actually happened to them
4

Your body in the room

How you sit, stand, or hold your hands when you're shrinking, and what the opposite would look like — feet flat, shoulders down, hands still
Something you can do with your body before you speak that takes two seconds — a breath, a hand on the table
5

Next time

The one sentence from your log you'll actually say this coming week, and in which room
How you'll finish the sentence — with a full stop, not a question, not “if that makes sense”
Afterward: what you said, what the room did, and whether you'd say it again
