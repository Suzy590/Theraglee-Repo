After a Boundary Is Crossed

A boundary that gets crossed is not proof it was wrong to set. This sheet walks you through what happened, what it cost, what you'll restate, and what you'll do differently next time.

12 fields BoundariesRelationships Basic
There is no verdict on whether you should have held firmer. Boundaries get crossed; that is part of having them. What matters is what you do in the days after, when the temptation is either to give up on the boundary or to give up on the person. This sheet looks at what happened, what it cost, and how you'll say it again. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

What happened

The boundary as you originally set it, in the words you used at the time
How it was crossed — what they did or said, when, and whether it was a slow lean or a straight step over
What you did in the moment — went along, went quiet, argued, left — without judging it
2

What it cost

What it cost you that day — time, money, sleep, a plan, your mood
What it cost in the week after, including anything you said yes to that you wouldn't have otherwise
What you've been telling yourself since — “I should have”, “it's not worth it”, “they'll never change”
3

What you'll restate

The boundary, said again. Keep the original if it was right; tighten it if the crossing showed a gap. One sentence
How and when you'll say it to them — face to face, a message, next time it comes up — and whether you need to say it at all
What you'll leave out this time — the apology, the explanation, the “I know it's hard for you”
4

What you'll do differently

The early sign that the boundary is about to be crossed, and what you'll do at that moment rather than afterward
One thing you'll do for yourself, or ask someone for, so that the next crossing costs less
A sentence to say to yourself the next time this happens — something steadier than “I should have”

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
