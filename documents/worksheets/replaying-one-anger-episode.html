Replaying One Anger Episode in Slow Motion

Anger moves fast, which is why it is hard to learn from in the moment. You slow one recent episode down frame by frame — trigger, body, thought, action, wish, cost — so the next one has more room.

12 fields AngerEmotion Regulation Basic
Pick one episode, recent enough that you can still feel its edges. This is a replay, not a trial. There is no score and no verdict on whether you were right to be angry; anger is information about something that mattered to you. You are looking for the moments that went by too quickly to notice, because those are the only places a different choice could ever fit.
1

The moment

What happened, in two or three plain sentences, the way a camera would have seen it
The exact trigger — the sentence, tone, look, delay or object that tipped it. Be precise: “the third time he sighed”, not “his attitude”
2

Your body first

The first sign in your body, before you knew you were angry — heat in the face, tight jaw, a held breath, a change in your voice
How long between that first body signal and the point where you acted? A second, ten, a minute?
3

The thought

The thought that arrived with the heat, in its own words — “here we go again”, “nobody listens to me”, “they think I'm stupid”
Underneath that thought, what did the moment seem to mean about you, or about how you are treated?
4

What you did and wanted

What you actually did or said, including anything you are not proud of. Plain description, no commentary
What you really wanted in that moment — to be heard, to be left alone, for it to stop, to be taken seriously
Did what you did get you what you wanted? Fully, partly, or did it get you something else?
5

Cost and next time

What the episode cost — the mood afterward, the look on their face, the apology owed, the hour lost, the thing said that can't be unsaid
Looking at the replay, the one frame where a different move was possible — usually right after the first body signal. What would that move be?
One sentence you would like to be able to say at that frame next time, written out in full
