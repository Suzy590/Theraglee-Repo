The Friction Map

The habits you keep are often the easiest ones, not the ones you want most. This helps you map what makes a wanted habit hard and an unwanted one easy, then change one small thing on each side.

12 fields Habits Basic
This is not about trying harder. It is about noticing how your day is arranged, because the arrangement decides more than your willpower does. Every habit has a path of small steps in front of it. When the path is short and clear, the habit happens. When it is long or cluttered, it does not. There are no right answers here — only an honest look at the paths, and one small change to each.
1

The two habits

The habit you want more of, and the habit you want less of. Name both plainly
Where and when each one usually happens, or fails to happen
2

The wanted habit's path

Every step between deciding to do it and actually doing it — finding the shoes, clearing the table, opening the app. Then what could make each step shorter
StepWhat makes it hardHow hard (1–10)One way to make it easier
The single step where you most often give up, and what you are usually feeling at that point
3

The unwanted habit's path

What is already set up so that this habit needs almost no effort — the snacks in view, the app on the home screen, the remote within reach
What it gives you in the moment. Be fair to it; it is doing a job
One step you could add in front of it — a drawer, a logout, a rule about the room it happens in
4

One swap each

The one change that makes the wanted habit easier, chosen from the table. Small and physical beats big and mental
The one change that makes the unwanted habit a little harder. It only needs to add a pause, not a wall
5

Try it for a week

When you will make both changes. Day, time, and what you need to buy, move, or delete
What you expect to happen, so you can compare it with what does
After a week: what changed, what did not, and which swap deserves to stay
