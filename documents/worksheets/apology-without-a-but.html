An Apology Without a But

After losing your temper, a good apology names what you did, its impact, and what you'll do next time — and stops there. You draft yours here so it comes out clean instead of turning into round two.

11 fields AngerCommunicationRelationships Basic
This is for the morning after. A real apology is short, specific and has no “but” in it, which is harder than it sounds when you still believe you had a point. You may well have had a point; it goes in a different conversation. Nothing here is a verdict on you as a person. Losing your temper is something you did, and this is about repairing it, not about being forgiven on the spot.
1

What you did

What you actually did, in plain words and in the first person — “I shouted”, “I slammed the door”, “I called you selfish”. No “things got heated”
The parts you'd rather leave out because they are the most embarrassing. Those are usually the parts they remember most
2

Its impact

What you saw on their face or heard in their voice, and what you think it was like to be on the other end of you
What it may have cost them since — sleep, trust, walking on eggshells, a story they now tell themselves about you
3

The but

Every “but” you're tempted to include: they started it, you were exhausted, they know how to push your buttons. Write them all here, and then leave them here
The point you still think is fair, in one sentence, and when you could raise it later, separately, once the repair has had time to land
4

Next time

What you'll do differently next time it gets hot — concrete and checkable, like “I'll say I need twenty minutes and go outside”, not “I'll try to stay calm”
What you're asking of them, if anything, that would make next time easier. Keep it small, and keep it for after the apology
5

The script

The whole apology in three to five sentences: what you did, its impact, what you'll do next time. Read it back and cut any “but”, “if” or “you”
What you'll do if they are not ready to accept it — they are allowed to still be hurt. A sentence for that moment, such as “I understand”
When and where you'll say it, in person if you can, and what you'll do with yourself in the hour afterward
