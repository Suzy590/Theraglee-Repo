A Letter to Who or What You Lost

Grief often holds words that never got said. This gives you a quiet place to write them — to a person, a pet, a home, a former life — with no need to send it anywhere.

11 fields GriefLoss Basic
This is a letter nobody has to read but you. It is not a test of how well you are grieving, and there is no correct thing to say. You can be angry, grateful, unfinished, or all three in one paragraph. Write in whatever order the words come. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

Before you write

Who or what this letter is to, and how you want to address them — a name, a nickname, “dear old house”
What you are hoping the letter might do — say goodbye, argue, thank, explain, catch them up, or just keep talking
Where you are writing this, and whether it is somewhere you can be interrupted. It is fine to stop and come back
2

The letter

Start with what has happened since. The ordinary news you would have told them first
What you miss most, in specifics — a sound, a habit, the way a particular day used to go
What was never said, or never finished — an apology, a thank-you, a question, the thing you were saving for later
What you are angry about, if anything. Being angry at someone you loved, or at a loss you did not choose, is allowed here
What you want them to know about how you are carrying on, even on the days you are not carrying on well
How you want to sign off. Take your time with the last line
3

After the letter

Read it back once. What you notice in your body, and what surprised you on the page
What you would like to do with it — keep it, read it somewhere that mattered, share it with one person, or write another one next month

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
