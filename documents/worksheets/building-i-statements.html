Building “I” Statements

Turn complaints into statements the other person can actually hear. Six rows to practice the shape — when you…, I feel…, because…, what I'd like… — with the situations that matter to you.

9 fields CommunicationRelationships Basic
An “I” statement is not a magic phrase, and this is not a test of getting the grammar right. It is a shape for saying something hard without an accusation attached, so the other person hears the feeling instead of bracing for the blame. It can feel stilted at first; that is normal. Practice it here with real situations until it starts to sound like you.
1

What you usually say

Three things you've said recently, or wanted to, that begin with “you” — “you never listen”, “you're always late”
What tends to happen when you say them that way — for you and for them
2

Six statements

One situation per row. Describe the behavior, not the person: “when you look at your phone while I'm talking”, not “when you ignore me”
When you…I feel…because…What I'd like is…
3

Check them

Read each “I feel” — is it a feeling (hurt, left out, worried) or a verdict in disguise (“I feel like you don't care”)?
Read each “what I'd like” — is it specific enough that they would know when they had done it?
The row that felt most true when you read it back, and why that one
4

Say one

Which statement you'll try first, with whom, and when — ideally not in the middle of the thing itself
After you've said it: what happened, and how it compared with the “you” version
5

Keep it yours

How you'll make the shape sound like you rather than a script — the words you'd swap in or out
