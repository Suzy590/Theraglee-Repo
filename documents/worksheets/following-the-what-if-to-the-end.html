Following the “What If” to the End

A catastrophic thought usually stops at the scariest frame and stays there. This walks you down each “and then what?” rung, rates how likely each step really is, and looks at how you would cope.

9 fields AnxietyWorryCBT Basic
Anxious thoughts tend to jump straight to the worst picture and then freeze the film there, so you never get to see what happens next. This worksheet asks you to follow one “what if” all the way down, one rung at a time, and to rate each rung honestly. It is not a test and there are no right answers. If you are in danger or cannot keep yourself safe, please reach out now — in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.
1

The thought at the top

The “what if” thought, written exactly as it arrives — for example, “what if I freeze during the presentation?”
How much you believe it right now (0–100%), and what happens in your body when you read it back
2

Down the ladder

Follow the thought down. Each row asks “and if that happened, then what?” about the row above, until there is nowhere left to go

3

At the bottom

The final rung — the very worst end point the thought was pointing to — written out plainly in one sentence
Roughly combine the likelihoods: how likely is the bottom rung once every single step has to happen, one after another?
Suppose the bottom rung happened anyway. What would the first week look like, and what would still be true about you and your life?
Which rung was the one your mind had actually been stuck on, and what it felt like to keep climbing past it
4

Back at the top

Read the first thought again. How much you believe it now (0–100%), and what, if anything, shifted
One short thing to say to yourself the next time this “what if” arrives, drawn from what you found lower on the ladder
