From Resentment to Request

Resentment is anger that has settled in and stopped asking for anything. Six rows take each grudge from what you're angry about to what you'd like instead to how you'd actually ask for it.

10 fields AngerCommunicationRelationships Basic
Resentment is a request that was never made, or was made once and then buried. It is not a character flaw; it is usually the residue of not wanting a fight. This converter is not about deciding whether your grievance is fair. It takes each one and asks what it wants, then finds words that could be said out loud without starting a war. You only need to say one of them.
1

The weight you carry

The people or situations you feel a low, steady anger toward at the moment. Names are fine; nobody sees this but you
How the resentment shows itself — a tone, keeping score, doing the thing yourself with a sigh, a story you tell others about them
2

The converter

One resentment per row. In the last column, write the request the way you would actually say it — specific, one thing, no history lesson

3

Check the requests

Read the “how I'd ask” column back. Cross out any “you always” or “you never”, and any request that is really a punishment in disguise
For each row, could the other person actually do what you're asking? If not, what smaller version could they?
Which row have you never said out loud in any form, and what has kept it silent — fear of the reaction, not wanting to seem petty, giving up?
4

Choose one

The single request you'll make this week. Pick the one most likely to be heard, not the one that hurts most
When and where you'll ask — not mid-argument, not by text at midnight. A calm moment with time to spare
What you'll do if the answer is no. Resentment grows fastest where a “no” is treated as the end of the conversation
5

Say it

The request in full, in your own voice, ready to say. Read it aloud once before you use it
