The Mind-Reading Check

When you are sure you know what someone thinks of you, it is worth checking. This helps you separate what you assumed from what you saw, and find out for real.

12 fields Thinking TrapsAnxiety Basic
Mind reading is guessing what someone else thinks and then treating the guess as fact. Everybody does it, and anxious or tired minds do it faster and darker. This is not a test, and there are no right answers — you might even turn out to be correct. The point is to slow the guess down, look at the evidence, and work out whether there is a way to find out instead of assuming.
1

The moment

What happened — who was there, what was said or not said, the look, the short reply, the unanswered message
What you decided they were thinking about you, in the exact words your mind used
How sure you felt at the time, and how your body reacted
2

The evidence

What you actually saw or heard. Only things a camera would have recorded
What you filled in from your own head — tone, motive, what they “must” have meant
Three other explanations for what they did that have nothing to do with you — tired, distracted, worried about something else
3

Your part

When you believe this is what they think, how do you act toward them — pull back, go quiet, over-apologize, get sharp
How your reaction might look from their side, and what they might guess about you in turn
4

Finding out

A way you could actually check, and the plain words you'd use — “you seemed quiet earlier, is everything okay between us?”
What stops you from asking, and what asking would cost compared with guessing
5

Next time

A short sentence to say to yourself the next time you catch a mind-reading thought — for example, “that is a guess, not a fact”
One person you'll check with this week rather than assume, and when
