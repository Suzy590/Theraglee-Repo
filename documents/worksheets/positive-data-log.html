Evidence for a Kinder Belief

A new belief needs evidence before it feels true. This gives you a week to collect small, ordinary proof for a kinder sentence about yourself that you're trying out.

10 fields CBTDepression Basic
An old belief like “I'm a burden” has years of evidence filed under it, while a newer, kinder belief has almost none — not because it is false, but because you haven't been collecting. This is not a test and there is nothing to score. For seven days you simply write down anything, however small, that fits the new sentence. Holding the door counts. Being thanked counts. The bar is deliberately low; the point is to start the file.
1

The two sentences

The old belief you're tired of — “I'm a burden”, “I always mess things up”, “people put up with me”
The kinder belief you're testing, worded so it's believable rather than grand — “I contribute”, “I'm someone people are glad to see”
How true the new sentence feels today, in your own words
2

Set the bar low

What will count as evidence. List small things in advance — a text answered, a job done, a laugh shared — so you recognize them when they happen
3

Seven days of evidence

Each day, write down at least one thing that fits the kinder belief, however ordinary

Anything you nearly didn't write down because it seemed too small, or because your mind said “that doesn't count”
4

Look at the file

Read the whole table back. Which entries surprise you, and which would you have missed a month ago
How your mind tried to dismiss the evidence — “anyone would have”, “they were just being polite” — and what you'd say back
5

Keep collecting

How true the kinder sentence feels now, compared with day one
Where you'll keep this log going — a note on your phone, a page by the bed — and the first thing you'll add tomorrow
