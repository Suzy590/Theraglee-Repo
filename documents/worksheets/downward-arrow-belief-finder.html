Follow the Thought Down

A small worry often has a bigger belief underneath it. This helps you follow one thought down, one “and what would that mean?” at a time, until you reach the belief — then answer it.

12 fields CBTDepression Basic
An everyday thought like “I sent that email too late” can sit on top of something much older, such as “I let people down” or “I am not enough.” This is not a test and there is no right answer waiting at the bottom. You follow the thought down by asking, each time, “and if that were true, what would it mean?” — and you stop when the answer starts repeating. Then, gently, you reply to what you found.
1

The starting thought

The thought that bothered you today, small and specific — “I said the wrong thing at lunch”, “I haven't texted back”
What feeling came with it, and how strong that feeling was
2

Follow it down

If that were true, what would it mean about you, your life, or other people

Keep going only if it hasn't started to repeat. And if that were true, what would it mean
Where the answers started repeating. Write the belief you landed on as one plain sentence
3

Meet the belief

How old this belief feels, and the earliest memory you have of believing it
What it has cost you to carry it, and what it has tried to protect you from
4

Answer it

Evidence from your actual life that does not fit this belief — moments, people, things you've done
A truer, kinder sentence you could put in its place. It doesn't have to be the opposite; “I sometimes let people down, like everyone, and I repair it” is fair
5

Back to the top

Return to the starting thought and read it with the new sentence underneath. What does it look like now, and what is the next ordinary thing to do
