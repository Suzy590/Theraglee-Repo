Worksheet

When the Mirror Gets Loud

A harsh thought in front of a mirror or a photo can take over a day. This sheet helps you slow one of those moments down, find whose voice it was, and remember what your body did for you.

12 fields BodySelf-Esteem Basic
This is not about learning to love how you look, and it is not a test of your confidence. It is about one moment — a mirror, a changing room, a photo someone tagged you in — and the thought that arrived with it. Thoughts about our bodies are often borrowed from somewhere else. This sheet helps you look at where this one came from and what was actually true about your body that day.
1

The moment

Where you were and what you were looking at — a mirror, a shop window, a photo, a video call
What you were doing just before, and how you were already feeling
2

The thought

The exact words of the thought, as harsh as they were
What the thought told you to do or not do — change clothes, skip the event, hide, delete the photo, eat differently
How much of the rest of the day it followed you around
3

Whose voice

Whose voice the thought sounds like — a parent, a coach, an ex, a magazine, a comment at school, your own from years ago
When you first learned that this part of your body was a problem, and who was there
What that person or source was gaining, needing, or afraid of when they said it
4

What your body did today

What your body did for you today, before and after that moment — carried bags, digested lunch, laughed, kept you warm, got you here
Something your body let you do this week that you would have missed without it
5

Next time

A sentence to say back to the voice — not a compliment, just a fact, like “this body got me through today”
One small thing you'll do next time the mirror gets loud — look away, name the voice, touch something solid, go outside
