The No List

Every yes needs room, and room comes from saying no to good things. This helps you name what you're choosing not to pursue this year, find the words to hold the line, and give the yes its first hour.

11 fields GoalsDecision Making Basic
This is not a list of things you have failed at or given up on. Choosing not to pursue something this year is a decision, and a generous one, because it leaves space for what you actually want. Some of the nos will be good things you would genuinely enjoy; that is what makes them hard, and also what makes the exercise worth doing. Nothing here is forever. It is only this year.
1

The yes

The one or two things you most want this year to hold — the yes that needs room to breathe
What is currently sharing the calendar and the energy with it — commitments, half-finished projects, other people's priorities
2

The no list

Things you are choosing not to pursue this year. Not forever, just this year. Include good things you'd genuinely like to do
For each no, why it is a no for now — wrong season, someone else's dream, the price, what it would take from the yes
A no that is hard to write because someone else expects it of you. Whose voice is that, and what does it say?
A no that feels like relief. Sit with that for a moment and say what the relief is about
3

Making it stick

What you'll say when one of the nos is offered to you — a sentence you could actually get out, like “Not this year, ask me in the spring”
The person most likely to push back, and what you'll say to them when they do
Where you'll keep this list so it is still in sight in March, when the nos start looking tempting again
4

Room for the yes

With those out of the way, the first thing the yes gets this month — an hour, a booking, a conversation
How you'll notice, a few months from now, that the no list did its job
