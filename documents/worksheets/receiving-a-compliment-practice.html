Receiving a Compliment

When someone says something kind about you, the reflex is to deflect it. This worksheet is a seven-day practice in letting compliments land — noticing what you do with them, and trying a simple thank you instead.

11 fields Self-EsteemSelf-Compassion Basic
There are no right answers here, and deflecting praise is not a character flaw — for many people it is manners, or safety, or both. But a compliment that bounces off never gets a chance to do its work. For one week, you will track the kind things people say, what you did with them, and how it felt to try receiving them. It will feel awkward. Awkward is fine.

The practice

The practice: say “thank you”, full stop, then let two seconds pass before saying anything else. Write it out in your own words so it is yours.
What you will do with your face and body while those two seconds pass

Seven days of receiving

One row per compliment, for seven days. Include tiny ones — “nice job”, “that was helpful”, “you look well”.

One to keep

One compliment from the week you will keep. Write it here in the exact words, and who said it.
