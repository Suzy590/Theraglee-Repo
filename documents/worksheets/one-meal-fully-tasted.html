One Meal, Fully Tasted

A single-meal reflection for eating with your attention on the food instead of a screen. You slow down for one sitting and notice appetite, taste, texture, and the moment you have had enough.

11 fields MindfulnessSelf-Care Basic
Pick one meal — not a special one, just the next one. Eat it without a screen if you can, and a little slower than usual. This is not a diet sheet and there is nothing to get right; you are not counting anything or judging what is on the plate. You are simply paying attention to something you do every day and mostly miss. Fill in the first part before you eat and the rest afterward.
1

Before the first bite

The meal, where you are eating it, and what you would normally be doing while you ate it
How hungry you are right now, in your own words — “ravenous”, “not really, just habit”, “could go either way”
What you notice with your eyes and nose before tasting anything — color, steam, a smell you usually skip past
2

While eating

The first bite, described as if to someone who has never eaten this — texture, temperature, where the taste lands
How the taste changed between the first bite and the fifth, if it changed at all
Where your mind went while you ate, and how many times you caught it and brought it back to the plate
The moment you noticed you had had enough — or that you didn't notice, and what that was like
3

After the meal

How you feel in your body now — full, comfortable, heavy, still hungry, satisfied, restless
One thing you tasted today that you have eaten a hundred times without ever really noticing
How this meal differed from the way you usually eat, in plain terms — slower, quieter, more, less, the same
4

Next time

One meal this week you'll eat this way again, and the one small thing you'll change — phone in another room, sitting down, the first three bites slow
