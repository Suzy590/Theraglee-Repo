Your First 30 Days Somewhere New

Starting a new job, school, or role is a stretch of not-knowing. This helps you decide what to learn, whom to meet, and what to give yourself permission not to know yet, one week at a time.

12 fields WorkLife Transitions Basic
Nobody expects you to know everything in the first month, though it can feel as if they do. This is not a performance review, and there are no right answers. It is a plan for how you'll spend the not-knowing — what to learn first, whom to meet, and what to leave alone until later — so that the days have a shape even while the role doesn't yet. Fill in what you can now and come back as the weeks pass.
1

Before day one

What you are stepping into — the role, the place, the start date — and the one thing you are most looking forward to
What you're most nervous about, said plainly: not knowing anyone, looking slow, the commute, the first question you can't answer
What you learned from the last time you started somewhere — what helped in the first weeks, and what you'd skip this time
2

The four weeks

One row per week: what you'll learn, whom you'll meet, one question you'll ask, and one thing you'll deliberately not worry about yet. Last row: what can wait
WeekLearnMeetAskLet go of for now
3

The people

The people who could make the first month easier — the one who knows where things are, the one who started last year, the one everyone goes to
How you'll introduce yourself in a sentence or two, and the one question you'll ask each new person so you're not doing all the talking
4

Looking after yourself

Things you give yourself permission not to know or not to be yet — write them as permissions, e.g. “I'm allowed to ask the same question twice”
The signs that you're pushing too hard — skipped lunches, rereading emails at midnight, apologizing for things nobody minded
What you'll keep the same outside the new place — the run, the Sunday call, the show you watch — so not everything changes at once
Who you'll tell how it's going, and when, so the first weeks are not carried alone
5

Day 30

On day 30, what you'll look back and check — not “did I nail it” but “did I learn what I set out to, and did I meet the people”
Day 30, filled in then: what turned out easier than expected, what was harder, and one thing you know now that day-one you didn't
