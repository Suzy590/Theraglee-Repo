Build Your Wind-Down Hour

Plan the last sixty minutes before lights out in four fifteen-minute blocks, starting from what genuinely settles you rather than from anyone else’s idea of a perfect bedtime routine.

11 fields SleepHabitsSelf-Care Basic
There is no correct wind-down routine, only the one you will actually do. This sheet starts with how your evenings go now, then asks what settles you and what merely feels restful while quietly keeping you awake. From there you build the last hour before lights out in four short blocks. Keep it modest. An hour you can repeat on an ordinary Tuesday is worth far more than a beautiful one you manage twice.
1

How evenings go now

Walk through last night. From an hour before lights out, what did you actually do, in order?
Which parts of that hour left you more awake than before — a work email, the news, a game that was hard to stop?
2

What actually settles you

Things that reliably settle you, even a little — a shower, stretching, a dull book, tidying one surface, talking to someone, a warm drink without caffeine
Things that feel like rest but tend to wake you up — scrolling, one more episode, reading about sleep at midnight. Be honest, not harsh.
3

The hour, in four blocks

Sixty to forty-five minutes before lights out. What happens in this block, and where in the house?
Forty-five to thirty minutes before. Ideally the screens are put down somewhere here — what takes their place?
Thirty to fifteen minutes before. The quieter, slower things go here — what are they for you?
The last fifteen minutes. What happens between the bathroom and the light going off?
4

Making it stick

The cue that starts the hour — an alarm with a kind label, the dishwasher going on, the dog’s last walk. Something outside your willpower.
What is most likely to derail it this week, and what you will do when that happens rather than abandoning the whole hour
The first night you will try it, and one thing to notice the next morning — how quickly you fell asleep, or simply how the hour felt
