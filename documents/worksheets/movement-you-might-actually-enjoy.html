Movement You Might Actually Enjoy

Movement that feels like punishment rarely lasts. This finder helps you try out ways of moving your body, rate how each one felt before and after, and keep the ones that leave you better off.

10 fields MovementBody Basic
This has nothing to do with burning anything off, changing your shape, or hitting a number. Movement here means anything that gets your body doing something — a stretch in bed, dancing in the kitchen, walking to the mailbox, swimming, gardening. The only measure that matters is how you feel afterwards compared with before. There are no right answers, and “I hated it” is useful information too.
1

Before you try anything

What “exercise” has meant to you so far — gym class, a program you quit, something a doctor said, something you loved as a kid
Ways of moving that you have ever enjoyed, even briefly, even a long time ago
2

Trying things out

Six ways of moving to try over the next couple of weeks, rated on how you felt before and after, where 10 is best

3

What you noticed

Which ones left you feeling better than before, and what specifically was better — mood, energy, sleep, a quieter head
Which ones felt like a chore or a punishment, and what made them feel that way — the setting, the pace, someone watching, an old memory
Anything that surprised you about how your body or your mood responded
4

What gets in the way

The thoughts that show up right before moving — “it won't count unless…”, “I'll look silly”, “I should be doing more”
What would make the enjoyable ones easier — a friend, a podcast, a time of day, lower expectations
5

Keeping what works

The one or two you want to keep, and the smallest version of each — five minutes counts, so does one song
When you'll do the next one. Day, time, and where.
