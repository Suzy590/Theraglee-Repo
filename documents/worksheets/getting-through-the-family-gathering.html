Getting Through the Family Gathering

Holidays and family gatherings can undo months of steadiness in an afternoon. This before, during, and after plan names the people and topics you expect, gives you an exit line, and makes sure you are not alone.

11 fields FamilyStress Basic
This is a plan, not a test, and there is no right way to do a family gathering. Dreading one does not make you ungrateful or difficult. It usually means you know exactly what tends to happen there. Planning for it is not the same as expecting the worst. It is a way of walking in with some choices already made, so you have more of yourself left by the end of the day.
1

Before you go

The gathering — where, when, how long, and who will be there. Include how you are getting there and, more importantly, how you can leave.
What usually happens that you dread. Be specific — the comment, the seating plan, the question about your life.
What you actually want from the day, if anything — to see one person, to eat well, to leave by four, to not fight
2

The people and the topics

One row per person or topic you expect trouble from. An exit line is a short sentence that ends the conversation, like “I'm going to check on the food.”

During the day

Where you will go for five minutes alone when you need it — the porch, the car, a walk with the dog
The signal you will notice in your body that says it is time to use an exit line or take a break
Your ally — what you will ask them to do, in one sentence, and when you will ask them before the day
The one thing you decide in advance you will not do — argue about that topic, explain your choices, stay past a certain time
4

After the day

How you will get home, and what you will do that evening to come back to yourself
Afterward: what actually happened, which parts of the plan you used, and what surprised you
One sentence you want to remember for the next gathering, whenever it comes
