Dividing the Invisible Load

Households run on work nobody sees — remembering, planning, noticing, worrying. This sheet maps who carries what with a partner or co-parent, then helps you plan a conversation about it that is not a fight.

11 fields RelationshipsParentingFamily Basic
This is not a scorecard and there are no right answers. The invisible load is the remembering, tracking, and worrying that keeps a family running, and it is often carried mostly by one person without anyone ever deciding it should be. Naming it is not an accusation. Fill this in for yourself first, and if it helps, invite your partner or co-parent to fill in their own copy before you compare.
1

Naming the load

The things you are keeping in your head right now — appointments, birthdays, school forms, the thing that needs fixing, who is low on socks
The things you believe your partner or co-parent keeps in their head. Be fair, and guess honestly.
2

Who carries what

One task per row. Noticing, planning, and doing are three different jobs — a task can be done by one person and carried by another.
The taskWho notices itWho plans itWho does itHow heavy (1–10)
3

Reading the table

Which rows surprised you, and which ones felt exactly as you expected them to
The task you most resent carrying, and what the resentment is really about — the task itself, or not being seen
A task you have been holding onto that you could genuinely hand over completely, including the noticing and the planning
4

The conversation

How you will open it, in a sentence that describes rather than accuses — for example, “I've noticed I'm the one tracking the school stuff and it's wearing me out.”
What you will ask for, specifically — one task, handed over whole, by a date you both agree
What you will do if they get defensive, and what you will do if they simply agree
5

After you talk

When you will have the conversation, and where — not at bedtime, and not in the middle of an argument
Afterward: what was said, what changed, and the one task that now lives somewhere else
