Six Asks, Lowest Stakes First

Asking for what you need is a skill that builds from small to large. This builder has you write six asks, from the easiest to the one you've been avoiding, and make each one specific enough to say.

9 fields AssertivenessCommunication Basic
This is not a test of how assertive you are, and nobody needs to make all six asks this month. The idea is to build a ladder. The bottom rung is something you could ask for today with barely a flutter; the top rung is the one you've been carrying around unspoken. Writing them all out, in order, makes the big one look less like a cliff and more like a few more steps.
1

What you're not asking for

Things you've been needing and not asking for — more help at home, a raise, an answer, quiet, to be visited. Just list them
What usually happens instead of asking — you hint, you do it yourself, you wait, you go quiet?
2

The ladder

Order your asks from lowest stakes to highest. Write the exact words you'd use, not a summary of them

Make them sayable

Look at your exact words in each row. Cross out, in your head, any apology or reason that comes before the ask itself. What's left?
For the highest-stakes ask, what you fear the answer will be, and what you'd do the next day if that answer came
For the same ask, what a yes would actually change in your week
4

The first rung

The lowest-stakes ask, when you'll make it, and what you'll do with your face and hands while you wait for the answer
Afterward: what you said, what they said, and how it compared with what you expected
5

The next rung

Which ask you'll climb to next, and one thing from the first ask that will make the second one easier
