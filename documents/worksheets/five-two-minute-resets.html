Five Two-Minute Resets for a Day With No Free Hour

On the days when rest is out of the question, two minutes is still possible. This helps you plan five tiny resets into the gaps that already exist, and see whether they change how the day ends.

10 fields Self-CareStress Basic
This is not another thing to fit in, and it is not a test. Some days have no hour to spare, and telling yourself to “take a break” on those days only adds to the load. A two-minute reset is small enough to fit between one thing and the next: a few slow breaths at the sink, a look out of a window, feet flat on the floor for one song. Nothing here needs to be done well, only tried.
1

The day in question

The kind of day this is for — back-to-back meetings, a shift, a day with small children, a hospital day — and what it usually leaves you feeling by evening
The gaps that already exist, even if they are only ninety seconds — the kettle, the elevator, the walk to the car, the wait for a file to load
2

What resets you

Things that settle you a little, fast — slow breathing, cold water on the wrists, stretching your neck, stepping outside, a photo you love, eyes closed in silence
What tends to derail a break, even a tiny one — picking up the phone, someone finding you, the feeling that you have not earned it
3

The plan

Five resets, each tied to a gap you already have. Fill the last column at the end of the day.

Making it happen

How you will remember each one — an alarm with a kind label, a sticky note, tying it to something you already do
What you will say to yourself if you miss two or three of them, so that missing one does not cancel the rest

End of the day

How the day ended compared with a normal day of this kind — steadier, about the same, worse
Which reset you would keep even on an ordinary day, and the one you would swap for something else
