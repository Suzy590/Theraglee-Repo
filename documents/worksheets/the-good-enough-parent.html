Worksheet

The Good Enough Parent

The standard many parents hold themselves to would exhaust anyone. This sheet helps you write out that standard, trace where it came from, and check it against what your child actually needs from you.

12 fields ParentingStress Basic
There are no right answers here and no verdict on the parent you are. Children do not need a perfect parent; they need one who is present enough, kind enough, and steady enough, most of the time. If the bar you hold yourself to feels impossible, that is information about the bar, not about you. This sheet is for looking at it closely enough to decide whether it is really yours.
1

The standard

The parent you feel you should be — write it out as a job description: how patient, how present, how organized, how cheerful
The moments this week when you fell short of it, and what you said to yourself afterward
What you never let yourself do because a good parent wouldn't — sit down, say no, be bored, ask for a break
2

Where it came from

Whose voice the standard sounds like — a parent, a relative, a book, a feed full of other people's kitchens
What your own parents got wrong that you are determined to get right, and how that shapes the bar
Who benefits from you holding this standard, and who pays for it — including you
3

What your child needs

Picture your child at their happiest with you this month. What were you actually doing? It is often something small.
What your child would say they need from you, in their words. If they are old enough, ask them.
One thing on your job description that your child has never asked for and would not notice if it stopped
4

The experiment

One thing you will deliberately do less than perfectly this week — store-bought cake, a movie so you can rest, a plain no
Your prediction: what you expect to happen, and how you expect to feel about it
The result: what actually happened, what your child did, and what you noticed about the bar afterward
