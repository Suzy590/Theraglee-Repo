Moments That Felt Worth It

Meaning tends to hide in small moments rather than big achievements. This seven-day log helps you catch one moment a day that felt worth it, then see what those moments have in common.

9 fields ValuesMotivation Basic
This is not a gratitude exercise and you do not have to feel thankful for anything. It is simply a week of noticing which moments, when you look back on the day, felt worth having been there for. Some days it will be obvious and some days you will struggle to find one; both are fine and both tell you something. There are no wrong answers and nobody will read this but you.
1

Set the bar low

Meaning is usually quieter than it sounds. Before the week starts, name one small moment from the past month that felt worth it for no obvious reason
2

A week of noticing

At the end of each day, one moment that felt worth it — as small as the dog leaning on your leg or a joke that landed
DayA moment that felt worth itWho was thereWhat I was doing
A day when you couldn't find one. What was the day like, and does anything come back now that it's over?
3

What they have in common

Read the seven moments. What do they share — people, making things, being outside, being useful, being still?
What is absent from them. Notice if the things you spend most of your week on don't appear at all
How much any of them cost — in money, in planning, in effort. Notice if the answer is “almost nothing”
What the moments say you care about, in a sentence that starts “It turns out I…”
4

More of it

One moment on the list you could reasonably arrange to happen again next week, and how you'd make room for it
Something to say to a person who was there for one of them — a text, a word at dinner, nothing elaborate
