When a Big Role Ends

Retirement and an emptying house both take away a role you were good at and a structure you never had to think about. This helps you name what is ending, what you'll miss, and what you'll build in its place.

13 fields Life TransitionsStress Basic
Retiring, or watching the last child leave, is often called a happy ending, which makes the flat or lost feeling harder to admit. This is a place to admit it. There are no right feelings here and no timetable. A role that shaped your days for decades does not end in an afternoon, and grief for it can sit alongside relief. Both are information about what mattered, not a sign that something is wrong with you.
1

What is ending

The role that is ending or has ended, named plainly — the job title, “the one who packed the lunches”, “the person people called”
What that role gave you that had nothing to do with money: being needed, a reason to get up, people to talk to, a way to introduce yourself
The parts of it you will honestly not miss, and are allowed not to miss
2

The structure around it

What an ordinary weekday looked like when the role was in full swing — from waking to sleeping, with the fixed points
What a weekday looks like now, or what you expect it to look like. Notice where the gaps are
Which of those gaps feel like freedom, and which feel like falling
3

What you'll miss

The thing you'll miss most, and the last time you had it
The feelings that have shown up that you didn't expect — irritability, envy, relief, guilt about the relief, tears at odd moments
4

What you'll build

Things you did before the role took over, or always meant to do, that could fill an hour of a weekday
One fixed point you'll put into each weekday — a walk, a class, coffee with someone, a shift somewhere — so the day has a spine
Who you'd like more of now, and how you'll ask for it without making it their job to fill the gap
5

This week

One thing from the list above you'll do this week, with the day and the time
A new way to answer “so what do you do?” that you would be comfortable saying out loud
