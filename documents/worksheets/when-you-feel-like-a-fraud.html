When You Feel Like a Fraud

Feeling like you're about to be found out is common in people who are actually doing fine. This helps you catch the moment it happens, open the evidence file you usually skip, and hear what a mentor would say.

13 fields WorkStress Basic
Feeling like a fraud is not proof that you are one. It shows up most in people who care about doing well and who have just been handed something new. Nothing here scores you or settles whether you are good enough. It gathers the facts you skip over when the fear is talking, and gives the fear a fair hearing rather than the last word. There are no right answers.
1

The moment

The most recent time the feeling hit — where you were, who was there, and what was being asked of you
What you did in that moment: went quiet, overexplained, worked twice as long, said yes to something you had no room for
The exact sentence the fear says, in its own words — e.g. “they're going to realize I don't belong here”
2

Being found out

What you imagine happening on the day you're “found out” — who says what, and what happens next
What you believe the people around you have that you don't — training, ease, confidence, a natural gift
Where you first learned that being good at something meant never struggling with it, and who taught you that
3

The evidence file

Things you have actually done in this role or place that a genuine fraud could not have done — be concrete, with dates or names if you can
Feedback you have received and discounted — the compliment you brushed off, the review you decided was just politeness
What you have learned in the last six months that you didn't know before. Not knowing it earlier wasn't fraud; it was the job of learning it
4

What a mentor would say

Picture someone who has watched you work and wants you to do well. Write what they would say about the moment in the first section
The one line from that which you could actually believe on a bad day
5

Next time it shows up

A sentence to say to yourself when the fear speaks next week — short enough to remember in the middle of a meeting
One thing you'll do differently the next time you don't know something: ask, say “I'll find out”, or let the silence sit

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
