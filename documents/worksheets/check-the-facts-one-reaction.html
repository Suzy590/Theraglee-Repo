Checking the Facts Behind One Strong Reaction

A strong reaction often rests partly on what happened and partly on what you assumed. This helps you separate the two for one recent moment and see whether the size of the feeling fits.

12 fields DBTEmotion Regulation Basic
This is not about proving your feeling wrong. Feelings are real whether or not they fit the facts, and a big reaction usually has good reasons behind it, even if they belong to an older moment. The point is only to look. When you separate what actually happened from what you read into it, sometimes the feeling shrinks on its own, and sometimes it turns out to be exactly the right size. Either answer is useful.
1

What happened

The moment, described like a camera would see it — who was there, what was said or done, in what order. No adjectives yet
The feeling that hit, and how big it was on a scale of 1–10
What your body did in the first few seconds — heat, tight chest, tears, a rush to speak, going blank
2

My interpretation

What you decided it meant, in the moment — about them, about you, about what happens next
The worst version of the story your mind told. Write it out in full, even if it sounds harsh on paper
Whose voice that interpretation sounds like, and whether you've felt this exact thing before in a different situation
3

What I know for certain

Only the parts you could prove to a stranger. What did you actually see and hear?
Two other explanations that fit the same facts, including at least one that has nothing to do with you
Something you'd need to know before you could be sure, and whether there's a way to find it out
4

Does the size fit

Given only the facts, how big would the feeling reasonably be (1–10)? Compare it with the number you wrote earlier
If the feeling fits the facts, what does it need from you? If it's bigger than the facts, what might the extra be about?
One thing you'll do or say in the next day based on what you know for certain, not on the story
