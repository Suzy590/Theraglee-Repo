Listening Without Fixing

A practice log across five conversations where your only job is to listen — no advice, no solutions, no “have you tried”. You note what you wanted to say and what happened when you didn't.

9 fields CommunicationRelationships Basic
Most of us listen with a fix half-formed before the other person has finished. This is not a test of your patience and there is no score. It is a small experiment, run over five real conversations, where you try only to understand and notice how hard that is. The urge to fix is not a fault; it usually comes from caring. Here you set it down for a few minutes and see what happens.
1

Before you start

How you usually respond when someone brings you a problem — jump in, reassure, suggest, change the subject, go quiet
What you think would happen if you just listened and said almost nothing. Your honest prediction.
2

The five conversations

One row per conversation. Fill it in as soon afterwards as you can, while the urge to fix is still fresh
Who and about whatWhat I wanted to sayWhat I said insteadHow they respondedHow hard it was (1–10)

What you noticed

The moments the fix was hardest to hold back — and what was going on in you at that point
What people did when you didn't fix — kept talking, went deeper, found their own answer, asked for advice
How it compared with your prediction above, and anything that surprised you
4

Phrases that help

Two or three things you said that kept the door open — “that sounds hard”, “what was that like”, “tell me more”
What you'll say when someone actually wants advice, so you can offer it without it becoming the default
5

Keep going

One person you'd like to listen to this way next week, and what you'll say to yourself when the fix rises
