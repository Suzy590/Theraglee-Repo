If It Didn't Have to Be Perfect

Some things never get started because they might not be done well or admired. This helps you take the audience away, count what waiting has cost, and do a deliberately unimpressive version this week.

10 fields MotivationGoals Basic
This is not about lowering your standards, and it is not a lecture about perfectionism. It is about noticing how many things you might enjoy that you have quietly filed under “only if I'd be good at it”. There is no score here and nobody watching. The only task at the end is to do one small thing badly, on purpose, and see what that is actually like compared with what you feared.
1

The things you don't start

Things you'd like to do but haven't, because you're not sure you'd be good at them — list as many as come, from learning guitar to hosting a dinner
Pick one. Who would you be trying to impress if you did it, and whose judgment are you most afraid of?
2

Take the audience away

Imagine nobody will ever see it, grade it or ask how it's going. Does it still appeal? What changes about it?
The worst version you can picture — the wobbly first attempt, the awful draft. What actually happens in the hour after that?
A thing you already do badly and enjoy anyway — singing in the car, cooking without a recipe. What lets you do that one freely?
3

What perfect costs

What waiting to be good enough has cost you so far — in time, in fun, in things that never happened at all
Where you learned that things were only worth doing if they'd be done well. A person, a classroom, a single comment that stuck
4

Do it badly, on purpose

Design the deliberately unimpressive version — twenty minutes, no special equipment, nobody told beforehand
When this week, and what you'll do with the result — keep it, throw it away, or show one safe person
Afterwards: what it was like to do it, and whether it was as bad as you feared — a line or two is plenty
