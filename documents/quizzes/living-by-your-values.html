What your score suggests

0 – 8

Values out of sight

Your days and your values have come apart, and you may not even be sure what the values are anymore. That happens when life fills up with other people’s needs. Start small: write down one thing you would defend if pushed, and give it fifteen minutes this week.

9 – 12

Drifting from the map

You know what matters to you, and your weeks are only partly built around it. Naming the gap is the useful part. Choose one recurring commitment that belongs to someone else’s values, and see if it can shrink or go.

13 – 16

Mostly in step

Your choices mostly track what you care about, with some comfort-driven detours. That is a good place to be. When a value and comfort clash next, pause for one breath before choosing. The pause is often enough to tip it.

17 – 20

Walking your talk

What you value and how you live match up, and the people around you can see it. That kind of alignment is steadying for everyone near you. Values evolve, so check the list once or twice a year and let it change. Consider talking about yours with someone who is still working theirs out.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
