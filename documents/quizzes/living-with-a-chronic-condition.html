What your score suggests

0 – 8

Fighting your own body

Living with a long-term health issue is already hard, and right now you are also fighting yourself: pushing past limits, refusing help, and speaking harshly when your body says no. Pacing is not surrender. Try planning one rest gap this week, on purpose, before you need it. A therapist who understands long-term health issues can help you make peace with the rest.

9 – 12

Limits under negotiation

You know your limits and you keep bargaining with them, which costs you on the days after. Asking for help is the part to practice. Choose one small request this week, from someone who has offered before, and let them do it without a follow-up apology.

13 – 16

Pacing with care

You mostly work with your body rather than against it, and you ask for help more than you used to. That is real progress. On the hard weeks, when your health seems to become your whole identity, a short note about something you enjoyed that day can widen the frame again.

17 – 20

Living alongside it

You have found a way to live with what you carry: pacing on purpose, asking plainly, and keeping your sense of self bigger than your health. That is hard-earned wisdom. Keep protecting it on the good days, when the temptation to overdo it is strongest, and share it gently with others walking a similar road.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
