What your score suggests

0 – 8

Anger is running the show

Anger seems to be arriving fast, staying long, and leaving damage behind. That is exhausting, and it usually means something underneath is not being heard. You are not a bad person for feeling this way. One small step: for a week, jot down each flare-up and what happened just before it. Talking with a therapist about the pattern can help a lot.

9 – 12

Anger has a loud voice

Anger shows up more than you would like, and it costs you energy and some peace with the people around you. That is a common place to be, especially under stress. Try naming the first body signal you feel, like a tight jaw, and treat that as your cue to pause.

13 – 16

Anger visits, then leaves

Anger comes up, as it does for everyone, but you mostly catch it and let it pass without much fallout. The work now is protecting that margin on hard days. Notice what conditions, like hunger or poor sleep, shorten your fuse, and plan around them.

17 – 20

You keep your footing

Anger rarely gets the better of you, and when it does you recover quickly. That steadiness is something you have built, not luck. Keep an eye on whether you ever swallow anger that deserves a voice, and share what works for you with someone who is struggling.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
