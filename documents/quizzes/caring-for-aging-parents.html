What your score suggests

0 – 8

Nothing left for you

Caring for your parent has swallowed your own needs, your calm and your sense of yourself, and there may be no one to hand any of it to. That is not sustainable, and it is not your fault. Find one respite hour this week, paid or borrowed, and take it without apology. A therapist or a caregiver group can help you carry this.

9 – 12

Carrying it alone

You are doing most of the caregiving, keeping the peace and paying for it after hours. That is more than one person should hold. Write down the three tasks that cost you most and ask someone, anyone, to take one of them.

13 – 16

Holding both of you

You care for your parent and you mostly remember to care for yourself, with some tears and guilt along the way. That is what doing this well looks like. Keep the asks for help coming; they get easier with practice.

17 – 20

Caring with your feet planted

You have found a balance between your parent’s needs and your own, shared the load, and made room for the sadness in it. That is rare. Things can change fast in caregiving, so keep your supports warm. Other caregivers could learn a lot from how you set this up.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
