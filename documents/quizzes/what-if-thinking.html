What your score suggests

0 – 8

Lost in the branches

Your what-ifs are branching fast and taking you far from the question you started with. That is exhausting, and it is a very common pattern, not a flaw in you. Try this: when a what-if arrives, write it down as a single sentence and answer only that one. A therapist can help you build that habit if it feels hard alone.

9 – 12

More branches than answers

Some of your what-if thinking helps you plan, but a good share of it spins without landing anywhere. You are far from alone there. A small experiment: give each what-if a two-minute limit, and at the end ask “Is there an action here?” If not, let it go for now and see whether it comes back.

13 – 16

Mostly a planning tool

Your what-ifs usually go somewhere useful, and you can catch the ones that don't before they run too far. Notice the times of day or the topics where they slip past you. Naming those triggers, even in a quick note, makes the next catch easier.

17 – 20

What-ifs work for you

You use what-if thinking the way it was meant to be used: as a quick check, then a decision. That balance is worth protecting. When life gets heavier, this is often the first thing to tip, so keep an eye on your sleep and how many questions follow you to bed.
