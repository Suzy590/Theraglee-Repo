What your score suggests

0 – 8

Holding on tightly

You're carrying responsibility for a lot that isn't yours to carry, and letting go feels like danger rather than relief. That is exhausting, and it usually comes from having learned, somewhere, that control kept you safe. One small step: choose one small thing this week to let someone else do their way, and notice what actually happens. A therapist can help you loosen the grip with care.

9 – 12

A firm grip

You can adjust when you have to, but it costs you, and you tend to hover over things that would probably be fine without you. That's common, especially for people others rely on. Try sorting a worry into “mine to act on” and “not mine” before you reach for it. The second list is usually longer.

13 – 16

Loosening your hold

You like a plan, but you can bend it, and you mostly let other people do things their own way. That's a healthy balance. Notice where the grip tightens, often around the people you love most. That's a good place to practice one deliberate step back.

17 – 20

Open hands

You hold plans and outcomes loosely and let other people be responsible for their own lanes. That ease is a real gift to the people around you. Keep it by noticing if a stressful stretch starts to pull your hands back toward the wheel, and give yourself grace when it does.
