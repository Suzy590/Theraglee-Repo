What your score suggests

0 – 8

Reactions out of nowhere

Right now your strongest reactions seem to arrive without warning, which leaves you feeling ambushed by your own feelings. That is disorienting, and it is very common before anyone has mapped the pattern. Start a simple log: each time you react hard, note the time, the place, who was there, and how you had slept. A therapist can help you read the map.

9 – 12

Some patterns coming into view

You can see a few of your triggers, mostly in hindsight, but they still catch you more than you would like. That is normal. Try naming one trigger you know well and deciding, in advance, one thing you will do differently next time it shows up.

13 – 16

You mostly see them coming

You know most of what sets you off and often prepare for it. That awareness is doing a lot of quiet work for you. Look for the trigger you still tend to miss, often the subtle one like fatigue or feeling dismissed, and add it to your list.

17 – 20

Well mapped, well handled

You know your triggers, you plan around them, and you rarely get blindsided. That is a mature, hard-won skill. Keep it fresh, since triggers shift with life changes, and be gentle with people who have not yet mapped theirs.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
