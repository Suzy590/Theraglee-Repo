What your score suggests

0 – 8

The floodgates open early

You seem to share a lot, fast, and then carry the regret afterward. That often comes from nerves or a hope to be known quickly, not from anything wrong with you. Try one small experiment: in your next new conversation, ask two questions for every one thing you reveal. A therapist can help if the regret has turned to shame.

9 – 12

A lot, a little soon

You open up readily, sometimes further than you meant to, and you catch it a beat late. Many warm people are like this. Practice a short pause before the personal stuff. You can even say “I’ll tell you about that another time,” and see how it feels to keep something for later.

13 – 16

Open with a gauge

You share generously and mostly at the right pace, with an occasional slip when nerves take over. That is a good place to be. Notice the settings where the slip tends to happen, often when nerves or tiredness take over, and jot them down so they stop surprising you.

17 – 20

You share on your terms

You know what to share, with whom, and when, and you read the room while you do it. That builds trust fast. Keep it by making sure you still let people in, because holding back can also become a habit. If someone you know overshares from nerves, gentle questions can help them slow down.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
