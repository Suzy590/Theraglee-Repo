What your score suggests

0 – 8

Feelings without names yet

Right now emotions tend to arrive as a vague fog or a knot in your body rather than something you can name. That is more common than people admit, and it can be learned. Start with a simple feelings list and, once a day, pick one word that fits. A therapist can also help you build this vocabulary.

9 – 12

A few words, most days

You can find a name for what you feel some of the time, but often it stays at “fine” or “stressed.” That leaves a lot unsaid. Try a two-minute evening check: what was the strongest feeling today, and where did you feel it in your body?

13 – 16

You know most of it

You can usually put a specific word to your emotions and trace where they came from. That is a real skill. Keep stretching it by noticing the quieter feelings, like tenderness or envy, that hide behind the loud ones.

17 – 20

Fluent in your own weather

You name your feelings with ease and precision, which makes them far easier to manage and share. Keep that going by staying curious rather than certain, since even fluent people misread themselves sometimes. Offering a word to someone who is struggling to find theirs can be a real kindness.
