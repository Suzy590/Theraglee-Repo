What your score suggests

0 – 8

Feelings kept at the door

Right now difficult emotions get held off almost before they arrive, and that takes constant effort. It is a smart strategy that probably kept you safe once. Try letting one small feeling stay for sixty seconds, just noticing it, before doing anything. A therapist can help you do this slowly and safely.

9 – 12

Avoiding more than you would like

You face some feelings and step around others, and the ones you avoid tend to come back. That is a very human pattern. Once a day, ask yourself what you are not feeling right now, and write down whatever answers. Nothing else is required.

13 – 16

Facing most of it

You mostly let feelings be felt, with the occasional detour through distraction or humor. That is healthy balance. Notice which feeling you still tend to duck, whether it is grief, anger or shame, and give it a little more room.

17 – 20

Open to what shows up

You tend to meet feelings as they come, even the unwelcome ones, and let them pass through. That is a real strength. Keep it by remembering that avoidance is sometimes wise too, on days when you have no capacity, and by extending patience to people who are still learning this.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
