What your score suggests

0 – 8

Money is everywhere

Financial worry has spread into your sleep, your body and your relationships, and avoiding the numbers has become the only way to cope. That is a very human response to fear. This week, look at just one account for two minutes with someone you trust nearby. A nonprofit credit counselor or a therapist can help, and both are more affordable than people expect.

9 – 12

The numbers weigh on you

You are keeping up, but money worry costs you sleep and sharp words at home. Try a short weekly money check-in at a set time, so the worry has a place to go instead of everywhere.

13 – 16

Aware, and mostly steady

You know your numbers and you deal with them, with some nerves when a big cost lands. That is a healthy relationship with money stress. Keep your check-ins regular so surprises stay small.

17 – 20

On top of it

You face your finances directly, talk about them without a fight and sleep through most nights. That is a form of stability many people are still building. Offer a calm ear to someone who avoids their statements.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
