What your score suggests

0 – 8

Lost and running low

You want to be there for them and are not sure how, and you are carrying your own feelings with no one to help. That is a lonely spot, and it says nothing bad about you. Send one short message this week that expects nothing back. If it ever feels like too much, for you or for them, in the US anyone can call or text 988 at any time.

9 – 12

Trying, unsure of the way

You are showing up in a way that costs you and often feels wrong, and you are watching for a timeline that grief does not keep. Grief has no schedule and no right way. Try replacing “how are you” with something specific, such as offering a meal or asking about the person they lost.

13 – 16

Present and steady

You stay when it is uncomfortable, you keep checking in, and you are starting to look after yourself too. That is exactly what a grieving person needs. Put the hard dates in your calendar, and let yourself have your own support so you can keep this up.

17 – 20

A safe person to grieve with

You let them be sad without fixing it, you show up long after others have drifted off, and you have your own support in place. That is the rarest kind of friend. Keep going, and keep the check-ins coming for as long as it takes. Sharing what you have learned may help someone else who wants to show up better.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
