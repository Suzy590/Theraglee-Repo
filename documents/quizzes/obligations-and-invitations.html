What your score suggests

0 – 8

Obligation runs the calendar

Your social life seems to be made of things you did not choose, and the only exits you can picture are illness or a cancellation. That is exhausting, and it often comes from an old rule that showing up is love. Try declining one thing this month, warmly and early, and see what really happens. A therapist can help if the guilt is loud.

9 – 12

Duty wins more than it should

You can say no to an invitation, but it takes agonizing, and a fair amount of your calendar is still duty. Many people live here. Try one simple rule: reply within a day, and let a no be two sentences. The longer a maybe sits, the more it turns into a reluctant yes.

13 – 16

Mostly chosen

Most of what you show up for, you chose, and you can bow out of things that have stopped working. That is a good balance. Notice the recurring event or the person whose invitations still feel like a summons, and decide whether that is a limit you want to set.

17 – 20

You go because you want to

You accept what you want, decline what you don’t, and stay warm while you do it. That keeps your yes meaningful. Keep it by making sure your no is not becoming a default either, since some of the best evenings started as reluctant ones. A friend drowning in obligations might need to hear how you decide.
