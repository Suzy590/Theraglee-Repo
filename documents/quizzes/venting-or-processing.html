What your score suggests

0 – 8

Venting on repeat

Talking about what upsets you seems to stir things up rather than settle them, and the story may be running on a loop. That is common, and it is not your fault; venting feels like relief right up until it does not. Try telling the story once, then writing down one sentence about what you actually need. A therapist can help you turn talk into movement.

9 – 12

Letting off steam, mostly

You get some relief from talking things through, but you often end up back where you started. That is a normal place to be. Next time you vent, add one question at the end: “what would help me feel done with this?”

13 – 16

Talking your way to clearer

You mostly use conversation to make sense of upsets, not just to release them, and you can tell the difference. That is a genuinely useful skill. Keep it by noticing which friends help you process and which ones mainly help you stay angry.

17 – 20

Talk that moves you forward

You process feelings out loud with clarity, own your part, and know what you need from a listener. That is rare and valuable. Stay open to plain venting now and then, since sometimes a good rant is exactly right, and offer the same kind of listening to others.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
