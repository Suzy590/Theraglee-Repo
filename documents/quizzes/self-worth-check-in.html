What your score suggests

0 – 8

Worth on the line each day

Your sense of worth seems to depend heavily on results and reactions, and it may sit low even on quiet days. That is a heavy way to live, and it often traces back to old lessons rather than the truth. Each night, try writing one thing you did that reflected your values, not your output. A therapist can help too.

9 – 12

Worth tied to the scoreboard

Your worth holds up on good days and slips on hard ones. You are not alone in that; most of us learned to grade ourselves by results. Try noticing one moment a day when you treated yourself as worthwhile before you had done anything to earn it.

13 – 16

Steady with a few wobbles

Most of the time your value feels like a given, and setbacks bruise it without cracking it. That is a solid base. Pay attention to the one or two situations that still shake it, and give those moments a kind sentence you can reach for.

17 – 20

Worth that holds

You seem to carry a quiet, durable sense that you matter, independent of the day’s results. Keep it honest by noticing when you dismiss praise or criticism too quickly. And consider telling someone younger how you got here; it is a rare thing to learn by example.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
