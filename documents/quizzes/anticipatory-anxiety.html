What your score suggests

0 – 8

Dread is arriving early

The waiting is costing you more than the events themselves, and there's almost always something on the horizon. That is a heavy way to spend a month, and many people live this way without naming it. One small step: for the next thing you're dreading, write your prediction beforehand and what actually happened afterward. A therapist can help you use what you find.

9 – 12

The lead-up costs you

You get through the things you dread, but the days before them are shadowed and the nights are short. That is a common pattern. Try giving the dread a fixed appointment, ten minutes in the evening to think it over, and gently returning to the present when it shows up outside that slot.

13 – 16

A little nervous, then fine

You feel some dread ahead of events, but it stays in proportion and mostly shows up the day before. That's healthy nerves doing their job. Notice which kinds of events pull the dread forward, and try preparing those a little earlier so the waiting has less to feed on.

17 – 20

You wait lightly

You prepare, set it aside, and sleep. Events arrive without weeks of dread ahead of them. That ease is worth protecting, since it tends to fade first when life gets crowded. If you notice a knot in your stomach starting to arrive earlier than usual, take it as a signal to slow down.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
