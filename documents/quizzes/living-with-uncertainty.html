What your score suggests

0 – 8

Not knowing is unbearable

Uncertainty seems to take over your whole attention right now, and that is a heavy way to live. Many people feel this, especially after a stretch of hard surprises. A small step: pick one unresolved thing and write down what you can do about it today and what you can't. Put the second list away. A therapist can help you build more ease with the waiting.

9 – 12

Waiting wears you down

You can get through not knowing, but it drains you and pulls you into checking, researching, and rehearsing. That is a normal response to an uncomfortable state. Try setting one fixed time a day to check for news or think it over, and practice letting the rest of the day belong to other things.

13 – 16

You can sit with it

You handle open questions better than most, keeping your routines going even when something is unresolved. The moments it gets to you are worth noticing: which kinds of unknowns hit hardest? Jotting that down after the next one can show you where your steadiness has an edge.

17 – 20

At ease in the open

You can live alongside an open question without it swallowing your days. That is a real strength, and one that people around you probably lean on. Keep it by noticing what helps you stay steady, and share it: a friend in the middle of a long wait might be glad to hear how you do it.
