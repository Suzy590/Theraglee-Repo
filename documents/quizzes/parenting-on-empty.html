What your score suggests

0 – 8

Running on fumes

You are giving your kids everything and there is nothing left over, and it sounds like you’re carrying that alone. That is exhaustion, not a failure of love. The next step is small and practical: name one hour this week that someone else covers, and say out loud to one person that you are not okay. A therapist can help you find your footing.

9 – 12

Stretched very thin

You are getting through, but patience is short and the guilt afterward is heavy. Most parents recognize this season. Try a five-minute reset before the evening rush, in the car or the bathroom, just breathing and no phone. It’s not a fix, but it lowers the temperature.

13 – 16

Tired but still yourself

You have some fuel in the tank, and you’re managing the rough moments with repair. That matters more than being perfect. Protect the time that is yours, and let one person know what they could take off your plate before you actually need it.

17 – 20

Full enough to give

You keep something for yourself and you accept help, and your kids get the benefit of that. That balance is not luck; you built it. Keep it by noticing the first signs of the tank running low, and by offering a fellow parent an hour of the kind you value.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
