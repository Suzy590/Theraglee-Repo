What your score suggests

0 – 8

Toolkit nearly empty

Getting through hard moments seems to depend on luck or on things that cost you later. That is exhausting, and it is not a character flaw; nobody handed you the tools. Pick one small, gentle thing, like a five-minute walk or a text to someone kind, and practice it on an ordinary day so it is ready when you need it. A therapist can help you build the rest.

9 – 12

A few tools, some worn out

You have some ways to cope, but a couple of them may be leaving you worse off, and there is not much backup. That is a very normal place to be. Try writing a short list of what helps and what only feels like it helps, then add one new item to the first column.

13 – 16

A solid, working set

You have several ways to get through a hard patch and mostly choose them on purpose. That is real skill. Keep an eye on the one habit that sneaks in when you are tired, and make sure your best tools are easy to reach on your worst days.

17 – 20

Well stocked and flexible

You have a range of ways to cope, you match them to the moment, and they leave you better off afterward. That takes practice and self-knowledge. Stay curious about whether any tool is becoming automatic rather than chosen, and consider sharing what works with someone who is struggling.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
