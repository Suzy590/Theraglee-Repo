What your score suggests

0 – 8

Strengths out of sight

It sounds as if your strengths are almost invisible to you, while your weaknesses are in sharp focus. That is not accuracy; it is a habit of attention, and it can be retrained. Ask three people who know you well to name one thing you do well. Write the answers down and keep them somewhere you will see them.

9 – 12

Strengths in soft focus

You know you have strengths, but they feel vague or borrowed compared with your very clear list of flaws. This week, each time you do something well, write a single line about it. In a month you will have evidence that argues back.

13 – 16

A fair picture, mostly

You can name what you are good at and you mostly use it, with a little reluctance to say so out loud. Try claiming one strength by name the next time a task in that area comes up. It helps others know where to lean on you.

17 – 20

Strengths seen and used

You seem to know your strengths clearly, believe others when they see them, and step into them without fuss. That kind of clarity helps everyone around you. Keep it honest by staying curious about your growth edges too, and help someone who only sees their flaws find one strength.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
