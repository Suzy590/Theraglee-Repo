What your score suggests

0 – 8

Avoidance is running things

Avoiding has become your main way of coping, and your world may feel like it's shrinking. That is a hard place to be and a common one, because avoidance works so well in the short term. One small step: choose the least scary thing on your list and do only the first minute of it. A therapist can help you take the next steps at a safe pace.

9 – 12

The list is growing

You face plenty, but the pile of things you've put off is getting noticeable, and each one costs a little peace. That is a familiar pattern for many people. Try writing your avoided list down and picking one item a week to tackle, starting with the easiest. Watching the list shrink is its own relief.

13 – 16

You face most things

You put things off now and then, like everyone, but you tend to circle back and get them done. Pay attention to which kinds of tasks or situations you stall on, since they usually share a theme. Naming that theme can make the next one easier to face.

17 – 20

You lean in

You tend to walk toward the things that make you nervous rather than around them, and you're probably better for it. Keep an eye on the balance, though: leaning in is a strength, not an obligation. Rest counts too, and you're allowed to let some things go without it being avoidance.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
