What your score suggests

0 – 8

Urges are in charge

Right now the space between an urge and an action is very small, and that is exhausting to live with. It does not mean you lack willpower; it means the muscle has not had a fair chance to grow. Try one thing: when an urge hits, hold something cold or count ten slow breaths before deciding. A therapist who knows DBT can help you practice this.

9 – 12

Surfing some, wiping out on others

You can ride out some urges but give in to others, and the losses add up. That is where most people start. Pick one urge you would most like to wait out, and time it next time; most peak and fade in under fifteen minutes. Seeing that on a clock helps.

13 – 16

Riding most waves

You usually notice an urge, let it rise, and choose what to do. That is real skill, not luck. Notice which conditions, like tiredness or loneliness, shrink your gap, and keep your favorite “wait it out” tool close on those days.

17 – 20

Waves come and go

You seem to treat urges as passing weather rather than orders, and you have a few reliable ways to wait them out. That is a well-built skill. Keep it sharp by staying honest about the rare urge that still wins, and share the wave idea with someone who might need it.
