What your score suggests

0 – 8

Your voice stays inside

Your ideas and preferences seem to live mostly in your head, and the groups around you rarely hear them. That is a loss for them as much as for you, and it often comes from an old fear rather than a lack of things to say. Try one sentence per meeting this week, even just “I agree with that.” A therapist can help if the fear is intense.

9 – 12

Speaking up costs you

You do speak up, but it takes effort, and you leave many gatherings with something unsaid. Many thoughtful people know this. Try preparing one point before you walk into a group, so you are not searching for it while your heart races. Having it ready makes the gap easier to take.

13 – 16

Heard, with some effort

Your voice usually makes it into the room, and the people you spend time with know where you stand. Keep noticing the groups where you still go quiet, because there is often a specific person or dynamic behind it. A quick note afterward can show you what it is.

17 – 20

You take your turn

You speak up easily, recover when talked over, and stay open to the group going another way. That is the right mix. Keep it by watching for anyone who has gone quiet, and making a little room for them. People who find groups easy are the best at doing that.
