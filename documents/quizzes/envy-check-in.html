What your score suggests

0 – 8

Comparison is running the day

Envy and comparison are taking a real toll on your mood and maybe on your relationships. That is painful, and it is not a sign of a bad heart; envy usually points to something you want and do not yet have. Try naming, on paper, one thing envy is asking for, without judging it. If it has been dragging you down for a while, a therapist can help.

9 – 12

Envy with the volume up

Comparison shows up often enough to color your days, and it tends to stay hidden and sour. That is common in a world built for comparing. Try a small experiment: for one week, mute or skip the feeds that leave you feeling smaller, and see what shifts.

13 – 16

Envy passes through

You feel envy like anyone, but you generally let it pass or use it to learn something about what you want. That is a healthy relationship with a hard feeling. Keep noticing the one comparison that still bites, since it often marks a goal you have not yet named.

17 – 20

Glad for others, clear on yourself

You can celebrate other people’s wins, own the occasional twinge, and turn it into information rather than misery. That is a mature and generous stance. Stay honest about envy on low days, when it visits everyone, and share your openness with someone who is quietly struggling with it.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
