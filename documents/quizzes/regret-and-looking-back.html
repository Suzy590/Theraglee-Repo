What your score suggests

0 – 8

Regret is running the show

Looking back has become a loop that eats your present and turns every old choice into evidence against you. That is a painful way to live, and it is common. Try this: write the regret as if a friend had done it, and answer them. Talking with a therapist about self-blame can loosen it further.

9 – 12

Looking back too long

Regret gets a lot of your attention, especially late at night, and the lesson has not yet turned into much relief. Pick one regret and write down what you would do differently now. Then do one small thing that proves it, this week.

13 – 16

Learning from it

You look back with some sting and mostly pull something useful from it. That is what a healthy relationship with regret looks like. When the apologizing creeps in as you tell a story, notice it and try telling the story once without it.

17 – 20

At peace with the past

You can hold your past choices with kindness and a bit of humor, and you have turned regret into something you actually live by. That is rare and worth protecting. Keep an eye on new regrets forming, and offer your younger-self tone to anyone around you who is stuck in theirs.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
