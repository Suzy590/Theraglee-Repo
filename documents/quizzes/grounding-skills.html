What your score suggests

0 – 8

Nothing to hold onto yet

When anxiety rises, you don't yet have a reliable way back to the present, and grounding may feel pointless from inside the fear. That's an honest place to start, and many people begin here. One small step: practice naming five things you can see, right now, while you're calm. Tools learned when calm are the ones that work under pressure. A therapist can help you find yours.

9 – 12

Tools you don't yet trust

You know a grounding technique or two, but you forget them in the moment or they feel like going through the motions. That's very common. Try pairing one tool with a daily cue, like your first coffee or a red light, so it becomes automatic before you need it.

13 – 16

A toolkit that works

You have grounding tools you trust, and they usually bring you back. The times they don't are worth a look: were you too far in before you reached for one? Practicing the tool a little earlier, at the first flicker rather than the full wave, can make it work more often.

17 – 20

Grounded by habit

Grounding has become an ordinary reflex for you, and it works. That's a real skill built from practice. Keep it fresh by trying a new tool now and then, and consider teaching the one you use most to someone who's struggling. Explaining it tends to deepen it.
