What your score suggests

0 – 8

The well is dry

Caring has used up what you had, and numbness, irritation or a heavy view of the world have moved in. This is what happens to helpers who are not being helped, and it is not a failure of heart. This week, let one person look after you, even in a small way. A therapist who understands caregivers can help you refill.

9 – 12

Giving from the reserves

You still care, but it costs more than it used to and other people’s pain follows you home. Try a short end-of-day habit of naming what you are setting down before you walk away from your role.

13 – 16

Caring, with a floor under you

You give a lot, feel it, and have enough support and boundaries to stay yourself. That balance is what makes you sustainable. Keep the people who hold you close, especially during heavy seasons.

17 – 20

A full well

You care deeply, stay steady while you do it and let others care for you in return. That is exactly how it is supposed to work. Offer that same steadiness to a helper who looks worn.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
