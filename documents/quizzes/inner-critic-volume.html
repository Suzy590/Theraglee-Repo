What your score suggests

0 – 8

The critic has the mic

Right now the harsh voice is loud, frequent, and believed. That is exhausting, and it is very common in people who care about doing well. Try writing down one critical thought a day beside what you would say to a friend in the same spot. If the volume stays this high, a therapist can help you turn it down.

9 – 12

Loud, but you argue back

The critic gets plenty of airtime, and it still stings, but you are not fully buying what it sells. That gap is where change happens. This week, give the voice a name or a silly accent when it starts. It is easier to question a character than a fact.

13 – 16

Background hum

The critic still shows up, usually around mistakes or comparisons, but it passes without running your day. You already question it more than you believe it. Notice which situations turn it up, and plan one kind sentence for those moments in advance.

17 – 20

You set the volume

You hear the critical voice as a thought, not a verdict, and you answer it with something steadier. Keep that going by noticing when stress or tiredness sneaks the volume back up. Sharing how you talk to yourself can quietly give a friend permission to do the same.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
