What your score suggests

0 – 8

Carrying it alone

Asking for help seems to feel like exposing a failure, so you carry almost everything yourself. That is lonely and heavier than it needs to be. Try one tiny ask this week, the kind you would happily grant someone else, and watch what actually happens. A therapist can help if the weight of it has been growing.

9 – 12

Asking as a last resort

You do ask for help, but only after you have exhausted every other option and only from a very short list. Many capable people live here. Try asking one step earlier than feels natural. If you would normally wait a week, ask on day three, and see how it lands.

13 – 16

You can ask, with a pause

Asking comes to you with a little hesitation, but you get there, and the people around you mostly know what you are carrying. That is a good place. Notice which kinds of help are still hard to ask for, practical or emotional, and journal about the difference when it shows up.

17 – 20

Help flows both ways

You ask for help about as easily as you give it, and you treat needing a hand as ordinary. That keeps your relationships honest and balanced. Keep it by receiving well, without rushing to repay. And if you know someone who never asks, an unprompted offer might be the door they need.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
