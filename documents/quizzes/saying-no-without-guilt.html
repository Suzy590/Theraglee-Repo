What your score suggests

0 – 8

Guilt does the talking

No costs you so much that you often skip it, or pay for it in guilt for days. That weight is learned, and it can be unlearned. Try a single sentence this week: “I can’t do that, but thank you for asking.” Say it once, and notice that the world keeps turning. A therapist can help if the guilt feels bigger than the moment.

9 – 12

No comes with a bill

You can decline, but the aftermath is heavy: replaying, over-explaining, wondering if they mind. Most people who care about others know this feeling. Try cutting your reasons in half next time. A shorter no is often kinder to both of you, and it gives the guilt less to feed on.

13 – 16

A no you can live with

You say no when you need to, and the guilt is a passing visitor rather than a resident. That is a sturdy place. Watch for the people or topics that still make it hard, and jot them down when they show up. Patterns tend to appear within a few weeks.

17 – 20

No is just a word

You decline without drama and let the other person own their reaction. Keep that steady by staying warm in your no, so it never hardens into coldness. And if a friend struggles here, tell them what it looks like from the inside. It helps to know it is possible.
