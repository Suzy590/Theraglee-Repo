What your score suggests

0 – 8

Drowning in the extended family

The wider family seems to take up a lot of room in your life, and it sounds like you feel alone in handling it. That is a heavy spot, and a common one. The first move is usually with your partner, not the relatives: ask for twenty minutes to say how it feels for you, without solving anything yet. A couples counselor can help if that talk goes nowhere.

9 – 12

Gritting your teeth

You manage the relatives, but it costs you, and the resentment tends to build between visits. Lots of people are here. Try agreeing with your partner on one small, specific boundary before the next visit, like a fixed leaving time, and hold it together.

13 – 16

Managing it, mostly together

You and your partner handle extended family as a team most of the time, and the visits are more workable than not. Watch for the one or two recurring sore spots and name them to each other in a calm week, not the week of the visit.

17 – 20

Warm and clear

You take what’s good from the wider family and let the rest go, and you and your partner stand together. That is a lot of emotional skill. Share the way you handle a pushy relative with a friend who’s still stuck, and keep checking in when circumstances change.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
