What your score suggests

0 – 8

Worry is steering

Right now worry is doing a lot of the driving, and that is tiring in a way other people may not see. Many people live like this for years before they name it. One small step: pick a fifteen-minute window today, write the worries down, and close the notebook. A therapist can help you loosen the grip further.

9 – 12

Worry has a seat

Worry is a regular passenger in your day. It doesn't run everything, but it costs you real energy and moments you'd rather keep. That's common, and it's workable. Try noticing which time of day worry is loudest and giving it a short, scheduled slot then, so it doesn't spread across the whole day.

13 – 16

You mostly hold the wheel

Worry shows up, but you tend to notice it and move on, which is a real skill. The moments it lingers are worth a second look: what was different about those days? A short evening note on what worried you and what actually happened can sharpen that noticing.

17 – 20

Worry rides in the back

Worry has little say over your day. You seem to let thoughts pass without following them, and you stay present with the people around you. Keep it going by protecting the routines that make that possible, and notice early if a stretch of stress starts to shift the balance.
