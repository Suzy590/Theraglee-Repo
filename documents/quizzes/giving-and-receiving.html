What your score suggests

0 – 8

Always holding the tray

You seem to give constantly and let almost nothing in, and being cared for feels closer to danger than to comfort. That often started as a way to stay safe or useful. Next time someone offers, try saying only “thank you” and stopping there. Let the urge to refuse be loud without obeying it. A therapist can help you understand where that comes from.

9 – 12

Giver by default

You give more than you receive, and being helped leaves you a little itchy until you have evened the score. Many kind people are wired this way. Try accepting one offer this week without planning to repay it, and sit with the awkwardness. It fades faster than you expect.

13 – 16

The flow is mostly even

You give freely and can let care in, with a small tug to reciprocate that mostly does not run the show. That is a healthy exchange. Watch for the seasons when you slip back into all-giving, often when you are stressed, and let someone know you could use a hand.

17 – 20

Care goes both ways

You give without keeping score and receive without flinching, which makes you easy to love. Keep it by staying alert to people who only ever give, because they often need permission to be on the other end. Your example, and an unprompted kindness, can be that permission.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
