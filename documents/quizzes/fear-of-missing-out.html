What your score suggests

0 – 8

Always somewhere else

The sense that everyone else is living more fully is loud right now, and it's driving your phone, your plans, and how you see your own life. That's a lonely feeling, and a very common one. One small step: for one evening this week, put the phone in another room and do something you actually enjoy, then notice how it felt. A therapist can help you quiet the comparison.

9 – 12

Half here, half elsewhere

You enjoy your life, but a steady glance at other people's keeps pulling you out of it, and you say yes to things you'd rather skip. Many people know this well. Try a small rule, like no scrolling in the first hour of the day, and notice whether your own life starts to feel a little bigger.

13 – 16

Mostly content with here

You feel the occasional sting of missing out, but you can let it pass and choose the evening you actually want. That's healthy. Notice what tends to trigger the sting, whether it's a certain app or a certain friend's feed, and consider whether that one thing deserves a smaller place in your day.

17 – 20

Happy where you are

You like your life, choose your plans freely, and treat other people's highlights as just that. That contentment is worth protecting, and worth sharing. If a friend seems caught in the comparison, an honest word about how ordinary your own evenings are can be more helpful than you'd think.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
