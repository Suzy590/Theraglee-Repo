What your score suggests

0 – 8

Frozen at the fork

Decisions, even small ones, are bringing up a lot of fear right now, and the ones you've put off are starting to pile up. That's a hard place to be, and a common one. One small step: pick the smallest decision on your list, give yourself five minutes, and make it. Notice that the world holds. A therapist can help you build from there.

9 – 12

Second-guessing every turn

You do decide, eventually, but it takes weeks of lists and the decision never quite closes. Many thoughtful people know this well. Try writing down, before you decide, what “good enough” would look like. When an option meets it, choose it, and write the date. That date is your permission to stop reopening it.

13 – 16

Choosing with some wobble

You make decisions in reasonable time and mostly let them stand, with the occasional late-night second guess. That's a healthy pattern. Notice which decisions bring the wobble, since it's usually the ones where someone else's reaction is in play. Those are worth a little extra self-kindness.

17 – 20

Deciding, then living

You gather what you need, choose, and move on, trusting that you'll adjust if you have to. That's an unusually calm relationship with choice. Keep it by remembering that some decisions deserve more time than others, and by noticing if a big life change starts to shake your usual ease.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
