What your score suggests

0 – 8

Caught in the loop

Sleep and mood have been pulling each other down, and it is hard to tell which one started it. That loop is well known and it is not your fault. Try one small change on the sleep side, such as a consistent wake time, since sleep is often the easier end to move. If the low mood lingers regardless of sleep, talking with a therapist can help.

9 – 12

Sleep sets the tone

A bad night tends to turn into a bad day, and you often only spot the link afterward. Try writing a single word for your mood and a number for your hours next to each other each morning for a week. The pattern is easier to trust once you can see it.

13 – 16

You spot the pattern

You know your mood dips after short sleep, and you make some allowance for it. Keep sharpening that: on a low-sleep day, name it out loud to yourself before you react to anything, and notice what changes.

17 – 20

Weathering the short nights

Short sleep does not knock your mood far off course, and when it does, you see it clearly and adjust. That is real resilience. Keep it by protecting sleep in the seasons when it is easiest to sacrifice, and by going gently with those around you who are still learning the link.
