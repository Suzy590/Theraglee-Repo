What your score suggests

0 – 8

Fear is arriving fast

Sudden waves of fear are hitting hard and often, and the dread of the next one is taking up your days. That is frightening, very common, and it responds well to help. A therapist who works with panic can show you what those surges are and how to ride them out. If it ever feels like too much, in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.

9 – 12

Waves that knock you over

The surges don't come constantly, but when they do they take a lot out of you, and part of you is always watching for the next one. That watchfulness is normal and it can ease. One small step: practice a slow out-breath for a minute each day when you're calm, so it's ready when you need it.

13 – 16

You know how to ride it

Fear surges rarely, and when it does you have ways to get through it without letting it change your plans. That's real skill. Notice what tends to come before a surge, whether it's poor sleep, caffeine, or a stressful week, and see whether you can soften one of those things ahead of time.

17 – 20

Steady in the surge

Sudden fear has little hold on you. You read a pounding heart as stress and let it pass. That steadiness is worth keeping, and worth sharing: if someone you know struggles with panic, hearing how you talk yourself through a surge could be a real help to them.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
