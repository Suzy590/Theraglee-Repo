What your score suggests

0 – 8

Shame is calling the shots

Shame seems to be a frequent visitor, and it may have convinced you that you are the problem rather than something you did. That is a painful place, and it is also a very human one. Shame shrinks when it is spoken. Consider telling one safe person one small thing. A therapist is a good first safe person.

9 – 12

Shame lingers a while

Shame lands hard and hangs around, and part of you still believes its verdict. You are not stuck, though; you are already questioning it. This week, when it shows up, try naming it out loud, even alone: “This is shame.” Naming it makes it smaller and less certain.

13 – 16

Shame passes through

Shame visits, as it does for everyone, but it mostly stays about what you did rather than who you are, and it fades. Notice the one or two things you still keep hidden, and ask whether they might be lighter shared with a trusted person.

17 – 20

Shame doesn’t get the last word

You feel shame like anyone, and then you treat yourself the way you would treat a friend. That is hard-won. Keep it by staying honest about your slips rather than skipping past them. And when someone confides in you, remember your own story makes you a safe listener.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
