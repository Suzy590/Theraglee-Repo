What your score suggests

0 – 8

Worst case feels certain

Your mind is jumping to the worst ending fast and staying there, and arguing with it doesn't seem to work. That is exhausting, and it's one of the most common thinking habits there is. One small step: when a worst case lands, write down the best case and the most likely case next to it, even if you don't believe them yet. A therapist can help you build that muscle.

9 – 12

The leap comes easy

You jump ahead often, and it takes real effort to come back. You usually manage it, but not before it has cost you an afternoon. That's familiar to many people. Try keeping a short record of predictions and outcomes for two weeks. Seeing the gap on paper is oddly reassuring.

13 – 16

You catch the leap

Your thoughts sometimes leap to the worst, but you notice and bring them back to size fairly quickly. That's a skill worth keeping sharp. Notice which areas of life the leaps cluster in, whether health, work, or relationships. That's where a little extra practice pays off.

17 – 20

Sized about right

You tend to see setbacks at their actual size and rarely spin them into disasters. That steadiness is real, and it likely calms the people around you too. Keep an eye on it during high-stress stretches, when even steady minds can start reaching for the worst case.
