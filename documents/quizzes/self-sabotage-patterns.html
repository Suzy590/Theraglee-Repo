What your score suggests

0 – 8

Pulling the rug from under you

It sounds as if part of you steps in to end good things before they can be lost or before you feel found out. That is not laziness; it is usually old protection that has outlived its use. Try noticing the exact moment the urge to bail arrives, and write down what you were feeling just before it. A therapist can help you trace it.

9 – 12

A hand near the switch

You see the pattern, at least in hindsight, and near the finish line something in you eases off. Try telling one person your goal and your usual escape route. Being seen makes it much harder to slip out the side door quietly.

13 – 16

Mostly out of your own way

You wobble when things get good but you usually push through, and you learn from the times you didn’t. Notice which kinds of success make you most uneasy, and plan one small steadying habit for that stretch, like a check-in with a friend.

17 – 20

Clear of your own way

You seem able to let good things stay good and to keep going when a goal is close. That is steadier than it looks from the inside. Keep it by staying honest about the rare times you do flinch, and share what you have learned with someone who bails at the finish line.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
