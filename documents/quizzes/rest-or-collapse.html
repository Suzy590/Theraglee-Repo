What your score suggests

0 – 8

Only the wall stops you

Rest has become something that happens to you, usually as a crash, rather than something you choose. That pattern is common among people who care a lot and carry a lot. Try scheduling one fifteen-minute pause a day that has no purpose, and treat it like an appointment. If stopping feels impossible, that is worth talking through with a therapist.

9 – 12

Rest comes with guilt

You do rest, though it tends to arrive late and with a side of guilt. The guilt is the part to work on. Notice the thought that shows up when you sit down, and write it out once so you can see how harsh it sounds on paper.

13 – 16

Pausing on purpose, mostly

You choose rest more often than it chooses you, and your body thanks you for it. Keep an eye on the busy seasons when the pauses quietly disappear, and decide in advance what the smallest version of rest looks like for those weeks.

17 – 20

Rest is part of the plan

You stop before you are empty, and you treat rest as fuel rather than a reward. That is a hard-won balance. Keep it, and if someone near you is heading for the wall, modeling this quietly may do more than any advice.
