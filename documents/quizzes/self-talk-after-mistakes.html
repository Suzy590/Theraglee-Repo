What your score suggests

0 – 8

Every slip becomes a verdict

After a mistake, your self-talk seems to turn a single error into a statement about who you are, and it can echo for days. That is painful and common, and it is learnable to change. Try writing the harsh sentence down, then rewriting it as a specific fact: what happened, and what you will do differently. A therapist can help with this if it feels stuck.

9 – 12

Harder on yourself than needed

Mistakes hit you with more force than they warrant, and the replay runs longer than it helps. You still get back up, which matters. This week, when you catch a global word like “always” or “useless,” swap it for one specific detail of what actually happened.

13 – 16

Blunt but back on your feet

You are honest with yourself about errors, and you recover without much drama. The tone can be a little sharp. Try adding one sentence of credit for what you did handle well; it keeps the honesty and softens the edge.

17 – 20

Mistakes stay the right size

You treat your errors as information rather than evidence against you, and you move on quickly to fixing them. That is a genuine skill. Keep it by noticing which mistakes still sneak past your usual fairness, and let the people around you hear how you talk yourself through a slip.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
