What your score suggests

0 – 8

Carrying everyone's safety

Your worry for the people you love runs day and night, and it may be pushing them to hide things from you, which only feeds it. That is a painful loop, and it comes from love. One small step: the next time you want to check on someone, wait fifteen minutes and do something with your hands first. A therapist can help you set the weight down.

9 – 12

Care with a heavy edge

You love hard and worry hard, and the people close to you probably feel both. That's common, especially for parents and partners. Try writing down one worry about someone and, next to it, what is actually in your power to do. Doing that one thing and then stopping is the practice.

13 – 16

Caring without gripping

You worry about the people you love the way most people do, and you can say your piece and then trust them. The nights it gets to you tend to be about something specific. Talking that specific thing over with the person themselves, rather than turning it over alone, usually settles it.

17 – 20

Love, loosely held

You care deeply and let people live their own lives, which is one of the kindest things you can do for someone. That balance is worth keeping. When a real crisis comes, you'll likely worry more for a while, and that's normal. Let the worry rise and fall with the situation rather than settling in.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
