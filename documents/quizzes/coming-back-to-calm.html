What your score suggests

0 – 8

Upsets that stay for days

Once something sets you off, it seems to stay with you far longer than the moment deserves, and it wears you down. That is a body and mind that have not been shown how to come back down yet, not a flaw in you. Try one thing: after an upset, walk for ten minutes before you let yourself think about it. A therapist can help you build faster ways back.

9 – 12

A slow climb back down

You recover, but it takes a large chunk of the day, and the replaying does not help. That is common under stress. Pick one physical reset, like cold water on your wrists or a brisk walk, and use it early, before the thoughts start circling.

13 – 16

Back on your feet within hours

You come down from upsets in a reasonable time and mostly know what helps. That is solid regulation. Notice what makes recovery slower on some days, often tiredness or a lack of sleep, and give yourself extra space then.

17 – 20

Quick to settle

You return to yourself fast after something stirs you up, and you know exactly what gets you there. That is a well-practiced skill. Make sure “settling quickly” never means skipping over something that still deserves attention, and lend your resets to someone who is stuck.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
