What your score suggests

0 – 8

Underwater and alone

You are running on almost nothing, the old you feels lost, and support is thin or absent. That is a very hard place, and it does not mean you are a bad parent. It means you need more hands. This week, tell one specific person one specific thing they could do. A therapist who works with new parents can also make a real difference.

9 – 12

Getting through, barely

You are keeping the baby going, and you are slipping to the bottom of every list. Many new parents land here. Try one ten-minute pocket a day that is only yours, and treat it as part of the baby’s care. It is.

13 – 16

Finding a rhythm

You are starting to fold the old you into the new life, and you let people help even when it feels awkward. That is the adjustment working. Protect the adult conversations that are not about the baby; they keep you a whole person.

17 – 20

Steady, with help around you

You are looking after yourself as well as the baby, asking for what you need, and keeping perspective about other people’s highlight reels. That is a strong foundation. Sleep and support can shift quickly, so keep checking in with yourself. You may be the parent another new parent needs to hear from.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
