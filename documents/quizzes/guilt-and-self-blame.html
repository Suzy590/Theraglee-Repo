What your score suggests

0 – 8

Carrying blame that is not yours

Guilt and self-blame seem to be sitting heavily on you, and you may be taking on faults that belong to no one or to several people. That weight wears a person down. One small step: next time you say sorry, ask whether you actually did something wrong. A therapist can help you set some of it down.

9 – 12

Heavy on your shoulders

You carry more than your share of the blame, and you know it. Try writing the situation as if a friend had lived it, then reading back what you would tell them. The gap between the two is where the extra weight is.

13 – 16

Fair with yourself, mostly

You own your part in things and let the rest go, with some harsh moments on tired days. That is a healthy balance. Keep it by noticing when your tone toward yourself sharpens, and softening one sentence at a time.

17 – 20

Guilt does its job and leaves

Guilt shows up for you the way it should, briefly, pointing to a repair, then leaving. That fairness toward yourself is a strength. Share it with someone who apologizes for everything, and keep it honest by still owning the parts that are yours.
