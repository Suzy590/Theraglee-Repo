What your score suggests

0 – 8

Sorry is a shield

Apologies seem to be leaving your mouth before you have decided anything, and they may be doing the work of keeping you safe rather than making amends. That habit usually has a history. For one day, try swapping “sorry” for “thank you” where it fits: “thanks for waiting” instead of “sorry I’m late.” Notice how different it feels.

9 – 12

Sorry on autopilot

You apologize more than the situation asks for, mostly out of habit and a wish to keep things easy. That is common and very fixable. Pick one setting, maybe email, and remove the apology from the opening line for a week. Most people find nobody notices except them.

13 – 16

Sorry when it counts

You mostly save apologies for moments that need them, with an occasional reflex sorry slipping through. That balance is healthy. Keep noticing the ones that come from nerves rather than from anything you did. A quick tally at the end of the day can keep the habit honest.

17 – 20

Sorry means something

When you apologize, it is because you have something to own, and that gives your sorry real weight. Protect that by staying willing to apologize fully when you do get it wrong. If someone near you apologizes constantly, a gentle “you don’t need to say sorry for that” can be a gift.
