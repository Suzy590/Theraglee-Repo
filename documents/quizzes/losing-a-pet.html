What your score suggests

0 – 8

A loss nobody is honoring

You are grieving hard, possibly blaming yourself, and it may feel like the world does not think you are allowed to. You are. Losing an animal who shared your days is a real loss with no timetable. Find one person who gets it, even online, and tell them about your companion. If it ever feels like too much, in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.

9 – 12

The house is too quiet

The absence is loud, the routines have holes in them and other people’s remarks sting. All of that is grief doing what grief does. Try writing down three things you loved about them and one thing you gave them. Keep the page.

13 – 16

Missing them, on your own terms

You feel the loss, you brush off the people who do not get it, and you are gently reshaping the days. That is grieving well. Let a photo stay where you can see it, and say their name when it comes up.

17 – 20

Loved well, grieved well

You gave them a good life, you know it, and you are carrying the loss with room for both sadness and gratitude. That is the whole shape of it. The sudden waves may still come, especially at the old walk time. Someone else who just lost an animal might need to hear from you.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
