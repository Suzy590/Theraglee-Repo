What your score suggests

0 – 8

Money is looming

Money is taking up a great deal of your mind and your nights, and it may have become something you avoid looking at directly. That is a heavy weight, and many people carry it quietly. One small step: open the account or bill you've been avoiding, look for two minutes, and close it again. Knowing the number is usually easier than dreading it. A therapist can help with the shame.

9 – 12

A constant background hum

You manage, but money is never far from your thoughts, and it shapes what you say yes to. That's common, and it isn't only about how much is in the account. Try setting one fixed time a week to look at your money, and giving yourself permission not to think about it outside that window.

13 – 16

Managing the numbers

Money worries visit, especially around big expenses, but you handle them and they pass. That's a healthy relationship with something most people find stressful. Notice what tips you into a bad night, since it's often a surprise rather than the total. A small buffer for surprises, even a tiny one, can quiet those nights.

17 – 20

Money in its place

Money is a topic, not a shadow. You look at it when you need to, plan around it, and don't lose sleep. That calm is worth keeping, and it may be worth sharing: if someone close to you goes quiet when money comes up, a steady, non-judgmental ear could help them more than advice.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
