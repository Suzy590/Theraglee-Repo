What your score suggests

0 – 8

Hard moments become harder

When something painful cannot be fixed right away, the urge to escape seems to take over and leave you worse off. That is not weakness; it is a nervous system that has not been shown another way. Try one small tool: hold ice or splash cold water on your face when the peak hits, then ride the next few minutes. A therapist who knows DBT can teach you more.

9 – 12

Getting through, at a cost

You survive the unfixable moments, but frazzled and sometimes with a decision you regret. That is a common starting point. Write a short list of three things that help your body settle, keep it on your phone, and try the first one before the peak, not after.

13 – 16

Steady enough to wait

You can mostly sit through painful stretches without making them worse, and you have some tools that work. That is real skill. Pay attention to the moments that still tip you, often when tired or alone, and add one tool specifically for those.

17 – 20

Weathering it well

You get through hard hours with steadiness and skill, and your choices in those moments tend to hold up afterward. That took practice. Keep it sharp by staying honest about the rare time it slips, and consider sharing what helps with someone who is in the middle of it.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
