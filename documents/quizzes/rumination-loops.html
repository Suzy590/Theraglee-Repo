What your score suggests

0 – 8

Caught in the replay

The loops are frequent, harsh, and hard to notice until they've taken hours. That is draining, and it is a pattern many people know well. One small step: when you catch a loop, say “I'm doing it again” out loud and stand up. Moving your body breaks the loop more reliably than arguing with it. A therapist can help you soften the voice inside it.

9 – 12

Hard to step out

You notice the loops, but stepping out takes real effort and the tone inside them is unkind. That's common. Try giving the loop a fixed time, like ten minutes with a notebook, and then closing it. When it comes back outside that window, remind yourself it has its slot.

13 – 16

Loops with an exit

You get caught in a replay now and then, but you can name it and move on before it costs you much. That's a good place to be. Notice which topics still hook you: they usually point to something that matters. Talking that thing over with someone can finish what the loop can't.

17 – 20

You let it go

You review what happened, learn what you can, and let it fade. That's how reflection is supposed to work, and it's rarer than you might think. Keep it going, and if you know someone who gets stuck in loops, your way of talking to yourself could be worth sharing.
