What your score suggests

0 – 8

Embarrassment that lingers

Embarrassing moments seem to sink deep and stay for a long time, shaping what you do and how you see yourself. That kind of shame is heavy, and it is more common than anyone admits. Try one thing: next time you cringe, put a hand on your chest and say what you would say to a friend who did the same. A therapist can help you loosen shame’s grip.

9 – 12

Cringing longer than you would like

Embarrassment fades for you, but slowly, and old moments still resurface with a sting. That is normal, and it can soften. Try writing one recent embarrassing moment down as if it happened to someone you love, and notice how differently it reads.

13 – 16

A blush, then back to normal

You feel embarrassment like anyone, but you recover fairly quickly and rarely let it change your plans. That is healthy self-regard. Notice the kind of moment that still stings the most, since it usually points to something you care about deeply.

17 – 20

Laughing it off, honestly

You take embarrassment lightly and genuinely, and your old awkward moments have become stories rather than wounds. That is a sign of solid self-worth. Keep it by staying kind rather than dismissive with yourself, and offer that same lightness to someone who is still mortified.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
