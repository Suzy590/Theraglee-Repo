What your score suggests

0 – 8

Held up by props

You've built a careful system of exits, scripts, and standbys, and it takes real effort to keep it running. It makes sense: those things have helped you get through. The catch is that they also keep you from finding out you could manage without them. One small step: drop a single prop in a low-stakes situation and see what happens. A therapist can help you plan those experiments.

9 – 12

Leaning on your toolkit

You get through anxious moments, but you credit the props more than yourself, and without them you'd hesitate. That's a very common place to be. Try noticing, after the next hard thing, which parts you actually handled on your own. Write them down. The list is usually longer than expected.

13 – 16

Mostly standing on your own

You keep a prop or two for the hardest situations, and otherwise you trust yourself in the room. That balance is healthy. The one or two rituals you still lean on are worth a curious look: what do you expect would happen without them? Testing that gently, once, can be freeing.

17 – 20

You trust yourself in the room

You walk into new situations without a safety kit and credit yourself when they go well. That confidence has probably been earned over time. Keep it by noticing if a stressful season brings any old props back, and be kind to yourself if it does. That's a signal, not a setback.
