What your score suggests

0 – 8

Still knocked down

The last setback seems to have taken your drive with it, and getting up may feel like too much right now. That is a normal response to something that hurt. One small step: separate the setback from what it says about you by writing down what happened in plain facts, with no verdict. A therapist can help you rebuild from there.

9 – 12

Slow to get back up

You recover, but it takes weeks, and the inner critic may be making it longer. Try writing the setback the way a good coach would describe it to a player they believe in, and notice how different the next step looks from that angle.

13 – 16

Bouncing back with effort

You get back up after setbacks, though it takes some work and a quiet day or two. That is resilience in practice. Keep it by naming one specific thing each setback taught you, so the pattern becomes learning rather than just endurance.

17 – 20

Back on your feet quickly

You seem to recover from setbacks fast and talk to yourself like a good coach along the way. That is a real strength. Keep it honest by still letting the loss sting before you move, and lend that steady voice to someone who is still knocked down.
