What your score suggests

0 – 8

Your time belongs to everyone else

Your hours seem to be spent by other people, and you are left with the scraps. That is exhausting, and it is very common among people who are good at helping. Start with one protected block a week, even thirty minutes, and defend it like a doctor’s appointment. If the guilt makes that hard, a therapist can help you look at why.

9 – 12

Time slips through

You try to hold some time for yourself, but interruptions and quick favors keep taking more than you meant to give. Most people recognize this. Try answering every “got a minute?” with “when do you need it by?” It buys you room to decide instead of react.

13 – 16

You keep most of it

Your calendar mostly reflects what matters to you, and you can push a request back without much strain. Keep noticing which people or tasks still steal a block now and then. Writing down where the week went, once a week, tends to show the leak.

17 – 20

Your time is yours

You decide how your hours get used, protect your own blocks, and still show up for people when it counts. That is a rare balance. Keep it by staying flexible for real emergencies, so the boundary does not turn brittle. Others could learn from how you say “not now.”

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
