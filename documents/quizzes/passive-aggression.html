What your score suggests

0 – 8

Anger going out the side door

Right now annoyance tends to leak out as sarcasm, silence or small withholding rather than plain words, and it leaves everyone unsure where they stand. That usually means direct anger never felt safe. Try one plain sentence this week: “I was annoyed when…” to someone kind. A therapist can help you find safer ways to say hard things.

9 – 12

Hinting more than saying

You often signal displeasure rather than state it, and people sometimes have to guess. That is very common and very fixable. Next time you catch yourself hinting, follow it within five minutes with the plain version of what you mean.

13 – 16

Mostly plain, a little sharp

You generally say what is bothering you, with the occasional pointed joke or cool tone. That is a healthy pattern. Notice who brings out the sharpness in you and see whether those relationships need a more direct conversation.

17 – 20

Straight talk, kindly delivered

You tend to say what you are unhappy about directly and calmly, and people know where they stand. That is a real relational skill. Keep it by staying warm when you are direct, and be patient with people who still go sideways, since they usually learned that for a reason.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
