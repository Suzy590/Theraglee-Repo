What your score suggests

0 – 8

Their weather is your weather

Other people’s moods seem to move straight into you, and your own plans and feelings get lost in the process. Being this attuned is a real strength that has slipped out of balance. Try one small check after a heavy conversation: ask yourself, “What was I feeling before this call?” A therapist can help you build a steadier line.

9 – 12

Porous around the edges

You feel with people, sometimes more than is good for you, and you can lose an evening to a mood that started somewhere else. Many caring people sit here. This week, try naming the feeling silently as “theirs” when it arrives. It sounds small, and it is surprisingly effective.

13 – 16

Empathy with a boundary

You can be present for someone without becoming them, and you catch yourself when you start to drift. That is a skill, not a coincidence. Keep an eye on the relationships where the line thins, often the ones closest to home, and note them in a journal when you notice.

17 – 20

Clear where you end

You know which feelings are yours and which arrived with someone else, and you can offer care without paying for it in your own mood. Keep it by staying warm as well as clear. Detachment is not the goal. Consider sharing how you do it with someone who is drowning in other people’s feelings.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
