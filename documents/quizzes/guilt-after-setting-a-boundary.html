What your score suggests

0 – 8

Guilt undoes the boundary

Your score suggests the guilt after a limit is so heavy that it often erases the limit itself, and you speak to yourself harshly while it does. That guilt is usually old and borrowed, not earned. Try writing the boundary down before you set it, with one line on why it matters, and reread that line when the guilt arrives. A therapist can help you loosen this.

9 – 12

Guilt visits, and stays late

You hold your boundaries more often than not, but the guilt afterward is out of proportion and hard on you. Many people know this. When it shows up, try asking one question: “Did I actually harm anyone?” The honest answer is usually no, and hearing it in your own voice helps.

13 – 16

Guilt passes through

You feel a tug after protecting yourself, but it passes, and you rarely let it undo what you decided. That is a good place to be. Notice which limits still bring a bigger wave, often the ones with family or old friends, and give yourself a little extra kindness on those days.

17 – 20

Kind to yourself afterward

You set a limit, feel whatever comes, and treat yourself with fairness while it passes. That is exactly how it is meant to work. Keep it by staying honest about the rare times the guilt is pointing at something real. And share the “did I actually harm anyone?” question with someone who needs it.
