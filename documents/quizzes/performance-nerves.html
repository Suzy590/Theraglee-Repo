What your score suggests

0 – 8

Nerves take the stage

Being watched or measured is hitting you hard, before, during, and after, and it may be closing doors you'd rather keep open. That's painful, and it's common among people who care about doing well. One small step: before your next performance, write down the one thing you want the audience to take away, and aim only for that. A therapist can help you turn the wall into a climb.

9 – 12

Performing through the fog

You get through, but nerves make the moment patchy and the aftermath harsh. Many capable people perform this way for years. Try changing the after-talk first: right afterward, name two things that went fine before you list what didn't. The nerves before tend to shrink when the review afterward gets fairer.

13 – 16

Nerves on your side

You feel nerves, but they mostly sharpen you, and you talk to yourself fairly afterward. That's how nerves are meant to work. Notice which settings still tip you into fog, since it's often a specific kind of audience or stakes. Practicing in those settings, in low-stakes ways, can close the gap.

17 – 20

Steady under the lights

You perform with a flutter of good nerves and a fair review afterward. That is a real strength, and one you can pass along. If someone around you freezes when they're watched, sharing how you prepare and how you talk to yourself afterward could make a genuine difference for them.
