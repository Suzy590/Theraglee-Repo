What your score suggests

0 – 8

The line moves when pushed

A boundary feels possible to set but hard to keep, and a second ask or a cold silence tends to undo it. That reflects how much you value connection, not weakness. Try one short phrase, like “I hear you, and my answer is the same,” and use it without adding reasons. A therapist can help if the panic is loud.

9 – 12

Holding, but it hurts

You hold your limits more often than not, but pushback shakes you, and you sometimes pay for it in guilt or negotiation. That is normal for anyone who cares what people think. Practice holding one small limit through a single follow-up ask, and notice that the discomfort passes.

13 – 16

Steady with a wobble

You keep your boundaries under pressure, with the occasional wobble when someone you love pushes hard. That is real resilience. Keep a note of which tactic still gets to you, guilt or silence or persistence, so you can prepare for it rather than be surprised.

17 – 20

Your no stays no

You hold your line calmly, repeat it without escalating, and let the other person manage their reaction. Keep it warm, so steadiness never turns to stubbornness. If someone in your life folds under every second ask, you are in a good position to help them see it is survivable.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
