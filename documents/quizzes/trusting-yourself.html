What your score suggests

0 – 8

Borrowing everyone else’s judgment

Right now your own sense of things seems to get overruled, by other people or by yourself, almost every time. That leaves you feeling unmoored, and it is common after years of being told you were wrong. Start small: make one low-stakes choice a day on gut alone, and note afterward how it went.

9 – 12

Doubting what you know

You have judgment and you often ignore it, then notice afterward that you were right. That is a pattern, not a flaw. This week, keep a short note of the hunches you have and whether they pan out. Evidence builds trust faster than pep talks.

13 – 16

Trust with a few gaps

In most areas you rely on your own read and can hold it under pressure. A few situations or people still make you fold. Notice where that happens, and try holding your version thirty seconds longer than usual.

17 – 20

Your own steady ground

You seem to trust your gut, your memory, and your choices, and you can still update when new facts arrive. That combination is rarer than it sounds. Keep it by staying curious rather than certain, and lend that calm to someone who is second-guessing themselves.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
