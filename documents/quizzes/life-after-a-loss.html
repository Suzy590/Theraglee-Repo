What your score suggests

0 – 8

Still in the deep water

Life after this loss feels frozen or hollow, and that can be true whether it has been six months or six years. Grief keeps its own calendar. One gentle next step: pick a single small thing they loved and do it this week, in their honor, with someone beside you. If it ever feels like too much, in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.

9 – 12

Moving, but heavily

You are moving through life, and each part of it takes more out of you than people can see. That is common, and it is not a failure to move on. Try naming one invitation or task you have been dodging and take the smallest piece of it. A therapist who knows grief can be a steady companion here.

13 – 16

Sad and living, both

You are living your life with the loss woven into it rather than walling it off. Sadness still turns up, and you let it. Keep telling the stories when their name comes up; that is one of the ways love keeps its shape after someone is gone.

17 – 20

Carrying them forward

You have made room for a life that holds the person you lost without being ruled by the loss. That took time and courage. Waves may still come at anniversaries or out of nowhere, and that is not a step backward. Consider writing down what you have learned about grieving; it may help you or someone else later.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
