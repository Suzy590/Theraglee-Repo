What your score suggests

0 – 8

Pride swallowed by shame

It sounds as if pride was never allowed, and even now a moment of feeling good about yourself brings a rush of shame. That was learned, usually early and usually for someone else’s comfort. Try saying one thing you are proud of to yourself, in a mirror, and let the cringe pass. It does pass. A therapist can help here too.

9 – 12

Pride with a flinch

You can feel proud, but it comes wrapped in embarrassment, jokes, or a quick move to the next thing. This week, tell one trusted person “I’m proud of this” with no softener attached. Notice that they are glad, not put off.

13 – 16

Quiet pride

You can own your pride most of the time, with an occasional flinch when the spotlight is bright or the subject is personal. Pay attention to which parts of yourself you still keep vague, and give one of them a bit more air with someone safe.

17 – 20

Proud and at ease

You seem able to feel proud of who you are and what you have done, say so plainly, and let the warmth stay. That is a healthy kind of pride, the kind that makes room for others. Keep it by celebrating other people as freely, and show someone who flinches at pride what it can look like.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
