What your score suggests

0 – 8

Disappearing into them

It sounds like the people close to you have taken up most of the room, and your own wants have gone quiet. That often happens to kind, attentive people, and it can be turned around. Start with a five-minute journal question each morning: “What do I want today?” Even a small answer counts. A therapist can help you find the rest.

9 – 12

Blurry at the edges

You have a self in there, but it bends a lot toward what others want, and the guilt after a no is loud. That is common in close relationships. Try saying one small honest preference a day, like which restaurant or which route, and let it stand.

13 – 16

Mostly holding your shape

You stay largely yourself in your relationships, with some drift when someone is upset. Notice the friend or hobby that slipped this year and give it a date on the calendar. Your relationships get better when there is more of you in them.

17 – 20

Close without losing yourself

You care deeply and still keep your own ground, your own people and your own plans. That is what makes you good to be close to. Keep an eye out for someone in your life who is shrinking to fit, and ask them what they want.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
