Anxiety spikes happen to everyone. What differs is what comes next. This check-in looks at how long a spike lingers for you, what helps it pass, and how you treat yourself afterward.

What your score suggests

0 – 8

Spikes that don't end

Right now a spike doesn't really finish. It rolls into the next day, and the way you talk to yourself afterward keeps it going. That is exhausting, and it's a pattern that can change. One small step: after the next spike, say one kind sentence to yourself, even if you don't believe it yet. A therapist can help you find what settles you and soften the voice that follows.

9 – 12

A long tail

You settle, but slowly, and the hours after a spike are often spent worrying it will return or being hard on yourself. Many people know that tail well. Try choosing one steadying action, a walk or a short call, and doing it right after the next spike, before the review starts.

13 – 16

You settle, given time

Spikes pass for you within a few hours, and you have something that usually helps. That's a solid recovery. The gap to close is remembering the tool in the moment. A note on your phone or a word from someone you trust can be the reminder that makes the difference.

17 – 20

Quick to steady

You settle quickly after a spike, treat yourself kindly, and reach for what helps without having to think about it. That is real resilience. Keep it going, and notice if a run of hard weeks starts to lengthen the tail. Catching that early is the whole game.
