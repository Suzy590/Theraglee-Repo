What your score suggests

0 – 8

The date takes everything

Hard dates are hitting you with their full weight, often before you even see them coming, and you are facing them mostly alone. That is exhausting, and it does not mean you should be over it. Before the next date, tell one person and ask them to check in. If it ever feels like too much, in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.

9 – 12

Bracing every time

You feel the date coming and mostly white-knuckle through it, then need a while to recover. Many grieving people do the same. Try marking your calendar a week ahead with one small plan: a place to go, a person to be with, a ritual that is yours.

13 – 16

Marked, and managed

You know the date is coming, you make some room for it, and you get back on your feet afterward. That is a wise way to carry a loss. Letting one more person in on what you need would lighten it further.

17 – 20

Honoring the day your way

You have found a way to meet hard dates that honors the person and looks after you, and you ask for the support you need. That is grief carried well, not grief finished. Some years will still hit harder, and that is not going backward. Your way of marking the day could help someone who is dreading their first.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
