What your score suggests

0 – 8

Running on borrowed calm

Right now, most of your calm seems to come from other people confirming things, and it doesn't last long. That is a tiring cycle, and it can strain the people you lean on. Many people find themselves here. One small step: the next time you feel the urge to ask, wait ten minutes and write down what you already believe. A therapist can help you build inner steadiness.

9 – 12

Leaning on others to settle

You can settle yourself sometimes, but checking with others is still your first move, and the relief fades fast. That's a common habit, especially in close relationships. Try tracking how often you ask for reassurance in a day. Just seeing the number often changes it.

13 – 16

Mostly settled from within

You ask for reassurance now and then, which is healthy, and mostly you can trust your own judgment. Notice the topics where the urge to check is strongest. Those usually point to something that matters to you, and they're a good place to practice sitting with the uncertainty a little longer.

17 – 20

Steady on your own

You can make a call, send a message, or leave a conversation without needing someone to confirm it was fine. That steadiness is a gift to the people around you as well as to you. Keep it going by staying honest with yourself about the rare times you do want a second opinion.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
