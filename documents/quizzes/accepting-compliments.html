What your score suggests

0 – 8

Kind words bounce off

Compliments seem to hit a wall in you, and something inside argues them down or hands them back before they can land. That usually comes from an old belief about being found out, not from the truth. Try one experiment: say “thank you” and nothing else, then count to three before speaking. It will feel strange, and that is the work. A therapist can help if the belief runs deep.

9 – 12

Thanks, but…

You can take a compliment, but you shrink it, qualify it, or hurry to return it before it settles. That is a very common reflex. This week, write down every compliment you receive, word for word, and reread the list on Sunday. Seeing them collected makes them harder to dismiss.

13 – 16

Kind words mostly land

You mostly let compliments in and feel them, with the occasional deflection when you are caught off guard. That is a healthy place. Notice the kind of praise that still makes you squirm, about looks or character or work, and let yourself sit with that one a little longer.

17 – 20

You can take a compliment

You receive kind words with grace, let them land, and give credit where it is due. That makes people enjoy praising you, which is a gift to them too. Keep it by giving specific compliments generously, and when you see someone bat one away, you can gently say it again.
