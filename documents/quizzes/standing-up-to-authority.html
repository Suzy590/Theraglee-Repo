What your score suggests

0 – 8

Authority silences you

Around anyone with power, your own judgment seems to switch off, and going along feels safer than speaking. That often comes from real experiences of being overruled or punished, so it makes sense. Try one low-stakes question this week, like asking a manager to explain a decision. Questions are safer than statements and open the door. A therapist can help if fear is running this.

9 – 12

You push back, quietly

You can stand up to authority on the big things, but it costs you, and many smaller disagreements go unsaid. Most people recognize this. Practice putting a disagreement in writing, where you can choose your words and no one is watching your face. It builds confidence for the in-person version.

13 – 16

Respectful and clear

You mostly say what you think to people in charge, with a bit of nervousness that does not stop you. That is a strong place. Watch for the specific kind of authority that still shrinks you, whether medical or legal or a certain personality, and note what you would have said.

17 – 20

A title doesn’t change you

You stay yourself in front of people with power, disagree when it matters, and do it with enough care that it usually lands well. Keep it by picking your battles, so your voice keeps its weight. And when a colleague goes quiet in front of the boss, you can be the one who asks their opinion.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
