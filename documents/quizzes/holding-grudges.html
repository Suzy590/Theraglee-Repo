What your score suggests

0 – 8

Carrying a heavy list

Old hurts are taking up a lot of room, and the weight is mostly on you rather than on the people who caused them. That is understandable, especially if some of those hurts were never acknowledged. Try writing one grudge out fully, including what you needed and did not get. You do not have to forgive anyone. A therapist can help you decide what to do with it.

9 – 12

Holding on tighter than you want

You hang on to some hurts longer than feels good, and running into those people still costs you. That is a very common pattern. Pick the smallest grudge on your list and ask yourself what it is protecting you from. Sometimes naming that is enough to loosen it.

13 – 16

Slow to let go, but steady

You feel hurts deeply and take your time, yet you generally get to the other side. That is a healthy pace. Notice the one or two that have stayed longer than the rest, and consider whether there is something you still need to say, even just on paper.

17 – 20

Traveling light

You process hurts, say what needs saying, and set them down without pretending they did not happen. That takes real maturity. Check now and then that letting go never means letting someone keep hurting you, and share your approach with someone who is stuck.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
