What your score suggests

0 – 8

Attention in pieces

Your attention is being pulled in so many directions that nothing gets a fair chunk of it, and you are ending days exhausted. This is very common in a world built to interrupt. Try one fifteen-minute block with the phone in another room. That is enough to remind your mind what focus feels like. If racing thoughts are part of it, a therapist can help.

9 – 12

Easily pulled away

You can focus, but the pull of interruptions wins more often than not, and it leaves you feeling scattered. Silencing notifications during one work block a day is a small change with a big return.

13 – 16

Holding the thread

You can hold a thread for a good while and recover from most interruptions. The drift shows up when work gets hard or dull. Try noticing the exact moment you reach for a distraction and naming what you were avoiding.

17 – 20

Deep and steady

You settle into work and stay there, and the day ends with a sense of having used your mind well. That is a real strength. Guard it by keeping the conditions that make it possible, and be generous with coworkers whose attention is more fragmented.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
