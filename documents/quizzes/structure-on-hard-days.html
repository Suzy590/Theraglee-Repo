What your score suggests

0 – 8

Hard days dissolve

Hard days seem to lose all shape, and the shame that follows may be making the next one harder. Structure on a bad day is not about being productive; it is a handrail. One small step: write three tiny anchors for your next rough day, like water, a shower, and one text, and stick it somewhere you will see it.

9 – 12

A few loose anchors

You have some sense of what helps on hard days, but it lives in your head and slips when you need it most. Try writing your bad-day plan down while you feel okay, and keep it somewhere you will see it before the next rough morning.

13 – 16

A shape that mostly holds

You keep a stripped-down routine on hard days, and it mostly holds. That is exactly what structure is for. Keep it small, forgiving, and written down, and notice which anchor makes the biggest difference so you protect it first.

17 – 20

Handrails in place

You seem to have a bad-day routine that holds even when mood and energy bottom out, and you feel okay about those days afterward. That is a real achievement. Keep the plan updated as life changes, and share the idea with someone whose worst days dissolve.
