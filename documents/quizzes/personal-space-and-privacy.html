What your score suggests

0 – 8

No door that closes

Your score suggests you rarely feel entitled to space, privacy, or a say over your own body, and that other people’s reach goes mostly unchallenged. That usually has a history, and it is worth understanding. Start small: one closed door, one “I’d rather not,” this week. A therapist can help if the shame around needing space runs deep.

9 – 12

The line gets crossed

You know where your lines are, but you often let them be stepped over and stew about it afterward. That is very common. Try naming one crossing out loud, right when it happens, in as few words as possible. “I’ll take that back, thanks” works for a phone. Short beats perfect.

13 – 16

Mostly yours

You keep your space and privacy most of the time, and you can speak up when someone reaches past the line. That is solid. Notice the person or situation where you still go quiet, and write a sentence you could use next time. Having it ready changes a lot.

17 – 20

Your space is respected

You hold your space, your privacy, and your body as your own, and the people around you have learned to ask first. Keep that by staying open, so the line reads as self-respect rather than a wall. If a friend seems to have no door of their own, you can model what closing one looks like.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
