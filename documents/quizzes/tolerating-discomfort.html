What your score suggests

0 – 8

Discomfort calls the shots

Right now, unpleasant feelings seem to demand an immediate exit, and that need is shaping a lot of your choices. That is exhausting, and it usually means the feelings have been very big for a long time. One small step: the next time unease arrives, count slowly to thirty before you do anything about it. A therapist can help you build up from there, gently.

9 – 12

Quick to escape

You can tolerate some discomfort, but your hand goes to the phone or the snack fast, and hard feelings tend to decide your plans. That's a common habit. Try naming the feeling out loud, or in a note, before you reach for the escape. Naming often takes some of the urgency out of it.

13 – 16

You can stay a while

You can sit with discomfort for a while and let it pass, and you mostly don't let it run your week. That's a real skill. Notice the situations where you still reach for a fix, especially when someone else is upset. Staying one minute longer than feels comfortable is the practice.

17 – 20

Room for the feeling

You let uncomfortable feelings be there without fighting or fleeing, and they pass. That is a hard-won ability, whether you learned it on purpose or not. Keep it by remembering that tolerating a feeling is different from ignoring a need. Sometimes the discomfort is pointing at something worth acting on.
