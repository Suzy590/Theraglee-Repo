What your score suggests

0 – 8

Anger comes out sideways or hard

Right now anger tends to come out in ways that hurt, whether that is shouting, silence or sarcasm. That usually means the feeling has no safe outlet yet. Try one thing this week: when you feel the heat rise, leave the room for ten minutes before saying anything. A therapist can help you find words for what sits underneath.

9 – 12

Anger leaks before it speaks

Your anger often finds an indirect route, like sharpness, hints or a cold shoulder, before you manage to say what is actually wrong. Many people learned that early and can unlearn it. Practice one plain sentence: “I felt hurt when…” and try it on something small.

13 – 16

Mostly said, sometimes spilled

You usually manage to put anger into words rather than actions, with the occasional sharp edge. That is solid ground. Notice which situations tip you from speaking to snapping, and give yourself a little extra pause in those.

17 – 20

Anger gets a clear voice

You tend to say what upset you plainly and then move on, which is rarer than you might think. Keep it up by checking, now and then, whether being measured ever shades into holding back. A calm, honest complaint is a gift to the people around you.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
