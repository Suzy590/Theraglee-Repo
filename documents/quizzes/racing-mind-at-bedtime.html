What your score suggests

0 – 8

The mind takes the night shift

Your thoughts get loudest exactly when you need them quietest, and lying down has become something to brace for. That is exhausting, and it is very common. Try a worry list on paper an hour before bed, written and closed, so the thoughts have somewhere to live. If nights stay this hard, a therapist can help you work with the pattern.

9 – 12

Loud, then slowly quieter

Your mind runs at bedtime, though you can usually bring it down with effort. That effort is worth spending earlier. Give yourself ten minutes of planned thinking time in the early evening, and notice whether the nights get lighter once the day has a place to land.

13 – 16

Thoughts pass through

Some nights are busier than others, but your thoughts mostly come and go without taking over. Notice which days feed the louder nights, whether it is late work or a hard conversation, and give those days an extra bit of wind-down.

17 – 20

Quiet in the dark

Your mind settles when the room does, and worries mostly know to wait until morning. That is a skill many people are still building, and it is worth appreciating. Keep the habits that support it, and be patient with yourself on the rare wired night.
