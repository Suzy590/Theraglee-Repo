What your score suggests

0 – 8

Family sets the terms

Around family, your limits seem to dissolve, and old roles and guilt do the deciding. That is one of the hardest places to hold a line, and you are not alone in it. Pick one small boundary, like ending calls at a set time, and practice only that for a month. A therapist can be a good ally here.

9 – 12

Holding on, with effort

You can push back with family, but it costs you, and you often give in when the guilt gets loud. That is honest, ordinary work. Before the next visit or call, decide one thing you will not do this time, and write it down. A plan made ahead beats one made in the moment.

13 – 16

Your line mostly holds

You keep your limits with family most of the time, and you can feel the pull without following it. That takes maturity. Notice which relative or topic still gets past you, and give yourself a script for it. Even two prepared sentences can change how the next conversation goes.

17 – 20

Grown up in every room

You stay yourself around your family, respond to guilt without folding, and let the people who raised you know where you stand. Keep it kind, so the boundary reads as care rather than distance. And if a sibling or cousin is struggling with this, you may be the one who can show them how.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.
