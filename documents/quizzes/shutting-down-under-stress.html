What your score suggests

0 – 8

Gone quiet and far away

Under stress you seem to go somewhere distant, and coming back is slow and lonely. This is a protective response, not a failure, and it often started long ago. Try one grounding anchor, like pressing your feet into the floor and naming five things you can see, when you notice the fog. A therapist can help you understand where the shutdown comes from.

9 – 12

Checking out too often

Shutting down is a regular part of how you weather stress, and it costs you time, connection and clarity. Many people who never learned to fight or flee learned to freeze instead. Practice saying “I need a minute” before you go blank, even if only in your head.

13 – 16

Quiet, but still here

You get quiet under pressure, yet you mostly stay reachable and recover reasonably fast. That is a workable rhythm. Tell the people close to you what your quiet means and how they can help, so it never reads as rejection.

17 – 20

Present through the storm

You tend to stay connected and thinking even when stress is high, and you know how to come back if you drift. That is genuinely steady. Guard it by resting before you run empty, and be patient with people whose stress makes them disappear.
