What your score suggests

0 – 8

Taking what you are given

Asking for more seems to feel like a kind of trespass, so you take what is offered and call yourself lucky. That belief often has old roots, and it is costing you. Try practicing on something tiny, like asking whether a floor model can be discounted. Small asks teach your body that asking is allowed. A therapist can help with the “who do I think I am” voice.

9 – 12

You ask, then fold

You can open a negotiation, but you move to the other side’s number the moment you feel resistance. That is common, and it is a matter of practice more than character. Next time, decide your number and one sentence to repeat if they push. Then let the silence do some work.

13 – 16

Holding your ground, mostly

You ask for what you are worth and hold your position for a round or two before finding middle ground. That is good negotiating. Notice the arena where you still fold fast, whether money or time or workload, and prepare for it a little more than the others.

17 – 20

You know your number

You name what you want, hold it under pressure, and stay fair while you do it. That mix earns respect as well as results. Keep it by staying generous in small negotiations, so you do not become someone who has to win everything. And coach a friend who takes the first offer.
