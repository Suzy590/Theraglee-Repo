What your score suggests

0 – 8

Carrying it alone

Caring for someone has taken over your health, your time and your sense of yourself, and you are doing it largely alone. Feeling resentment or dread does not make you a bad caregiver, it makes you a tired one. This week, tell one person the honest version. A therapist or a caregiver support group can help you find relief that does not depend on you doing less loving.

9 – 12

Stretched thin

You are managing, but at the cost of your own health and any time for yourself, and few people know how hard it is. Try naming one specific task someone else could take, and ask for exactly that.

13 – 16

Held together with support

Caregiving is hard and you feel it, yet you have people who know and you still have some room for yourself. Keep that room, even in a rough month. Book your own next appointment before it slips.

17 – 20

Caring without disappearing

You give a lot and you have stayed a person while doing it, with support and time of your own. That balance takes work. Tell another caregiver what has helped, they may not know it is possible.

A self-assessment is a mirror, not a diagnosis. If anything here worries you, bring it to a licensed professional.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or your local emergency number. Theraglee is a wellbeing tool, not a substitute for professional care.
