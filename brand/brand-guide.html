Typography

Title — Outfit 300, −0.035em

A little lighter, every morning

Standfirst — Instrument Serif italic, green

The serif italic is the brand's speaking voice. Use it once per document, near the top.

Section heading — Outfit 500

Your days

Body — Outfit 400, 1.62 line height

Body copy sits at a 66-character measure. Outfit's geometric roundness carries the warmth, so the writing can stay plain — no exclamation marks needed.

Category accents

Each document sets data-doc on <body>. That single hook re-points --accent, --accent-soft and --accent-line; every other rule is shared. This is what keeps six document types looking related while still feeling distinct.

challenge Lime. Numbered day rail, tick boxes, streak language.
article Green. Reading measure, serif lede, labelled callouts.
checklist Green. Banded rows, action over explanation.
journal Sand. Serif prompts, ruled space, minimal chrome.
quiz Green. Numbered questions, score pills, banded results.
worksheet Sand. Numbered sections, generous fill space, tables.

Rules of thumb

  • The masthead never names the category. It carries the brand line; the eyebrow chip below the title names the category.
  • The eyebrow carries a fact, not just a name — 30-Day Challenge, Article · 5 min read. Don't repeat that fact in the meta row underneath it.
  • One serif moment per document. The standfirst, or the journal prompt. More than that and it stops feeling like a voice.
  • Every atomic unit survives a page break. Days, checklist rows, questions, bands and fields all carry break-inside:avoid.
  • Print strips the chrome, not the structure. The sheet loses its border, shadow and radius; the rails, rules and boxes stay.
